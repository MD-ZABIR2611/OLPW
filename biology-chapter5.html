Plant Nutrition & Photosynthesis

CORE
SECTION 01 — CHAPTER OVERVIEW

Photosynthesis & Leaf Structure

Plants are autotrophs; they make their own food using light energy. This chapter covers the process of photosynthesis, the structure of the leaf, and the limiting factors that affect the rate of photosynthesis.

SECTION 02 — LEARNING OBJECTIVES

Chapter Goals

  • Write the word and balanced chemical equations for photosynthesis.
  • Identify the cellular structures involved in photosynthesis.
  • Explain how the leaf is adapted for photosynthesis.
  • Interpret graphs showing limiting factors.
  • Describe the starch test in leaves.
SECTION 03 — CORE THEORY

The Process of Photosynthesis

Photosynthesis occurs in the chloroplasts of plant cells. Chlorophyll absorbs light energy, which is used to convert carbon dioxide and water into glucose and oxygen.

Equations

Word: carbon dioxide + water → glucose + oxygen

Chemical: 6CO₂ + 6H₂O → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂

Limiting Factors

The rate of photosynthesis is limited by three main factors:

1. Light Intensity: As light intensity increases, the rate increases, until another factor becomes limiting.

2. Carbon Dioxide Concentration: CO2 is required for the reaction; higher levels increase the rate.

3. Temperature: Photosynthesis relies on enzymes. As temperature rises, the rate increases until the optimum temperature. Above this, enzymes denature and the rate plummets.

Mineral Ions

Plants also need mineral ions for healthy growth. Nitrate ions are needed to make amino acids and proteins for growth. Magnesium ions are needed to make chlorophyll.

SECTION 04 — KEY DEFINITIONS

Examination Definitions

PHOTOSYNTHESIS: The process by which plants use light energy to make glucose from carbon dioxide and water, producing oxygen as a waste product.

LIMITING FACTOR: A factor that restricts the rate of a reaction (e.g., light, CO2, or temperature).

SECTION 06 — PRACTICAL LAB

Testing a Leaf for Starch

AIM

To determine if photosynthesis has occurred by testing for starch.

METHOD

1. Dip leaf in boiling water (breaks cell walls).

2. Boil leaf in ethanol (removes chlorophyll, makes leaf pale/white).

3. Wash in warm water (softens leaf).

4. Spread on tile, add iodine solution.

RESULT

Blue/Black indicates starch is present (photosynthesis occurred). Orange/Brown indicates no starch.

SECTION 07 — EXAM TECHNIQUE

Explaining Limiting Factors

If a graph shows a line levelling off, you must identify the new limiting factor. E.g., If light increases but the rate stops rising, CO2 or temperature is now limiting the rate.

SECTION 08 — COMMON MISTAKES

Avoid These Errors

Mistake: Saying plants "breathe" in CO2.

Correction: Plants do not breathe; they exchange gases through stomata via diffusion.

Mistake: Confusing nitrate and magnesium deficiencies. (Nitrate = yellow/stunted growth; Magnesium = yellow leaves/chlorosis).

SECTION 09 — QUICK REVISION

Fast Facts

  • Chlorophyll absorbs light energy.
  • Palisade cells are tightly packed at the top of the leaf to absorb max light.
  • Iodine turns Blue/Black in the presence of starch.
