Cybersecurity & Digital Security

CORE
SECTION 01 — CHAPTER OVERVIEW

Protecting Systems & Data

As computing becomes ubiquitous, the threats to data and systems grow. This chapter covers the types of cyber threats, how attacks occur, and the countermeasures (both technical and human) used to protect digital assets.

SECTION 02 — LEARNING OBJECTIVES

Chapter Goals

  • Classify types of malware (Virus, Worm, Trojan, Spyware).
  • Explain social engineering attacks (Phishing, Pharming).
  • Describe technical defenses (Firewalls, Encryption).
  • Explain the difference between authentication and access rights.
SECTION 03 — CORE THEORY

Threats & Countermeasures

Malware is malicious software. A Virus attaches to files and requires user action to spread. A Worm spreads independently across networks without user action. A Trojan disguises itself as legitimate software.

Social Engineering

Phishing: Sending fake emails pretending to be a bank to steal passwords.

Pharming: Redirecting a user from a legitimate website to a fake one without their knowledge (via DNS poisoning).

Shoulder Surfing: Physically watching someone type their password.

Defenses

Firewalls: Filter incoming and outgoing network traffic based on rules.

Encryption: Scrambles data so only authorized users with the decryption key can read it.

Multi-factor Authentication (MFA): Requires two or more proofs of identity (e.g., password + SMS code).

SECTION 04 — KEY DEFINITIONS

Examination Definitions

CYBERSECURITY: The protection of computer systems and data from theft, damage, or unauthorized access.

MALWARE: Software intentionally designed to cause damage or gain unauthorized access to a system.

ENCRYPTION: The process of converting readable plaintext into unreadable ciphertext.

Brute-Force Attack Simulation

SECTION 07 — EXAM TECHNIQUE

Discussing Security

Always pair a threat with a specific countermeasure. Don't just say "use a firewall". Say "Install a firewall to inspect incoming packets and block unauthorized IP addresses from accessing the server."

SECTION 08 — COMMON MISTAKES

Avoid These Errors

Mistake: Saying "Antivirus software protects against phishing".

Correction: Antivirus scans files for malware. Phishing is social engineering. The defense against phishing is user education and email spam filters.

SECTION 09 — QUICK REVISION

Fast Facts

  • Viruses need user action; Worms self-replicate.
  • Trojans look legitimate but contain hidden malware.
  • MFA requires "something you know" + "something you have".
  • Encryption protects data confidentiality; it does not prevent hacking of the system itself.
SECTION 10 — EXAM QUESTIONS

Test Your Knowledge

Question 1 (Standard)

A company is worried about employees falling for phishing emails. Describe two technical or human measures the company can take to reduce this risk.

2 marks: 1) Provide cybersecurity training so employees know how to spot fake URLs and unexpected attachments. 2) Install email filtering software to quarantine suspicious emails before they reach the inbox.
