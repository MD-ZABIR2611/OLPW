Cells & Movement of Substances

CORE
SECTION 01 — CHAPTER OVERVIEW

Cellular Transport

Cells require nutrients and oxygen to survive, and must remove waste products. This chapter covers the physical processes that move substances into and out of cells: diffusion, osmosis, and active transport.

You will also learn how the surface area to volume ratio affects the rate of these processes, which is a crucial concept for understanding why organisms have specialised exchange surfaces.

Chapter Goals

SECTION 03 — CORE THEORY

The Three Transport Mechanisms

Diffusion

Diffusion is the net movement of particles from a region of higher concentration to a region of lower concentration, down a concentration gradient. It is a passive process (requires no energy) and occurs in gases and liquids.

Osmosis

Osmosis is a specific type of diffusion. It is the net movement of water molecules from a region of higher water potential (dilute solution) to a region of lower water potential (concentrated solution), through a partially permeable membrane.

Active Transport

Unlike diffusion and osmosis, active transport requires energy from respiration. It is the movement of particles from a region of lower concentration to a region of higher concentration (against a concentration gradient). This is how root hair cells absorb mineral ions from the soil.

Surface Area to Volume Ratio

For substances to enter or leave a cell, they must cross the cell membrane. The larger the surface area of the membrane relative to the cell's volume, the faster the rate of diffusion. This is why cells are microscopic and why organs like the small intestine have millions of villi.

SECTION 04 — KEY DEFINITIONS

Examination Definitions

DIFFUSION: The net movement of particles from a region of higher concentration to a region of lower concentration, down a concentration gradient.

OSMOSIS: The net movement of water molecules from a region of higher water potential to a region of lower water potential, through a partially permeable membrane.

ACTIVE TRANSPORT: The movement of particles from a region of lower concentration to a region of higher concentration, against a concentration gradient, requiring energy from respiration.

SECTION 06 — PRACTICAL LAB

Potato Osmosis Investigation

AIM

To investigate the effect of different sucrose concentrations on the mass of potato cylinders.

VARIABLES

Independent: Concentration of sucrose solution (0.0M, 0.2M, 0.4M...).

Dependent: Change in mass of potato cylinders.

Control: Volume of solution, temperature, time, potato type.

CALCULATION

Percentage Change = (Final Mass - Initial Mass) / Initial Mass × 100

If mass increases, water entered (solution had higher water potential). If mass decreases, water left (solution had lower water potential).

SECTION 07 — EXAM TECHNIQUE

Describing Graphs

When asked to describe osmosis results, do not just say "it went up". State the concentration, the direction of change, and the biological reason.

Good Answer: "As sucrose concentration increases above 0.4M, the mass of the potato decreases because water moves out of the potato cells by osmosis into the sucrose solution."

SECTION 08 — COMMON MISTAKES

Avoid These Errors

Mistake: Saying osmosis is the movement of "solute" or "particles".

Correction: Osmosis is strictly the movement of water molecules.

Mistake: Forgetting to mention the partially permeable membrane in the osmosis definition.

SECTION 09 — QUICK REVISION

Fast Facts

  • Diffusion: High to low concentration (Passive).
  • Osmosis: High water potential to low water potential through a membrane (Passive).
  • Active Transport: Low to high concentration (Requires energy).
  • Always calculate percentage change in potato practicals to compare fairly.
SECTION 10 — EXAM QUESTIONS

Test Your Knowledge

Question 1 (Standard)

A student places a potato cylinder in pure water. After 1 hour, its mass increases from 10.0g to 10.5g. Calculate the percentage change in mass.

2 marks:

Change = 10.5 - 10.0 = +0.5g

% Change = (0.5 / 10.0) × 100 = +5%

Question 2 (Challenge)

Explain why root hair cells use active transport, rather than diffusion, to absorb mineral ions from the soil.

3 marks: The concentration of mineral ions inside the root hair cell is higher than the concentration in the soil. Diffusion would cause ions to move out of the cell. Active transport allows the cell to move ions against the concentration gradient (from low to high), using energy from respiration.
