Chapter Overview

What is it?

Some reactions go both ways at once. This chapter covers reversible reactions, dynamic equilibrium, how conditions shift equilibria (Le Chatelier's principle), and how all of it is applied in the Haber process to make ammonia for fertilisers.

Why It Matters

Almost every industrial chemical process - ammonia, sulfuric acid, ethanol - runs on a carefully chosen compromise of temperature, pressure and catalyst to maximise yield AND speed at minimum cost.

What You Will Learn
  • Reversible reactions and dynamic equilibrium
  • Le Chatelier's principle (concentration, pressure, temperature)
  • The Haber process conditions and compromises
  • Recycling of unreacted gases
  • Making ammonium fertilisers

Detailed Theory

01

Reversible Reactions & Dynamic Equilibrium

Reversible reaction: a reaction where the products can react again to reform the original reactants. The symbol ⇌ shows two-way reaction.

Dynamic equilibrium: in a CLOSED system, when the forward and reverse reactions happen at the SAME rate. The concentrations of reactants and products stay constant (not equal!) because both reactions are still occurring.

Example: hydrated copper(II) sulfate ⇌ anhydrous copper(II) sulfate + water. Heating drives it one way (blue to white), adding water drives it back (white to blue) - a classic reversible change.

02

Le Chatelier's Principle

Principle: when a change is made to the conditions of a system at equilibrium, the position of equilibrium shifts to OPPOSE the change.

  • Concentration: adding more reactant shifts equilibrium towards the products.
  • Pressure (gases): increasing pressure shifts equilibrium to the side with FEWER moles of gas.
  • Temperature: increasing temperature favours the ENDOTHERMIC direction; decreasing temperature favours the EXOTHERMIC direction.
  • Catalyst: speeds up BOTH directions equally - it does NOT change the position of equilibrium or the yield, only how fast equilibrium is reached.
03

The Haber Process

Purpose: the industrial manufacture of ammonia (NH₃) from nitrogen and hydrogen.

  • Step 1: N₂ is obtained from fractional distillation of liquefied air; H₂ from natural gas (methane) reacting with steam.
  • Step 2: Purified gases are passed over an IRON catalyst at 450°C and 200 atm.
  • Step 3: Only about 15% converts per pass; the ammonia is cooled and liquefied to remove it, and unreacted N₂/H₂ are RECYCLED back over the catalyst.

Why 450°C? The forward reaction is exothermic, so low temperature gives high yield but is too slow; 450°C is a compromise giving acceptable yield at an economic rate. Why 200 atm? High pressure favours the product side (4 moles of gas → 2 moles) but higher pressures cost too much to build and run safely.

04

Ammonia into Fertilisers

Ammonia is neutralised with acids to make nitrogen fertilisers: NH₃ + HNO₃ → NH₄NO₃ (ammonium nitrate), 2NH₃ + H₂SO₄ → (NH₄)₂SO₄ (ammonium sulfate). Fertilisers replace nitrates in the soil so plants can make amino acids and proteins - growing bigger crops.

Worked Examples

Case Study 01 / Intermediate

Predicting the Shift

Question: For N₂(g) + 3H₂(g) ⇌ 2NH₃(g) (exothermic), state and explain the effect on the yield of ammonia of (a) increasing pressure, (b) increasing temperature.

(a) Increasing pressure shifts equilibrium to the side with fewer gas moles - the NH₃ side (2 moles vs 4) - so yield INCREASES.

(b) The forward reaction is exothermic; raising temperature favours the endothermic (reverse) direction, so yield DECREASES.

Case Study 02 / Advanced

The Catalyst Trap

Question: A student claims adding more iron catalyst increases the yield of ammonia. Explain the error.

A catalyst speeds up the forward AND reverse reactions equally (1). It helps the system reach equilibrium FASTER but does not shift the position of equilibrium, so the yield is unchanged (1).

08

Laboratory Protocol

Experiment 01 / Reversible Change

Blue ↔ White Copper Sulfate

Method: Gently heat blue hydrated copper(II) sulfate crystals in a test tube. Then add a few drops of water to the white residue.

Observation: Heating turns the crystals WHITE (water driven off, condensation appears); adding water turns it BLUE again with heat released.

Exam Application: This proves the reaction is reversible: CuSO₄·5H₂O ⇌ CuSO₄ + 5H₂O. It is also used as a test for water - white CuSO₄ turning blue shows water is present.

09

Error Database

Wrong

A catalyst increases the yield of ammonia in the Haber process.

Correct

A catalyst changes only the RATE - it speeds up both directions equally and the yield stays exactly the same.

Wrong

At equilibrium the amounts of reactants and products are equal.

Correct

At equilibrium the RATES are equal and concentrations are CONSTANT - but usually not equal to each other.

Wrong

Equilibrium can be reached in an open beaker with gas escaping.

Correct

Equilibrium needs a CLOSED system - if gas escapes, the system never reaches equilibrium.

Exam Simulation

Basic

1. State the catalyst, temperature and pressure used in the Haber process.

Mark Scheme Answer [3 Marks]

Iron catalyst (1). 450°C (1). 200 atm (1).

Advanced

2. Explain why unreacted nitrogen and hydrogen are recycled in the Haber process, and why ammonia is removed by cooling.

Mark Scheme Answer [3 Marks]

Only about 15% of the mixture converts each pass, so recycling wastes nothing and keeps production economical (1). Removing the ammonia as a liquid reduces the product concentration (1). By Le Chatelier's principle, equilibrium shifts right to replace it, increasing the overall yield (1).
