Chapter Overview

What is it?

This chapter details the chemistry of carbonates, focusing on limestone (calcium carbonate). It covers thermal decomposition, the "limestone cycle," and the manufacture of cement, concrete, and glass.

Why It Matters

Limestone is a vital industrial raw material. It is used extensively in construction, agriculture (to neutralize acidic soil), and in the manufacture of cement, mortar, and glass, which are the building blocks of modern infrastructure.

Detailed Theory

01

Thermal Decomposition of Carbonates

Definition: When carbonates are heated, they break down (decompose) into the metal oxide and carbon dioxide.

General Equation: Metal Carbonate → Metal Oxide + Carbon Dioxide.

Trend: The more reactive the metal, the more stable its carbonate is to heat. Sodium and potassium carbonates do not decompose with a Bunsen burner. Copper(II) carbonate decomposes easily (green powder turns black).

02

The Limestone Cycle

A series of reactions that demonstrates the properties of calcium compounds:

  • Step 1 (Calcination): Heat limestone (CaCO₃) to form quicklime (CaO) and CO₂.
  • Step 2 (Slaking): Add water to quicklime to form slaked lime (Ca(OH)₂). This is highly exothermic.
  • Step 3 (Carbonation): Add more water and bubble CO₂ through the solution. It turns milky as insoluble CaCO₃ reforms.

Worked Examples

Case Study 02 / Intermediate

Identify the Gas

Question: A student heats a green powder and it turns black. A gas is evolved that turns limewater milky. Identify the green powder and the black solid.

1. The gas is CO₂ (turns limewater milky), meaning a carbonate decomposed.

2. The green powder must be Copper(II) carbonate (CuCO₃).

3. The black solid is Copper(II) oxide (CuO).

Answer: Green = CuCO₃. Black = CuO.

09

Laboratory Protocol

Experiment 01 / Thermal Decomposition of CuCO₃

Heating a Carbonate

Method: Place a spatula of copper(II) carbonate in a boiling tube. Heat with a Bunsen burner. Deliver the gas produced into limewater using a delivery tube.

Exam Application: Examiners ask why the limewater turns milky. Answer: The CO₂ gas produced reacts with calcium hydroxide in the limewater to form insoluble calcium carbonate, which appears as a milky suspension.

11

Error Database

Wrong

Limewater is pure water from limestone areas.

Correct

Limewater is an aqueous solution of calcium hydroxide (Ca(OH)₂). It is used to test for the presence of carbon dioxide gas.

Wrong

Sodium carbonate decomposes easily when heated.

Correct

Sodium is a very reactive metal. Its carbonate is thermally stable and will not decompose with a standard Bunsen burner flame.

Mark Scheme Answer [2 Marks]

Advanced

2. Explain why calcium oxide (quicklime) is used to neutralize acidic soils, and write the equation.

Mark Scheme Answer [3 Marks]

1. Calcium oxide is a base. It reacts with and neutralizes the acid in the soil.
2. It is cheaper and works faster than limestone (which is insoluble).
3. Equation: CaO + 2HCl → CaCl₂ + H₂O (or general acid neutralization).
