Thermal Energy Transfer

CORE
THEORY

Conduction, Convection & Radiation

Thermal energy moves from hot to cold regions via three primary mechanisms: Conduction, Convection, and Radiation. Conduction occurs mainly in solids where vibrating particles pass energy to neighbors. Convection occurs in fluids where hot, less dense liquid/gas rises and cold sinks. Radiation uses infrared waves and requires no medium.

Dull, black surfaces are the best emitters and absorbers of infrared radiation. Shiny, white surfaces are poor emitters and absorbers, making them good reflectors.

DEFINITIONS

Key Terms

Conduction: Transfer of thermal energy through a material without the material itself moving.

Convection: Transfer of thermal energy in a fluid by the movement of the fluid itself.

Radiation: Transfer of thermal energy via infrared electromagnetic waves, requiring no particles.

EXAM PRACTICE

Worked Example

Question 1 (Core)

Explain why a vacuum flask has a silvered inner surface.

The vacuum flask prevents heat transfer by conduction and convection because there are no particles in a vacuum.

The silvered surface reflects infrared radiation back into the hot liquid, preventing heat loss by radiation.
