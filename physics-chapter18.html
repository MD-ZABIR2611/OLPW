Sound & Ultrasound

CORE
THEORY

What Sound Is

Sound is a longitudinal wave of compressions and rarefactions travelling through a medium. It is produced by vibrating sources and CANNOT travel through a vacuum, because there are no particles to vibrate.

Typical speed: about 330-350 m/s in air, faster in liquids and fastest in solids. Loudness depends on amplitude; pitch depends on frequency.

Hearing and Echoes

Humans hear roughly 20 Hz to 20 000 Hz (20 kHz). Above 20 kHz is ultrasound.

An echo is reflected sound. For a cliff at distance d, the sound travels there and back: speed = 2d / t.

Ultrasound uses: sonar (depth of sea, shoals of fish), non-invasive prenatal scanning, cleaning and quality testing of metals.

DEFINITIONS

Longitudinal Wave: A wave whose oscillations are parallel to the direction of travel, made of compressions and rarefactions - like sound.

Compression: The region of a sound wave where the particles are squashed closest together and pressure is highest.

Rarefaction: The region of a sound wave where particles are spread furthest apart and pressure is lowest.

Echo: Sound reflected off a hard surface and heard again; used to measure distances via speed = 2d / t.

Pitch: How high or low a note sounds; set by the frequency of the wave.

Loudness: How strong a sound seems; set by the amplitude of the vibrations.

Ultrasound: Sound with frequency above 20 000 Hz, beyond human hearing; used in sonar and medical scanning.

FORMULAE & CALCULATIONS

Echo Equation

Remember the sound travels to the surface AND back.

speed = 2 × distance to wall / time
PRACTICAL WORK

Measure the speed of sound: stand a known distance from a large wall, clap and listen for the echo, or use two microphones and an electronic timer. Sound needs a medium - a bell in a vacuum jar goes silent as the air is pumped out.

EXAM PRACTICE

Test Your Knowledge

Question 1

A student fires a starter pistol towards a cliff and hears the echo after 1.5 s. Sound travels at 340 m/s. How far away is the cliff?

Total distance = speed × time = 340 × 1.5 = 510 m (1).

Distance to cliff = 510 / 2 = 255 m (1).

Question 2

Explain why an astronaut on the Moon cannot hear an explosion nearby.

Sound is a longitudinal wave that needs particles to travel (1).

The Moon has no atmosphere - it is a vacuum, so the vibrations cannot reach the astronaut (1).
