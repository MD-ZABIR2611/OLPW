Chapter Overview

What is it?

This chapter introduces the kinetic particle theory, explaining how all matter is made of particles in constant motion. It covers the differences between solids, liquids, and gases, and what happens during changes of state.

Why It Matters

Understanding particle theory is the foundation of all chemistry. It explains physical properties, why reactions occur at different rates, and how gases behave under pressure and temperature changes.

What You Will Learn
  • States of matter & particle arrangement
  • Kinetic theory & particle movement
  • Changes of state (melting, boiling, etc.)
  • Heating and cooling curves
  • Diffusion and Brownian motion
  • Effects of temperature and pressure on gases

03

Detailed Theory

01

States of Matter & Particle Model

Solid: Particles are packed tightly in a rigid lattice. They vibrate about fixed positions. Strong intermolecular forces. Incompressible, fixed shape and volume.

Liquid: Particles are close together but can slide past one another. Weaker forces than solids. Flow to take the shape of the container, fixed volume, incompressible.

Gas: Particles are far apart and move randomly at high speeds. Negligible forces. Highly compressible, no fixed shape or volume.

02

Changes of State

Melting: Solid to liquid. Heat energy breaks the lattice bonds.

Boiling: Liquid to gas. Occurs at a specific boiling point throughout the liquid.

Evaporation: Liquid to gas, occurring only at the surface at any temperature.

Sublimation: Solid directly to gas (e.g., dry ice, iodine).

Why temperature stays constant: During melting or boiling, the thermal energy supplied is used to overcome intermolecular forces, not to increase kinetic energy. Therefore, temperature plateaus.

03

Diffusion & Brownian Motion

Diffusion: The net movement of particles from a region of high concentration to a region of low concentration, down the concentration gradient.

Why it happens: Particles possess kinetic energy and move randomly, eventually spreading out evenly.

Rate of Diffusion: Increases with temperature (higher kinetic energy). Decreases with particle mass (heavier particles move slower). This is why ammonia (lighter) diffuses faster than hydrogen chloride (heavier).

Worked Examples

Case Study 01 / Basic

Explain Compression

Question: Explain why a gas can be compressed, but a solid cannot.

1. Gas particles are far apart with large empty spaces between them.

2. Solid particles are tightly packed in a lattice with almost no space.

Answer = Gases have large spaces between particles, allowing them to be pushed closer. Solids have no space.

09

Laboratory Protocol

Experiment 01 / Diffusion of Gases

Ammonia and Hydrogen Chloride

Method: Cotton wool soaked in concentrated ammonia is placed at one end of a glass tube. HCl soaked cotton is placed at the other. The white ring of ammonium chloride forms closer to the HCl end.

Exam Application: Examiners ask why the ring is closer to the HCl. Answer: Ammonia particles are lighter, move faster, and diffuse further than HCl particles in the same time.

11

Error Database

Wrong

Particles become larger when a substance is heated.

Correct

Particles do not change size. They gain kinetic energy and move faster/vibrate more vigorously. The spaces between them increase.

Wrong

During boiling, the temperature stops rising because the heat stops being applied.

Correct

Temperature stops rising because the heat energy is being used to break intermolecular forces (overcome attraction), rather than increase kinetic energy.

Exam Simulation

Basic

1. Describe the arrangement and movement of particles in a liquid.

Mark Scheme Answer [2 Marks]

Arrangement: Close together/random. Movement: Slide past each other / move in streams.

Intermediate

2. Explain, in terms of particles, why the temperature of boiling water remains constant even though heat is still being applied.

Mark Scheme Answer [2 Marks]

The heat energy is used to overcome the intermolecular forces of attraction between particles, rather than increasing their kinetic energy.

Advanced

3. Explain why an increase in temperature increases the rate of diffusion.

Mark Scheme Answer [3 Marks]

1. Higher temperature means particles have more kinetic energy.

2. They move faster and more vigorously.

3. Therefore, they spread out and mix with other particles more quickly.
