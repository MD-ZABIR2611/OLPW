SECTION 01

CHAPTER OVERVIEW

WHAT IS IT?

Stoichiometry is the calculation of reactants and products in chemical reactions. Before this chapter, you learned *what* happens in a reaction (atoms rearrange). Now, you will learn *how much* of each substance is involved.

WHY IT MATTERS

In the real world, chemicals cost money. A factory making medicines cannot afford to waste reactants or produce toxic by-products. Stoichiometry allows chemists to calculate exact masses, volumes, and concentrations needed to make a specific product.

WHAT YOU WILL LEARN
  • Relative masses ($A_r$, $M_r$)
  • The mole concept and Avogadro's constant
  • Balancing equations and mole ratios
  • Reacting masses, gas volumes, and concentration
  • Limiting and excess reactants
  • Percentage yield and composition
  • Empirical and molecular formulae

SECTION 03

DETAILED THEORY

01 / RELATIVE MASSES

RELATIVE ATOMIC & FORMULA MASS

DEFINITION

Atoms are incredibly small. Weighing a single atom is practically impossible, so chemists use a relative scale based on the mass of a carbon-12 atom. Relative Atomic Mass ($A_r$) is the average mass of an atom of an element relative to 1/12th the mass of an atom of carbon-12.

EXPLANATION

Relative Formula Mass ($M_r$) or Relative Molecular Mass is the sum of the relative atomic masses of all atoms in a molecule or formula unit. You can find $A_r$ on the periodic table (usually the larger number above the symbol).

EXAMPLE

Calculate the $M_r$ of $H_2O$.
($A_r$ of H = 1, $A_r$ of O = 16)
$M_r = (2 \times 1) + 16 = 18$.

EXAM CONNECTION

A common question asks students to calculate $M_r$ for ionic compounds. Remember: never write units for $A_r$ or $M_r$ as they are relative ratios.

02 / THE MOLE

THE MOLE & AVOGADRO CONSTANT

DEFINITION

A "mole" is simply a specific number of particles, just like a "dozen" means 12. Because atoms are so tiny, a mole represents a massive number of particles ($6.02 \times 10^{23}$). The mass of one mole of a substance is equal to its relative mass in grams (e.g., 1 mole of $H_2O$ has a mass of 18 g).

WHY IT HAPPENS

The Avogadro constant ($L$) is defined as the number of particles per mole ($6.02 \times 10^{23} \text{ mol}^{-1}$). It bridges the microscopic world of atoms with the macroscopic world of grams.

EXAM CONNECTION

You must state the value of $L$ ($6.02 \times 10^{23}$). When asked to convert between particles and moles, always divide the number of particles by $L$.

03 / EQUATIONS

BALANCED EQUATIONS & MOLE RATIOS

DEFINITION

A balanced chemical equation shows the exact ratio of moles of reactants and products.

EXPLANATION

Unbalanced: $H_2 + O_2 \rightarrow H_2O$ (Oxygen atoms don't match).
Balanced: $2H_2 + O_2 \rightarrow 2H_2O$.
This means 2 moles of hydrogen react with 1 mole of oxygen to produce 2 moles of water.

EXAM CONNECTION

In calculations, always write the balanced equation. The coefficients (big numbers) are the mole ratios you will use to convert moles of reactant to moles of product.

04 / REACTANTS

LIMITING & EXCESS REACTANTS

DEFINITION

Limiting Reactant: The reactant that is completely used up first. It dictates exactly how much product can be made.
Excess Reactant: The reactant that is left over once the reaction is complete.

WHY IT HAPPENS

Reactants rarely exist in the exact mole ratio required by the balanced equation. One will run out first, stopping the reaction.

EXAM CONNECTION

To identify the limiting reactant, calculate the moles of each reactant. Divide the moles by the coefficient from the balanced equation. The smallest value is the limiting reactant.

05 / YIELD

PERCENTAGE YIELD & COMPOSITION

DEFINITION

Percentage Yield: $\frac{\text{actual mass produced}}{\text{theoretical maximum mass}} \times 100$.
Percentage Composition: The percentage by mass of a specific element in a compound.

EXPLANATION

No reaction is perfectly efficient. Some product is lost during filtration, transfers, or the reaction is reversible. For composition: in water ($H_2O$), $M_r = 18$, mass of $O = 16$. $\% O = (16 / 18) \times 100 = 88.9\%$.

EXAM CONNECTION

Percentage yield can never exceed 100%. If your calculated yield is >100%, you have made a calculation error.

06 / FORMULAE

EMPIRICAL & MOLECULAR FORMULAE

DEFINITION

Empirical Formula: The simplest whole number ratio of atoms of each element in a compound (e.g., $CH_2$).
Molecular Formula: The actual number of atoms of each element in one molecule (e.g., $C_2H_4$).

HOW TO DETERMINE

1. Divide the mass (or %) of each element by its $A_r$ to get moles.
2. Find the simplest whole number ratio by dividing by the smallest number of moles.
3. For molecular formula, divide the Molecular Mass by the Empirical Mass to find the multiplier.

EXAM CONNECTION

When given percentage composition, assume a 100g sample. This makes percentages directly equal to mass in grams.

07 / GASES

MOLAR VOLUME OF GASES

DEFINITION

At room temperature and pressure (rtp), one mole of *any* gas occupies a volume of $24 \text{ dm}^3$ ($24,000 \text{ cm}^3$).

EXPLANATION

If a question mentions gas volume, use the formula: $moles = \frac{volume}{24}$ (if volume is in $dm^3$). This replaces the need to use $mass/M_r$ for gases.

EXAM CONNECTION

Always check units. If volume is in $cm^3$, convert to $dm^3$ by dividing by 1000, OR use $moles = \frac{volume}{24000}$.

08 / CONCENTRATION

CONCENTRATION OF SOLUTIONS

DEFINITION

Concentration tells you how much solute is dissolved in a solvent. It is measured in $mol/dm^3$ or $g/dm^3$.

EXPLANATION

$1000 \text{ cm}^3 = 1 \text{ dm}^3$. To convert $mol/dm^3$ to $g/dm^3$, multiply by the $M_r$ of the solute.

EXAM CONNECTION

In titration calculations, always convert volume from $cm^3$ to $dm^3$ before applying $C = n/V$.

WHERE: mass $\rightarrow$ g | $M_r \rightarrow$ g/mol

WHERE: particles $\rightarrow$ number of atoms/molecules

WHERE: volume $\rightarrow$ $dm^3$ (at rtp)

WORKED EXAMPLES

CASE STUDY 01 / BASIC MOLES CALCULATION
QUESTIONCalculate the number of moles in 5.0 g of NaOH.
INFORMATION GIVENMass = 5.0 g, Substance = NaOH
FINDMoles
FORMULAmoles = mass / $M_r$
SUBSTITUTIONCalculate $M_r$: Na(23) + O(16) + H(1) = 40
WORKINGmoles = 5.0 / 40 = 0.125
FINAL ANSWER0.125 mol
CASE STUDY 02 / INTERMEDIATE REACTING MASSES
QUESTIONCalculate the mass of magnesium oxide (MgO) produced when 6.0 g of magnesium (Mg) is burned in excess oxygen.
INFORMATION GIVENMass of Mg = 6.0 g. Oxygen is in excess.
FINDMass of MgO in g
STEP 1: EQUATION2Mg + $O_2 \rightarrow$ 2MgO
STEP 2: MOLES OF REACTANT$M_r$ of Mg = 24. moles = 6.0 / 24 = 0.25 mol.
STEP 3: MOLE RATIORatio of Mg : MgO is 2:2 (or 1:1). Moles of MgO = 0.25 mol.
STEP 4: MASS OF PRODUCT$M_r$ of MgO = 24 + 16 = 40. mass = 0.25 * 40 = 10 g.
FINAL ANSWER10 g
CASE STUDY 03 / ADVANCED MULTI-STEP TITRATION
QUESTION25.0 $cm^3$ of KOH neutralizes 20.0 $cm^3$ of 0.10 $mol/dm^3$ HNO$_3$. Calculate concentration of KOH in $g/dm^3$.
STEP 1: EQUATIONKOH + HNO$_3 \rightarrow$ KNO$_3$ + $H_2O$
STEP 2: MOLES OF ACIDVolume = 20/1000 = 0.020 $dm^3$. moles = C * V = 0.10 * 0.020 = 0.002 mol.
STEP 3: MOLE RATIORatio is 1:1. Moles of KOH = 0.002 mol.
STEP 4: CONCENTRATION (mol)Vol of KOH = 25/1000 = 0.025 $dm^3$. C = 0.002 / 0.025 = 0.08 $mol/dm^3$.
STEP 5: CONCENTRATION (g)$M_r$ of KOH = 39+16+1 = 56. Conc = 0.08 * 56 = 4.48 $g/dm^3$.
FINAL ANSWER4.48 $g/dm^3$

SECTION 09

LABORATORY PROTOCOL

EXPERIMENT 01 / DETERMINE EMPIRICAL FORMULA OF MgO
OBJECTIVE

Determine the empirical formula of magnesium oxide by heating magnesium in a crucible.

APPARATUS

Crucible with lid, pipe-clay triangle, tripod, bunsen burner, tongs, digital balance, magnesium ribbon.

METHOD

1. Weigh crucible + lid. Add Mg ribbon, weigh again.
2. Heat strongly with lid slightly open.
3. Lift lid occasionally to allow oxygen in.
4. Stop when Mg turns into white ash (MgO).
5. Weigh crucible + lid + product.

VARIABLES

Independent: Mass of magnesium used.
Dependent: Mass of magnesium oxide formed.

OBSERVATIONS

Magnesium ribbon glows with a brilliant white light. Forms a white brittle ash.

RESULTS

Calculate mass of Mg used (initial mass - crucible mass). Calculate mass of O reacted (final mass - initial Mg mass). Convert to moles to find ratio (usually 1:1).

ERRORS

1. Product escapes as smoke when lid is lifted.
2. Incomplete combustion (some Mg remains unreacted).
3. Crucible may not be dry.

IMPROVEMENTS

1. Lift lid carefully and minimally.
2. Heat until no further change in mass is observed (ensures complete reaction).

SAFETY PROTOCOL

Do not look directly at the bright flame (UV damage to eyes). Wear safety goggles. Crucible will remain extremely hot for a long time after heating; use tongs.

ERROR DATABASE

WRONG
Forgetting to balance the equation before calculating mole ratios.
CORRECT
Always write the balanced equation first. The coefficients (big numbers) give you the exact mole ratio needed to convert moles of reactant to moles of product.
WRONG
Calculating percentage yield > 100%.
CORRECT
Percentage yield can never exceed 100%. If it does, you have made a calculation error, the product is wet (contains water mass), or it is contaminated.

CORRECT
Empirical is the simplest ratio (e.g., $CH_2$). Molecular is the actual molecule (e.g., $C_2H_4$). You must divide molecular mass by empirical mass to find the multiplier.

RAPID REVISION

KEY DEFINITIONS

  • Mole: $6.02 \times 10^{23}$ particles
  • $A_r$: Mass relative to 1/12th C-12
  • Empirical: Simplest ratio
  • Limiting Reactant: Used up first

KEY EQUATIONS

  • $moles = mass / M_r$
  • $moles = volume / 24$ (Gas at rtp)
  • $Conc = moles / volume (dm^3)$
  • $\% Yield = (actual / theoretical) * 100$

KEY FACTS

  • 1 mole of gas = $24 dm^3$ at rtp
  • $1000 cm^3 = 1 dm^3$
  • Yield is never > 100%
  • Assume 100g if given % composition

KEY EXAM POINTS

  • Check volume units ($dm^3$ vs $cm^3$)
  • Always balance equations first
  • Show every step in calculations
  • Round final answer to 3 sig figs

EXAM SIMULATION

BASIC / MULTIPLE CHOICE MARKS: 1

What is the mass of one mole of calcium carbonate ($CaCO_3$)? ($A_r$: Ca=40, C=12, O=16)

MARK SCHEME ANSWER (1 MARK)

B) 100 g.
Working: $40 + 12 + (16 \times 3) = 100$ g.

BASIC / STATE MARKS: 1

State the value of the Avogadro constant.

MARK SCHEME ANSWER (1 MARK)

$6.02 \times 10^{23}$ (per mole).
Common mistake: Writing $10^{23}$ without 6.02.

INTERMEDIATE / CALCULATE MARKS: 3

Calculate the number of moles in $48 dm^3$ of carbon dioxide gas at room temperature and pressure (rtp).

MARK SCHEME ANSWER (3 MARKS)

1. Moles = volume / 24.
2. Substitution: 48 / 24.
3. Answer: 2.0 mol.

INTERMEDIATE / EXPLAIN MARKS: 2

Explain why a reaction might not give a 100% yield.

MARK SCHEME ANSWER (2 MARKS)

Any two from:
1. The reaction is reversible.
2. Product is lost during filtration/transfer.
3. Reactants may be impure.
4. Side reactions occur producing unexpected by-products.

ADVANCED / MULTI-STEP CALCULATE MARKS: 5

$25.0 cm^3$ of potassium hydroxide ($KOH$) solution exactly neutralises $20.0 cm^3$ of $0.10 mol/dm^3$ nitric acid ($HNO_3$). Calculate the concentration of the $KOH$ solution in $g/dm^3$. ($A_r$: K=39, O=16, H=1)

MARK SCHEME ANSWER (5 MARKS)

1. Moles of acid = (20/1000) * 0.10 = 0.002 mol. (1 mark)
2. Equation: KOH + HNO$_3 \rightarrow$ KNO$_3$ + $H_2O$. Mole ratio 1:1. Moles KOH = 0.002 mol. (1 mark)
3. Conc of KOH = 0.002 / (25/1000) = 0.08 $mol/dm^3$. (1 mark)
4. $M_r$ of KOH = 39 + 16 + 1 = 56. (1 mark)
5. Conc in $g/dm^3$ = 0.08 * 56 = 4.48 $g/dm^3$. (1 mark)
