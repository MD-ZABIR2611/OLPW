Turning Effects & Centre of Gravity

CORE EXTENDED
THEORY

Moments & Equilibrium

When a force is applied to an object that can pivot, it causes a turning effect called a moment. The size of the moment depends on the magnitude of the force and the perpendicular distance from the pivot to the line of action of the force.

The principle of moments states that for an object to be in equilibrium (balanced), the sum of clockwise moments must equal the sum of anticlockwise moments. The centre of gravity is the single point where the entire weight of an object appears to act.

DEFINITIONS

Key Terms

Moment: The turning effect of a force, measured in newton-meters (N·m).

Centre of Gravity: The point through which the entire weight of an object appears to act.

FORMULAE & CALCULATIONS

Calculating Moments

Moment (M) = Force (F) × perpendicular distance (d)
VISUALS / FORCE DIAGRAMS

Lever System

A simple lever uses the principle of moments. A small force applied far from the pivot can balance a large force placed close to the pivot.

EXAM PRACTICE

Worked Example

Question 1 (Core)

A spanner is 20 cm long. A force of 30 N is applied at the end to tighten a nut. Calculate the moment of the force.

Convert distance: d = 0.2 m

Formula: M = F × d

Substitution: M = 30 N × 0.2 m

Answer: M = 6 N·m
