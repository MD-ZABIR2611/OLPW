Choosing a Team Project Topic

The team project starts long before any research: choosing the right issue and framing it as a focused question decides how well the whole project can go.

08Key terms

01

Key Concepts

01

The team project is the assessed group component: candidates work in a team to research an issue of global significance and produce a collaborative outcome.

02

A strong topic is debatable, so reasonable people hold different perspectives and there is an argument to analyse rather than a fact to report.

03

A strong topic is researchable from both local and global angles, with accessible primary sources such as interviews and surveys plus reliable secondary sources.

04

A strong topic is manageable: focused enough for the time available, so teams narrow broad themes like climate change to a specific question about their own community.

05

Team outcomes can take many forms — a campaign, website, exhibition, video, article or presentation — but must show genuine collaboration and be aimed at a real audience.

06

Successful teams plan with an action plan and timeline, allocate research areas to members, and meet regularly; the individual reports then examine each member's own research question.

02

Key Terms

TermExam definition
Team projectThe assessed group component in which candidates work as a team to research an issue of global significance and produce a collaborative outcome.
Team outcomeThe shared product of a Global Perspectives team project, such as a campaign, website, exhibition or video, aimed at a real audience.
Global issueA question or problem that affects people in more than one country and can be viewed from local, national and international perspectives.
Research questionA focused question that guides an investigation and can be answered through evidence, rather than a broad topic or statement.
StakeholderA person or group affected by, or with an interest in, an issue, such as governments, businesses, communities or individuals.
PerspectiveA particular attitude or viewpoint towards an issue, often shaped by a stakeholder's culture, values, experience or interests.
FeasibilityThe practicality of a proposed project, judged by the time, access to sources, skills and resources available to complete it.
Action planA working document that breaks a project into tasks with deadlines and responsibilities, used by a team to manage its time.
03

Exam Tip

Exam tip

If asked to improve a proposed topic, follow the chain: too broad, then a narrower focused question, then check it is debatable, researchable and manageable. Quoting a possible research question is an easy way to prove a focused mind.

04

Practice Questions

Structured practice — write your answer, then reveal the model.

FOUNDATION

State four characteristics of an effective Global Perspectives team project topic.

Model answerIt addresses an issue of global significance, relevant in more than one country (1). It is debatable, so people hold different reasonable perspectives (1). It is researchable locally and globally, with sources and people available to study (1). It is manageable within the time and scope allowed for the project (1).
CHALLENGE

A team proposes the topic "social media" for its team project. Evaluate this choice and explain how the team could turn it into a successful project.

Model answer"Social media" is too broad: it names a subject rather than a question, so the project could become a description instead of an argument (1). It is suitable in principle because social media has clear global and local relevance, and people hold contrasting views about its effects (1). The team should narrow it to a focused question, such as whether social media does more to connect or to divide young people in their community (1). The refined question must remain debatable, researchable and manageable, with primary evidence such as a local survey available (1). Judgement: with a sharper question the topic is strong, so the project should proceed after refinement (1).
