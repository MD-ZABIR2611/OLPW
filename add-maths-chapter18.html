Trigonometric Identities and Equations in Depth

Moving beyond right-angled triangles: manipulating the Pythagorean identities, proving results rigorously, and capturing every solution inside a stated interval.

Key Concepts

01

The identity sin²θ + cos²θ = 1 holds for all θ; dividing it by cos²θ gives sec²θ = 1 + tan²θ, and dividing by sin²θ gives cosec²θ = 1 + cot²θ. These relationships are given in the List of formulas.

02

tan θ = sin θ/cos θ, sec θ = 1/cos θ, cosec θ = 1/sin θ and cot θ = cos θ/sin θ; converting everything to sin and cos is the standard way to simplify expressions and solve equations.

03

To solve an equation, reduce it to a single function of a single angle, find the principal value from the calculator, then use the symmetry of the graph or a CAST diagram to list every solution in the given domain, in degrees or radians as requested.

04

Equations with a multiple angle such as 2θ or 3θ are solved for the multiple angle over the stretched domain first, then every solution is divided by the coefficient, discarding any values that fall outside the original domain.

05

Equations with sec, cosec or cot are rewritten in terms of cos, sin or tan first, noting any restrictions, e.g. cos θ = 0 would make sec θ undefined, so such values must be rejected.

06

To prove an identity, start from the more complicated side, express everything in sin and cos, simplify using the identities, and arrive at the other side; terms must never be moved across the equals sign during a proof.

02

Key Terms

TermExam definition
Trigonometric identityAn equation involving trigonometric functions that is true for every value of the angle for which both sides are defined.
Principal valueThe calculator solution of a trigonometric equation in the range −90° to 90°, from which all other solutions are found by symmetry.
PeriodThe interval over which a trigonometric graph repeats; for sin bθ and cos bθ it is 360°/b, or 2π/b radians.
AmplitudeThe maximum displacement of a trigonometric graph from its centre line; for a sin bθ + c the amplitude is a.
SecantThe reciprocal of the cosine function, sec θ = 1/cos θ, which is undefined wherever cos θ = 0.
CosecantThe reciprocal of the sine function, cosec θ = 1/sin θ, which is undefined wherever sin θ = 0.
CotangentThe reciprocal of the tangent function, cot θ = 1/tan θ = cos θ/sin θ, undefined wherever sin θ = 0.
CAST diagramA memory aid showing which trigonometric functions are positive in each quadrant, used to find all solutions of an equation.
03

Exam Tip

Exam tip

After finding the principal value, list every solution inside the given interval before dividing by a coefficient for multiple-angle equations, and give radian answers as exact multiples of π — a decimal such as 1.05 instead of π/3 loses the final accuracy mark.

Practice Questions

Structured practice — write your answer, then reveal the model.

FOUNDATION

Given that sin θ = 3/5 and θ is acute, use the identity sin²θ + cos²θ = 1 to find the exact values of cos θ and tan θ.

Model answercos²θ = 1 − sin²θ = 1 − 9/25 = 16/25 (1). Since θ is acute, cos θ = 4/5 (1). tan θ = sin θ/cos θ = (3/5)/(4/5) = 3/4 (1). So the exact values are cos θ = 4/5 and tan θ = 3/4, matching the 3-4-5 triangle (1).
CHALLENGE

Prove the identity sin θ tan θ + cos θ = sec θ, showing every step of your reasoning.

Model answerStart with the left-hand side and write tan θ = sin θ/cos θ (1). Then sin θ tan θ + cos θ = sin²θ/cos θ + cos θ = (sin²θ + cos²θ)/cos θ, combining over a common denominator (1). Using sin²θ + cos²θ = 1 gives 1/cos θ (1). Since 1/cos θ = sec θ, the left-hand side equals the right-hand side and the identity is proved (1).
