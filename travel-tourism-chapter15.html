Transport Operations in Travel & Tourism

Tourists can only visit what they can reach. Air, rail, sea and road each play a distinct role, while tour operators and travel agents organise and sell the journey.

Key Concepts

01

Air transport offers scheduled flights, which run to a published timetable with seats sold individually, and charter flights, which tour operators hire entirely for package holidays; low-cost carriers such as Ryanair and easyJet keep fares low by charging separately for extras.

02

Full-service network carriers such as Emirates operate through hub airports where passengers connect between flights; high-speed rail such as Eurostar competes with airlines on short city-to-city routes.

03

Sea travel includes ferries on short crossings and cruising, a fast-growing sector in which lines such as MSC Cruises and Royal Caribbean sell the ship itself as the holiday destination.

04

Road transport — coaches, hire cars and self-drive — serves short distances, rural areas and touring holidays where a flexible itinerary matters more than speed.

05

Tour operators create package holidays by buying aircraft seats and hotel beds in bulk and retailing them through brochures and websites; travel agents sell products for commission and give advice to customers.

06

Travellers choose a transport mode by weighing cost, speed, distance, convenience, luggage needs and environmental impact, so each mode suits a different trip purpose.

02

Key Terms

TermExam definition
Scheduled flightA public flight that operates to a published timetable between set airports, with individual seats sold to passengers.
Charter flightA flight hired in its entirety by a tour operator to carry package-holiday passengers, not bookable directly by the public.
Low-cost carrierAn airline that keeps fares low by removing included services, charging for extras and flying one aircraft type with fast turnarounds, e.g. Ryanair.
Full-service carrierAn airline that includes checked baggage, meals and seat selection in the fare and often connects passengers through a hub airport.
Hub airportA major airport that an airline uses as a connecting point, where passengers transfer between flights to reach many destinations.
Tour operatorA company that combines transport, accommodation and other services into a package holiday and sells it to the public.
Travel agentA retailer that sells travel products to customers, gives advice and earns commission from suppliers.
Package holidayA holiday sold at one price including at least two of transport, accommodation and other tourist services, protected by package travel regulations.
03

Exam Tip

Exam tip

Transport questions are often scenario-based: match the mode to the traveller's needs — cost, speed, distance, luggage and purpose — and use the scenario's own details in every justification.

04

Practice Questions

Structured practice — write your answer, then reveal the model.

FOUNDATION

Distinguish between a scheduled flight and a charter flight, and define a package holiday.

Model answerA scheduled flight runs to a published timetable and sells individual seats to the public (1). A charter flight is hired entirely by a tour operator to carry its package-holiday passengers (1). A package holiday sells at least two of transport, accommodation and other tourist services together for one price (1). Charter flights therefore serve package tourists, while scheduled flights serve both business and leisure travellers (1).
CHALLENGE

Evaluate the effect of the growth of low-cost carriers such as Ryanair on traditional tour operators and travel agents.

Model answerLow-cost carriers sell cheap seats booked directly online, encouraging independent travel and reducing demand for traditional package holidays (1). Travel agents lose commission as customers book flights themselves (1). Tour operators have responded with dynamic packaging and by launching their own airlines or budget brands (1). Travel agents can adapt by selling flights, insurance and add-ons to independent travellers (1). Judgement: the sector has been forced to change, but agents who add expertise and service can survive alongside the low-cost model (1).
