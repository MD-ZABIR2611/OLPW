Coordinate Geometry of the Circle

From (x − a)² + (y − b)² = r² to deciding whether a line cuts, touches or misses a circle — all with coordinate geometry, and no calculus required.

Key Concepts

01

A circle with centre (a, b) and radius r has equation (x − a)² + (y − b)² = r²; the equation is provided in the List of formulas.

02

The expanded form x² + y² + 2gx + 2fy + c = 0 has centre (−g, −f) and radius √(g² + f² − c), found by completing the square in x and y; it represents a real circle only when g² + f² − c > 0.

03

To see how a line meets a circle, substitute the line equation into the circle equation to obtain a quadratic in x: two distinct roots give a chord, equal roots give a tangent, and no real roots mean the line does not meet the circle.

04

A tangent is perpendicular to the radius at the point of contact, so the tangent gradient is the negative reciprocal of the radius gradient; its equation follows from y − y₁ = m(x − x₁), and no use of calculus is expected.

05

Comparing the perpendicular distance d from the centre to the line with the radius gives the same classification: d less than r means the line cuts twice, d equal to r means it touches, and d greater than r means it misses.

06

For two circles, compare the distance between centres with the sum and difference of the radii to decide whether they intersect, touch externally or internally, or are separate; the common chord equation is found by subtracting one circle equation from the other.

02

Key Terms

TermExam definition
ChordA straight line segment joining two points on the circumference of a circle.
Tangent to a circleA straight line touching a circle at exactly one point, perpendicular to the radius at the point of contact.
Common chordThe chord joining the two intersection points of two circles; its equation is found by subtracting the two circle equations.
Centre of a circleThe fixed point equidistant from all points on a circle; the point (a, b) in the standard equation of the circle.
RadiusThe constant distance from the centre to any point on the circle, denoted r in the centre-radius form of the equation.
General form of a circleThe expanded equation x² + y² + 2gx + 2fy + c = 0 of a circle, which has centre (−g, −f) and radius √(g² + f² − c).
Length of a chordCalculated as 2√(r² − d²), where d is the perpendicular distance from the centre to the chord, or from the two intersection points of the line and circle.
Touching circlesTwo circles that touch externally when the distance between centres equals the sum of their radii, and internally when it equals the difference.

Exam Tip

Exam tip

For show-that-the-line-is-a-tangent questions, form the quadratic in x or y and state the discriminant condition explicitly — equal roots, discriminant zero, exactly one point of contact — before writing the conclusion; the mark is awarded for the comparison being seen, not just asserted.

Practice Questions

Structured practice — write your answer, then reveal the model.

FOUNDATION

Find the centre and radius of the circle x² + y² − 6x + 4y − 12 = 0.

Model answerGroup the terms: (x² − 6x) + (y² + 4y) = 12 (1). Complete the squares: (x − 3)² − 9 + (y + 2)² − 4 = 12, so (x − 3)² + (y + 2)² = 25 (1). Centre = (3, −2) (1). Radius = √25 = 5 (1).
CHALLENGE

The line y = x + c meets the circle x² + y² = 25. Determine the values of c for which the line is a tangent to the circle, explaining your reasoning.

Model answerSubstituting gives x² + (x + c)² = 25, i.e. 2x² + 2cx + c² − 25 = 0 (1). A tangent meets the circle at exactly one point, so this quadratic has equal roots and its discriminant is zero (1). Discriminant = (2c)² − 4(2)(c² − 25) = 4c² − 8c² + 200 = 200 − 4c² = 0, so c² = 50 (1). Hence c = ±5√2 (1); for these values the line touches the circle at a single point, and for other values of c it cuts the circle twice or misses it (1).
