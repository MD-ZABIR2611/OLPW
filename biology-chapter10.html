Gas Exchange, Respiration & Excretion

CORE
SECTION 01 — CHAPTER OVERVIEW

Breathing, Energy & Waste

This chapter combines three critical physiological systems: the mechanics of breathing and gas exchange in the lungs, the release of energy through respiration, and the removal of toxic waste by the kidneys.

SECTION 02 — LEARNING OBJECTIVES

Chapter Goals

  • Describe the mechanism of ventilation (inspiration and expiration).
  • Explain the adaptations of alveoli for gas exchange.
  • Write word equations for aerobic and anaerobic respiration.
  • Describe the role of the kidneys in excretion.
SECTION 03 — CORE THEORY

Respiration & Excretion

Gas Exchange: Breathing (ventilation) moves air in and out of the lungs. During inspiration, the diaphragm contracts and flattens, intercostal muscles pull ribs up and out, decreasing pressure so air rushes in. Expiration is the reverse.

Gas exchange occurs in the alveoli. They have a large surface area, thin walls (one cell thick), and a dense capillary network to maintain a steep concentration gradient for diffusion of O2 into blood and CO2 out.

Respiration Equations

Aerobic: glucose + oxygen → carbon dioxide + water

Anaerobic (Animals): glucose → lactic acid

Anaerobic (Yeast): glucose → ethanol + carbon dioxide

Excretion

Excretion is the removal of toxic metabolic waste. The lungs excrete CO2. The kidneys filter the blood to excrete urea (a waste product from the breakdown of excess amino acids in the liver). The kidneys also reabsorb glucose, water, and salts to maintain homeostasis.

SECTION 04 — KEY DEFINITIONS

Examination Definitions

RESPIRATION: The chemical reaction in cells that breaks down glucose to release energy.

EXCRETION: The removal of toxic materials and waste products of metabolism from an organism.

GAS EXCHANGE: The diffusion of oxygen into the blood and carbon dioxide out of the blood at the alveoli.

SECTION 06 — PRACTICAL LAB

Effect of Exercise on Breathing Rate

AIM

To investigate how exercise affects the rate of breathing.

METHOD

1. Count breaths per minute at rest.

2. Exercise (e.g., step-ups) for 3 minutes.

3. Immediately count breaths per minute. Continue measuring every minute until return to rest.

CONCLUSION

Breathing rate increases because muscles respire more to release energy. This uses up oxygen and produces CO2. The brain detects CO2 increase and triggers faster, deeper breathing to remove CO2 and supply O2.

SECTION 07 — EXAM TECHNIQUE

Respiration vs Breathing

Never confuse respiration with breathing. Breathing is a mechanical process (ventilation). Respiration is a chemical reaction in cells. If an exam asks about respiration, do not mention "inhaling".

SECTION 08 — COMMON MISTAKES

Avoid These Errors

Mistake: Writing "energy is produced" in respiration.

Correction: Energy is released, not created. (Conservation of energy).

Mistake: Saying excretion is "removing feces". Feces is undigested food, so it is egestion, not excretion. Excretion removes metabolic waste (like urea).

SECTION 09 — QUICK REVISION

Fast Facts

  • Inspiration: Diaphragm flattens, ribs move up/out.
  • Alveoli: Large surface area, thin walls, dense capillaries.
  • Anaerobic respiration in humans produces lactic acid (causes fatigue/oxygen debt).
  • Kidneys filter blood and excrete urea.
SECTION 10 — EXAM QUESTIONS

Test Your Knowledge

Question 1 (Challenge)

Explain why an athlete accumulates an "oxygen debt" during a 100m sprint.

3 marks: During a sprint, the muscles need energy very quickly. The heart and lungs cannot deliver oxygen fast enough for aerobic respiration, so the cells switch to anaerobic respiration. This produces lactic acid. The "oxygen debt" is the amount of oxygen needed to break down the lactic acid after the race is over.
