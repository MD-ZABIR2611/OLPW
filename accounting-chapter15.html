Manufacturing Accounts

Separating factory costs from office costs to find the cost of manufacturing the goods a firm sells.

05Key concepts
07Key terms
02Practice questions
—Your status
01

Key Concepts

01

A manufacturing account is prepared BY a manufacturing business IN ADDITION to the trading account, to show the cost of making the goods it later sells.

02

Prime cost = direct materials + direct labour + direct expenses (costs that can be traced to the product).

03

Factory overheads (indirect factory costs) - factory rent, factory power, depreciation of factory machinery - are added to prime cost.

04

Work in progress: opening WIP is added and closing WIP is deducted to reach the production cost of goods completed.

05

The transfer price of goods made is usually cost of production plus a mark-up, and is transferred to the trading account.

02

Key Terms

TermExam definition
Prime costThe total of direct materials, direct labour and direct expenses - the costs directly traceable to production.
Direct materialsRaw materials that become part of the finished product, shown as raw materials consumed.
Factory overheadsIndirect costs of the factory such as factory rent, factory power and depreciation of factory machinery.
Work in progressPartly finished goods: opening work in progress is added and closing work in progress deducted in the manufacturing account.
Cost of productionPrime cost plus factory overheads, adjusted for work in progress - the cost of the goods completed in the period.
Transfer priceThe price at which finished goods are transferred from the factory to the trading account, often cost plus a mark-up.
03

Exam Tip

Exam tip

Lay the manufacturing account out in order: raw materials consumed, direct labour, direct expenses, PRIME COST, then factory overheads, then adjust for work in progress to get the production cost of goods completed. Examiners award layout marks - label every subtotal.

04

Practice Questions

Structured practice — write your answer, then reveal the model.

FOUNDATION

A manufacturer’s records show: raw materials purchased $48,000, raw materials inventory fell from $5,000 to $3,000, direct wages $22,000, factory rent $9,000. Calculate the raw materials consumed and the prime cost.

Model answerRaw materials consumed = opening $5,000 + purchases $48,000 − closing $3,000 = $50,000 (1). Prime cost = $50,000 + direct wages $22,000 = $72,000 (1). Factory rent is an overhead, not part of prime cost (1).
CHALLENGE

State the effect on the production cost of goods completed of: (a) closing work in progress increasing, (b) reclassifying an office salary as a factory salary.

Model answer(a) Closing work in progress is deducted, so production cost of goods completed DECREASES (1). (b) The amount moves from office (administrative) expenses into factory overheads, so the production cost INCREASES and gross profit is unaffected overall (1).
