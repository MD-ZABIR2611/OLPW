Chapter Overview

What is it?

This chapter explores the energy changes that occur during chemical reactions. It defines exothermic and endothermic reactions, explains how breaking and forming bonds affects energy, and details how to calculate these changes using bond energies and calorimetry.

Why It Matters

Understanding energetics is crucial for controlling reactions. It allows industrial chemists to maintain safe temperatures in factories, engineers to design efficient fuels, and biologists to understand cellular respiration and photosynthesis.

What You Will Learn
  • Exothermic vs endothermic reactions
  • Activation energy & profile diagrams
  • Bond breaking vs bond making
  • Calculating enthalpy change ($\Delta H$)
  • Calorimetry experimental method

03

Detailed Theory

01

Exothermic & Endothermic Reactions

Exothermic: Transfers heat to the surroundings. Temperature increases. (e.g., Combustion, Neutralisation). Energy released making bonds > energy required to break bonds.

Endothermic: Takes in heat from the surroundings. Temperature decreases. (e.g., Thermal decomposition, Photosynthesis). Energy required to break bonds > energy released making bonds.

02

Activation Energy

Definition: The minimum amount of energy that reactant particles must possess in order to collide and react successfully.

Why it happens: Even exothermic reactions need a "spark" to start. Existing bonds must be broken before new ones can form. Breaking bonds always requires an initial input of energy.

03

Bond Breaking & Bond Making

  • Bond Breaking: Always ENDOTHERMIC. Energy must be supplied to break the attraction between atoms.
  • Bond Making: Always EXOTHERMIC. Energy is released when atoms form a stable bond.

06

Equations & Formulae

Overall Energy Change
$\Delta H$ = $\sum E_{reactants}$ - $\sum E_{products}$

Energy required to break bonds minus Energy released when making bonds.

Calorimetry Heat Energy
$q$ = m $\times$ c $\times$ $\Delta T$

Where q = heat energy (J), m = mass of water (g), c = 4.2 J/g°C, $\Delta T$ = temp change (°C).

07

Worked Examples

Case Study 03 / Advanced

Bond Energy Calculation

Question: Calculate $\Delta H$ for $H_2 + Cl_2 \rightarrow 2HCl$.
Bond energies (kJ/mol): H-H = 436, Cl-Cl = 243, H-Cl = 432.

1. Energy IN (breaking bonds): 1(H-H) + 1(Cl-Cl) = $436 + 243 = 679$ kJ

2. Energy OUT (making bonds): 2(H-Cl) = $2 \times 432 = 864$ kJ

3. $\Delta H$ = Energy IN - Energy OUT

4. $\Delta H = 679 - 864 = -185$ kJ

Answer = -185 kJ/mol (Exothermic)

Laboratory Protocol

Experiment 01 / Calorimetry

Measuring Energy Change of Combustion

Objective: To determine the energy released when a fuel (e.g., ethanol) burns.

Sources of Error: Heat loss to surroundings (air); Heat absorbed by the can itself; Incomplete combustion of fuel.

Exam Application: Examiners frequently ask why the experimental result is lower than the theoretical value. Answer: Heat loss to the surroundings prevents all energy from heating the water.

11

Error Database

Wrong

Bond breaking releases energy.

Correct

Bond breaking is ALWAYS endothermic (requires energy). Bond making is ALWAYS exothermic (releases energy).

Wrong

A catalyst lowers the overall enthalpy change ($\Delta H$) of a reaction.

Correct

A catalyst lowers the activation energy by providing an alternative pathway. The overall $\Delta H$ remains exactly the same.

13

Exam Simulation

Intermediate

2. In terms of bond breaking and bond making, explain why a reaction is exothermic.

Mark Scheme Answer [2 Marks]

In an exothermic reaction, the energy released when making new bonds in the products is greater than the energy required to break the bonds in the reactants.

Advanced

3. Calculate the energy change ($\Delta H$) for the reaction: $N_2 + 3H_2 \rightarrow 2NH_3$.
Bond energies (kJ/mol): N$\equiv$N = 945, H-H = 436, N-H = 391.

Mark Scheme Answer [4 Marks]

1. Energy IN (breaking): 1(N$\equiv$N) + 3(H-H) = $945 + (3 \times 436) = 945 + 1308 = 2253$ kJ

2. Energy OUT (making): 6(N-H) = $6 \times 391 = 2346$ kJ

3. $\Delta H$ = Energy IN - Energy OUT = $2253 - 2346 = -93$ kJ

4. Answer: -93 kJ/mol (exothermic).
