Chapter Overview

What is it?

Organic chemistry is the chemistry of carbon compounds. Carbon atoms form four covalent bonds and can join into long chains and rings, which is why there are millions of organic compounds.

Why It Matters

Fuels, plastics, medicines, solvents, food and every living thing are built from organic molecules. Crude oil alone supplies most of the world's fuels and the raw materials for plastics.

What You Will Learn
  • Fractional distillation of crude oil
  • Alkanes, alkenes and cracking
  • Isomers and naming compounds
  • Alcohols: ethanol production and uses

03

Detailed Theory

01

Crude Oil and Fractional Distillation

Crude oil is a mixture of hydrocarbons (compounds of hydrogen and carbon only) formed from the remains of sea organisms over millions of years. It is separated by fractional distillation.

The oil is heated until it vaporises and enters a fractionating column that is hot at the bottom and cooler at the top. Vapours rise and condense at the level where the temperature falls below their boiling point. Small molecules have weak intermolecular forces, low boiling points and leave near the top; large molecules condense near the bottom.

Trends going down the column
  • Chain length and boiling point increase
  • Viscosity (thickness) increases
  • The fraction is harder to ignite and burns with a smokier flame
02

Homologous Series

A homologous series is a family of compounds with the same general formula and functional group, similar chemical properties, and a gradual trend in physical properties. Each member differs from the next by CH2.

SeriesGeneral formulaFunctional groupFirst member
AlkanesCnH2n+2C–C single bonds onlyMethane, CH4
AlkenesCnH2nC=C double bondEthene, C2H4
AlcoholsCnH2n+1OH–OH (hydroxyl)Methanol, CH3OH
Carboxylic acidsCnH2n+1COOH–COOH (carboxyl)Methanoic acid, HCOOH

Names use a stem for the number of carbon atoms: meth- (1), eth- (2), prop- (3), but- (4), pent- (5).

03

Alkanes

Alkanes are saturated: every carbon has four single bonds. They are fairly unreactive, apart from burning and reacting with chlorine in UV light.

  • Complete combustion (plenty of oxygen) gives carbon dioxide and water.
  • Incomplete combustion (limited oxygen) gives carbon monoxide, a toxic gas that binds to haemoglobin, and soot (carbon).
  • Substitution with chlorine in ultraviolet light: a hydrogen atom is replaced by a chlorine atom, e.g. methane gives chloromethane and hydrogen chloride.
04

Alkenes and Cracking

Alkenes are unsaturated: they contain a C=C double bond, which makes them much more reactive than alkanes. They undergo addition reactions, where the double bond opens and atoms add across it.

  • Bromine water: orange bromine water turns colourless with an alkene (test for unsaturation).
  • Hydrogen (nickel catalyst, 150 °C): gives an alkane. Used to harden vegetable oils into margarine.
  • Steam (phosphoric acid catalyst, 300 °C, 60 atm): gives an alcohol.
Cracking

Long-chain alkanes are in low demand, while petrol and alkenes are in high demand. Cracking breaks large alkanes into smaller alkanes and alkenes using heat (about 600–700 °C) and a catalyst such as aluminium oxide or silica. Example: C10H22 → C8H18 + C2H4.

05

Structural Isomers

Structural isomers have the same molecular formula but different structural formulae. Butane (a straight chain) and methylpropane (a branched chain) are both C4H10. Branched isomers usually have lower boiling points because their molecules pack less closely, so intermolecular forces are weaker.

06

Alcohols and Ethanol

Alcohols contain the –OH group. Ethanol (C2H5OH) is made in two ways:

FermentationHydration of ethene
Raw materialSugar (glucose) from crops, renewableEthene from crude oil, non-renewable
ConditionsYeast, 25–35 °C, no oxygen300 °C, 60 atm, phosphoric acid catalyst
RateSlow, batch processFast, continuous process
PurityAbout 15%; needs fractional distillationAlmost pure ethanol

Uses of ethanol: solvent, fuel (often blended with petrol), and in alcoholic drinks. Ethanol burns cleanly: C2H5OH + 3O2 → 2CO2 + 3H2O.

07

Carboxylic Acids and Esters

Carboxylic acids contain –COOH. They are weak acids: they only partly ionise in water, so they have a pH of about 3–4. They show typical acid reactions: with metals (salt + hydrogen), bases (salt + water) and carbonates (salt + water + carbon dioxide).

Ethanoic acid (vinegar) forms when ethanol is oxidised, either by bacteria in air or by heating with acidified potassium manganate(VII).

A carboxylic acid reacts with an alcohol, with a concentrated sulfuric acid catalyst, to form an ester and water. Esters have fruity smells and are used in perfumes and flavourings. Ethanol + ethanoic acid gives ethyl ethanoate.

08

Polymers

Polymers are long-chain molecules made by joining many small molecules (monomers).

  • Addition polymerisation: alkene monomers join by opening their C=C bonds; the polymer is the only product. Ethene gives poly(ethene), used for bags and bottles.
  • Condensation polymerisation: two different monomers, each with two functional groups, join and a small molecule (usually water) is lost at each link. Nylon (a polyamide) and PET (a polyester) are examples. Proteins are natural polyamides, made from amino acids.

Most plastics are non-biodegradable: they fill landfill sites and harm marine life, and burning them releases toxic gases. Recycling and reducing single-use plastics help.

Worked Examples

Example 01 / Basic

Using the General Formula

Question: Give the molecular formula of the alkane with 6 carbon atoms and the alkene with 5 carbon atoms.

1. Alkanes: CnH2n+2. For n = 6: H = 2(6) + 2 = 14.

2. Alkenes: CnH2n. For n = 5: H = 10.

Answer: Hexane C6H14; pentene C5H10.

Example 02 / Intermediate

Balancing a Cracking Equation

Question: Dodecane, C12H26, is cracked to give octane and one other product. Write the equation.

1. Octane is C8H18.

2. Left over: C = 12 − 8 = 4, H = 26 − 18 = 8, so C4H8 (butene, an alkene).

Answer: C12H26 → C8H18 + C4H8

Example 03 / Advanced

Drawing a Repeat Unit

Question: Propene is CH2=CH–CH3. Describe the repeat unit of poly(propene).

1. Open the double bond: the two carbons that were double-bonded become the backbone.

2. Keep the CH3 group as a side branch on the second carbon.

Answer: –[CH2–CH(CH3)]n– with bonds extending from both ends of the bracket.

08

Laboratory Protocol

Experiment 01 / Bromine Water Test

Alkane or Alkene?

Method: Add a few drops of orange bromine water to each hydrocarbon in a test tube and shake.

Result: Alkene: decolourises (orange to colourless). Alkane: no change, stays orange.

Experiment 02 / Fermentation

Making Ethanol from Sugar

Method: Dissolve glucose in warm water, add yeast, and fit an airlock or delivery tube into limewater. Keep at about 30 °C for several days.

Exam points: Limewater turns milky, showing CO2 is made. Above about 40 °C the yeast enzymes denature. Air is kept out so the yeast respires anaerobically and ethanol is not oxidised to ethanoic acid.

09

Error Database

Wrong

Bromine water turns clear with alkanes.

Correct

Alkanes are saturated, so bromine water stays orange. Only unsaturated compounds such as alkenes decolourise it.

Wrong

Cracking makes only alkenes.

Correct

Cracking gives a shorter alkane and at least one alkene. Balance carbon and hydrogen to find both.

Wrong

Carboxylic acids are strong acids.

Correct

They are weak acids: they only partially ionise in water, so their pH is around 3–4.

Wrong

Addition polymerisation gives off water.

Correct

In addition polymerisation the polymer is the only product. Water is lost in condensation polymerisation.

Advanced

6. Butane and methylpropane are isomers. Explain what this means and suggest which has the lower boiling point.

Mark Scheme Answer [3 Marks]

Same molecular formula (C4H10) [1] but different structural formulae [1]. Methylpropane: it is branched, so the molecules pack less closely and have weaker intermolecular forces [1].

Advanced

7. Explain why carbon monoxide is formed when fuels burn in a poor supply of air, and why it is dangerous.

Mark Scheme Answer [3 Marks]

Incomplete combustion due to insufficient oxygen [1]. CO combines with haemoglobin in red blood cells [1], reducing the blood's ability to carry oxygen, which can be fatal [1].

Advanced

8. Compare addition and condensation polymerisation.

Mark Scheme Answer [4 Marks]

Addition: monomers contain C=C [1]; the polymer is the only product [1]. Condensation: monomers have two functional groups, often two different monomers [1]; a small molecule such as water is also formed [1].
