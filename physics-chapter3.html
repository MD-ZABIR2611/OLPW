Mass, Weight & Gravity

CORE EXTENDED
THEORY

Gravitational Fields

A gravitational field is a region of space where a mass experiences a force. All masses attract each other; the Earth exerts a gravitational pull on all objects, causing them to fall toward the ground when dropped. The strength of this field, denoted as 'g', is approximately 9.8 N/kg on Earth's surface.

Mass is a measure of the amount of matter in an object; it does not change with location. Weight is a vector quantity representing the gravitational force acting on that mass; it changes depending on the local gravitational field strength.

DEFINITIONS

Key Terms

Mass: A measure of the quantity of matter in an object, measured in kilograms (kg).

Weight: The gravitational force acting on an object, measured in newtons (N).

Gravitational Field Strength (g): The force per unit mass acting on an object in a gravitational field (N/kg).

FORMULAE & CALCULATIONS

Calculating Weight

Weight (W) = mass (m) × gravitational field strength (g)
PRACTICAL WORK

Measuring Mass vs Weight

Mass is measured using an electronic balance, which compares the object's matter to a standard. Weight is measured using a spring balance (newtonmeter), which measures the extension of a spring caused by the gravitational pull on the object.

EXAM PRACTICE

Worked Example

Question 1 (Extended)

An astronaut has a mass of 80 kg. The gravitational field strength on the Moon is 1.6 N/kg. Calculate the astronaut's weight on the Moon.

Formula: W = mg

Substitution: W = 80 kg × 1.6 N/kg

Answer: W = 128 N
