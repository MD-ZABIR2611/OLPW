Pressure

CORE EXTENDED
THEORY

Force per Unit Area

Pressure is defined as the force acting perpendicularly per unit area. A sharp knife cuts better than a blunt one because the same force is applied over a much smaller area, resulting in higher pressure.

In fluids (liquids and gases), pressure increases with depth. This is because the deeper you go, the more fluid weight is pushing down on you. Atmospheric pressure decreases with altitude.

DEFINITIONS

Key Terms

Pressure: Force applied perpendicular to a surface per unit area, measured in Pascals (Pa).

FORMULAE & CALCULATIONS

Pressure Equations

pressure (p) = force (F) / area (A)
liquid pressure (p) = density (ρ) × gravity (g) × depth (h)
VISUALS / FLUID PRESSURE

Depth and Pressure

As depth increases, the weight of the fluid above increases, exerting greater force on the same area.

EXAM PRACTICE

Worked Example

Question 1 (Core)

A box weighs 400 N. Its base has an area of 0.5 m². Calculate the pressure exerted on the floor.

Formula: p = F / A

Substitution: p = 400 N / 0.5 m²

Answer: p = 800 Pa
