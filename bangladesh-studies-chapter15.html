Constitution, Government & Culture

The 1972 Constitution, how Bangladesh is governed, and the culture - language, festivals and literature - that shapes national identity.

Key Concepts

01

The Constitution of 1972 established Bangladesh as a unitary parliamentary republic built on four principles: nationalism, socialism, democracy and secularism.

02

Government has three branches: the executive (Prime Minister and Cabinet), the legislature (the Jatiya Sangsad, Parliament) and the judiciary (courts headed by the Supreme Court).

03

The President is the head of state; the Prime Minister holds executive power and leads the largest party in Parliament (300 elected seats plus 50 reserved for women).

04

Language and culture are central to national identity: the martyrs of 21 February 1952 are honoured worldwide as International Mother Language Day.

05

Culture includes the festivals (Pohela Boishakh, Ekushey February), the literature of Rabindranath Tagore and Kazi Nazrul Islam, folk traditions and dress.

02

Key Terms

TermExam definition
ConstitutionThe supreme written law of Bangladesh (adopted 1972) setting out the structure of government and citizens’ fundamental rights.
Jatiya SangsadThe national Parliament of Bangladesh - the legislature that makes laws and is elected for five-year terms.
SecularismA founding principle: the state does not privilege any religion and citizens may practise their faith freely.
Ekushey February21 February, the day language martyrs were killed in 1952, now UNESCO’s International Mother Language Day.
Pohela BoishakhThe Bengali New Year festival (14 April), celebrated with processions, traditional dress and fair food.
MicrocreditSmall loans to the poor without collateral, pioneered in Bangladesh by the Grameen Bank to support self-employment.
03

Exam Tip

Exam tip

Government questions want the SEPARATION OF POWERS: name the branch, who leads it and one function. Culture questions reward SPECIFIC examples - name a festival, an author, a place - rather than general statements.

Practice Questions

Structured practice — write your answer, then reveal the model.

FOUNDATION

Name the three branches of government in Bangladesh and state the role of each.

Model answerExecutive - the Prime Minister and Cabinet run the country day to day (1); Legislature - the Jatiya Sangsad makes laws and scrutinises the government (1); Judiciary - the courts, headed by the Supreme Court, interpret the laws and uphold the Constitution (1).
CHALLENGE

Explain why the Bengali Language Movement of 1952 remains so important in Bangladesh’s culture today.

Model answerStudents were killed on 21 February 1952 defending Bangla as a state language when Pakistan tried to impose Urdu (1). The sacrifice became a symbol of national identity, led directly to the independence movement, and is commemorated every year (1); UNESCO made it International Mother Language Day, giving it global significance (1).
