Chapter Overview

What is it?

This chapter explores the arrangement of elements in the Periodic Table based on atomic number and electronic configuration. It details the specific properties, trends, and chemical behaviors of Group I, II, VII, VIII, and Transition Metals.

Why It Matters

The Periodic Table is the chemist's roadmap. By knowing an element's position, you can predict its physical and chemical properties without ever seeing it. This predictive power is a cornerstone of modern chemistry and material science.

03

Detailed Theory

01

Development & Modern Periodic Table

Development: Dmitri Mendeleev arranged elements in order of relative atomic mass but noticed a repeating (periodic) pattern in chemical properties. He left gaps for undiscovered elements and swapped elements where their properties didn't fit the mass order.

Modern Periodic Table: Elements are arranged in order of increasing atomic number (number of protons).

  • Periods: Horizontal rows. The period number equals the number of electron shells an atom has.
  • Groups: Vertical columns. The group number equals the number of electrons in the outer shell (for Groups I-VIII / 1-8).
02

Group I — Alkali Metals

Group I metals (Lithium, Sodium, Potassium) are highly reactive soft metals with one outer shell electron.

Physical Properties: Soft (can be cut with a knife). Low density (Li, Na, K float on water). Low melting points compared to other metals.

Reactivity Trend: Reactivity increases down the group. As you go down, the atoms get larger. The outer electron is further from the positive nucleus, meaning the attraction is weaker. It is easier to lose the electron, making the metal more reactive.

Reaction of Sodium with Water

2Na(s) + 2H2O(l) → 2NaOH(aq) + H2(g)

03

Group VII — Halogens

Group VII non-metals (Fluorine, Chlorine, Bromine, Iodine) have seven outer shell electrons. They exist as diatomic molecules (e.g., Cl₂).

Physical Properties: As you go down the group, melting/boiling points increase, and the state changes from gas to solid.

  • Fluorine & Chlorine: Pale Yellow / Pale Green Gases
  • Bromine: Red-Brown Liquid (evaporates easily)
  • Iodine: Dark Grey Solid (sublimes to purple vapor)

Reactivity Trend: Reactivity decreases down Group VII. Halogens gain electrons to achieve a full outer shell. As you go down, the atom gets larger. The incoming electron is further from the nucleus, so the attraction is weaker. It is harder to gain the electron, making the halogen less reactive.

Halogen Displacement: A more reactive halogen will displace a less reactive halogen from an aqueous solution of its salt. (e.g., Chlorine displaces Bromine from Potassium Bromide).

04

Transition Metals

Found in the center of the Periodic Table. They have unique properties compared to Group I/II metals.

  • Physical: Hard, dense, high melting points.
  • Variable Oxidation States: They can form ions with different charges (e.g., Iron can be Fe²⁺ or Fe³⁺; Copper can be Cu⁺ or Cu²⁺).
  • Coloured Compounds: They form brightly colored compounds (e.g., Copper(II) sulfate is blue, Iron(II) is pale green).
  • Catalysts: Many transition metals or their compounds act as catalysts (e.g., Iron in the Haber process, Nickel in hydrogenation).

Key Definitions

TermExam Definition
PeriodA horizontal row of the periodic table; the number indicates the shells an atom possesses.
GroupA vertical column of elements with similar chemical properties; indicates outer shell electrons.
Alkali MetalsElements in Group I, known for high reactivity and reacting with water to form alkaline solutions.
HalogensElements in Group VII, known as salt-formers; toxic non-metals.
Noble GasesElements in Group 0/VIII; unreactive due to full outer electron shells.
Transition MetalA metal from the center of the periodic table characterized by variable oxidation states and colored compounds.

Case Study 02 / Intermediate

Halogen Displacement

Question: Chlorine gas is added to potassium iodide solution. Write the equation and state the observation.

1. Cl is higher in Group VII than I, so it is more reactive and will displace I.

2. Equation: Cl2 + 2KI $\rightarrow$ 2KCl + I2

3. Iodine (I2) turns the solution brown/black.

Answer: Solution turns brown/black due to displaced iodine.

11

Error Database

Wrong

Reactivity increases down Group VII because the atoms get bigger.

Correct

Reactivity decreases down Group VII. Halogens need to gain an electron. A bigger atom means the nucleus is further from the outer shell, making it harder to attract an incoming electron.

Wrong

Transition metals only form +2 ions.

Correct

Transition metals have variable oxidation states. Iron can be Fe²⁺ or Fe³⁺, Copper can be Cu⁺ or Cu²⁺. This is a defining feature of transition metals.

Exam Simulation

Intermediate

2. Explain why Potassium is more reactive than Sodium.

Mark Scheme Answer [3 Marks]

Potassium is lower in Group I, so it has more electron shells. This makes the atom larger. The outer electron is further from the positive nucleus, so the attraction is weaker. It is easier to lose the electron, making it more reactive.

Advanced

3. Chlorine is added to a solution of Potassium Iodide. Write the ionic equation for this reaction and state the resulting color change.

Mark Scheme Answer [3 Marks]

1. Chlorine is more reactive than Iodine, so it displaces it.

2. Ionic equation: Cl2(aq) + 2I⁻(aq) $\rightarrow$ 2Cl⁻(aq) + I2(aq)

3. Color change: The solution turns from colorless to brown/black (due to displaced iodine).
