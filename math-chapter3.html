Algebra & Algebraic Manipulation

SECTION 01 — CHAPTER OVERVIEW

The Language of Mathematics

Algebra allows us to represent unknown values and solve real-world problems using symbols. This chapter covers the foundational skills of algebraic manipulation, including expanding brackets, factorising expressions, and rearranging formulas.

SECTION 02 — LEARNING OBJECTIVES

Chapter Goals

  • Simplify expressions by collecting like terms.
  • Expand single and double brackets.
  • Factorise linear and quadratic expressions.
  • Change the subject of a formula.
  • Apply the laws of indices to algebraic terms.
SECTION 03 — CORE THEORY

Expressions & Formulas

An expression is a collection of terms (e.g., $3x + 5$). A formula shows the relationship between variables (e.g., $A = \pi r^2$).

Key Techniques

Expanding Double Brackets: Multiply each term in the first bracket by each term in the second. Use FOIL (First, Outer, Inner, Last) for binomials.

Factorising: The reverse of expanding. Pull out the Highest Common Factor (HCF).

Difference of Two Squares: $a^2 - b^2 = (a+b)(a-b)$

SECTION 04 — KEY DEFINITIONS

Examination Definitions

VARIABLE: A symbol, usually a letter, that represents an unknown number.

COEFFICIENT: The number multiplied by the variable (e.g., in $5x$, 5 is the coefficient).

LIKE TERMS: Terms that have the exact same variables and powers (e.g., $3x$ and $7x$). They can be added or subtracted.

SECTION 06 — CALCULATIONS & WORKED EXAMPLES

Changing the Subject

Make $x$ the subject of the formula: $y = 3x + 5b$

STEP WORKING NOTES
1. Isolate $x$ term $y - 5b = 3x$ Subtract $5b$ from both sides
2. Divide by 3 $\frac{y - 5b}{3} = x$ Divide every term by 3
3. Final Answer $x = \frac{y - 5b}{3}$ $x$ is now the subject
SECTION 07 — EXAM TECHNIQUE

Showing Algebra Steps

Always write out each line of working. Examiners award method marks even if the final answer is wrong. Do not try to do multiple steps (like expanding and simplifying) in your head simultaneously.

SECTION 08 — COMMON MISTAKES

Avoid These Errors

Mistake: "Squaring" a bracket: $(x+y)^2 = x^2 + y^2$. This is wrong.

Correction: $(x+y)^2 = (x+y)(x+y) = x^2 + 2xy + y^2$. You must multiply the terms, not just square the individual parts.

SECTION 09 — QUICK REVISION

Fast Facts

  • Only add/subtract LIKE terms.
  • FOIL: First, Outer, Inner, Last.
  • Difference of two squares: $a^2 - b^2 = (a-b)(a+b)$.
  • When changing the subject, do the same thing to both sides.
SECTION 10 — EXAM QUESTIONS

Test Your Knowledge

Question 1 (Standard)

Factorise completely: $4x^2 - 9y^2$.

2 marks:

This is a difference of two squares.

$a = 2x$, $b = 3y$.

Answer: $(2x - 3y)(2x + 3y)$
