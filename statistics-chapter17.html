Chapter 17 · Statistics

Crude and Standardised Rates

Crude rates divide by total population, so an older town can look sicker than a younger one. Direct standardisation re-expresses both towns against one standard population to allow a fair comparison.

Key Concepts

01

A crude rate per thousand is (number of events/total population)×1000, used for birth rates, death rates and fertility rates.

02

An age-specific rate restricts both events and population to one age band, such as deaths of people aged 60-69 divided by the population of that band.

03

Crude rates mislead when the populations have different age structures, because older populations naturally have more deaths per thousand people.

04

Direct standardisation applies each population age-specific rate to a standard population and divides the total expected events by the total standard population, then ×1000.

05

Standardised rates can rank two populations differently from crude rates; the reversal must be explained by differences in age structure.

06

Other exam rates include fertility rate per thousand women of childbearing age and accident rates per thousand or per million workers.

02

Key Terms

TermExam definition
Crude death rateThe number of deaths in a population in a year divided by the total population, usually expressed per thousand people.
Crude birth rateThe number of live births in a year divided by the total population, usually expressed per thousand people.
Standardised rateA rate computed by applying a population age-specific rates to one standard population, allowing fair comparison between populations.
Standard populationA single reference population with fixed age-group sizes used as the basis for direct standardisation.
Age-specific rateA rate calculated within one age group, dividing events in that group by the population of the same group.
Fertility rateThe number of live births per thousand women of childbearing age in a given year.
Accident rateThe number of accidents divided by the number of workers or hours worked, often expressed per thousand or per million.
Direct standardisationThe method of multiplying each standard-population age-group size by the corresponding age-specific rate and dividing the total expected events by the total standard population.

Exam Tip

Exam tip

Lay standardisation out in a table with columns for the standard population, each age-specific rate and the expected number of events; the (1) marks follow the columns.

04

Practice Questions

Structured practice — write your answer, then reveal the model.

FOUNDATION

A town of 50 000 people records 400 deaths in one year. Calculate the crude death rate per 1000 of the population, and state two purposes for which a dam might be built.

Model answerCrude death rate = (400/50 000)×1000 = 400×1000/50 000 = 8 per 1000 (1). A dam provides water storage for drinking and irrigation supplies (1) and hydroelectric power generation / flood control (1). The unit per 1000 must be stated for full credit (1).
CHALLENGE

Town A has 5000 young people with death rate 4 per 1000 and 5000 old people with death rate 16 per 1000. Town B has 8000 young at 5 per 1000 and 2000 old at 20 per 1000. Using the standard population of 6000 young and 4000 old, show that standardisation reverses the ranking given by the crude rates.

Model answerCrude A = (5000×4 + 5000×16)/10 000 = (20 + 80)/10 = 10 per 1000 (1). Crude B = (8000×5 + 2000×20)/10 000 = (40 + 40)/10 = 8 per 1000, so B looks healthier (1). Standardised A = (6000×4 + 4000×16)/10 000 = (24 + 64)/10 = 8.8 per 1000 (1). Standardised B = (6000×5 + 4000×20)/10 000 = (30 + 80)/10 = 11 per 1000 (1). The ranking reverses because Town B is older: once age structure is removed, Town A actually has the lower underlying mortality (1).
