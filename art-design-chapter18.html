Experimental and Mixed-Media Approaches

Mixed media reward risk-taking: combining collage, assemblage, transfers and unconventional materials can generate textures and meanings that pure painting or drawing cannot reach.

Key Concepts

01

Collage assembles cut or torn papers, photographs, fabric and found printed matter onto a surface; the juxtaposition of unrelated images creates new, often surreal, meanings.

02

Photomontage is collage built mainly from photographs, associated with Dada artists such as Hannah Höch, who used it to satirise politics and mass media between the wars.

03

Assemblage builds three-dimensional constructions from found objects, shifting art from representing things to re-presenting them: Picasso's Bull's Head turns a bicycle saddle and handlebars into a sculpture of a bull.

04

Image transfer moves a printed or photocopied image onto another surface using solvents, heat or acrylic mediums, allowing photographs to be embedded into paint layers and then reworked by hand.

05

Monoprinting, frottage, wax resist and ink bleeds are quick experimental processes that generate unplanned marks; selecting, cutting and combining these accidents is itself a developmental skill.

06

Experimental work still needs control: successful mixed-media pieces are unified by a consistent palette, a deliberate composition and a clear relationship between concept and materials.

02

Key Terms

TermExam definition
CollageAn artwork made by sticking materials such as paper, photographs and fabric onto a supporting surface.
PhotomontageA collage constructed mainly from photographs or photographic reproductions to create a new composite image.
AssemblageA three-dimensional artwork constructed from found objects or pre-formed elements combined into a single composition.
Found objectA natural or manufactured object not made by the artist, selected and presented for its aesthetic or conceptual qualities.
Image transferA technique that lifts an image from printed or photocopied paper onto another surface using heat, solvent or acrylic medium.
FrottageA texture technique made by rubbing pencil or crayon over paper placed on a rough surface, pioneered by Max Ernst.
Mixed mediaArtwork that deliberately combines two or more materials or techniques, such as paint, collage and print, within one piece.
MonoprintA unique print made by drawing or painting on a smooth plate and transferring that single impression onto paper.

Exam Tip

Exam tip

In extended projects, date and photograph every experiment and write a one-line note on what it contributes; assessors look for evidence that experiments informed the final outcome, not just that they were done.

04

Practice Questions

Structured practice — write your answer, then reveal the model.

FOUNDATION

Define collage and photomontage, and state one difference between them.

Model answerCollage is an artwork made by sticking materials such as paper, fabric and photographs onto a surface (1). Photomontage is a collage built mainly from photographs to create a new composite image (1). The difference: photomontage uses photographic images only or mainly, while collage may combine any materials (1). Accept: photomontage is often used for satirical or political effect, as with the Dada artists (1).
CHALLENGE

Discuss two reasons an artist might choose mixed media or assemblage instead of painting, referring to meaning as well as appearance.

Model answerMeaning: found objects carry their own histories — Picasso's Bull's Head turns a bicycle saddle and handlebars into a bull, so the materials themselves carry the subject's identity (1). Appearance: combining materials creates textures and surfaces that paint alone cannot produce, such as torn paper edges, wax or transferred photographs (1). Juxtaposition lets artists generate new, often surreal meanings by placing unrelated images side by side, which pure painting achieves only indirectly (1). Mixed media also express process and experimentation, signalling risk-taking and conceptual thinking that a conventional painting might not (1).
