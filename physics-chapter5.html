Forces & Their Effects

CORE EXTENDED
THEORY

Dynamics & Newton's Laws

A force is a push or a pull. Forces can change the shape of an object, change its speed, or change its direction of motion. When forces are balanced, the object remains stationary or moves at constant velocity (Newton's First Law). When unbalanced, the object accelerates (Newton's Second Law).

Friction is a contact force that opposes motion. Air resistance (drag) is friction in fluids. Hooke's Law states that the extension of a spring is directly proportional to the force applied, provided the elastic limit is not exceeded.

DEFINITIONS

Key Terms

Resultant Force: The single force that has the same effect as all the forces acting on an object.

Hooke's Law: The extension of a spring is directly proportional to the force applied, up to the limit of proportionality.

FORMULAE & CALCULATIONS

Equations of Force

Resultant Force (F) = mass (m) × acceleration (a)
Force (F) = spring constant (k) × extension (x)
VISUALS / LOAD-EXTENSION GRAPH

Spring Experiment

By adding masses to a spring and measuring the extension, a load-extension graph can be plotted. The linear portion proves Hooke's Law, where the gradient represents the spring constant (k).

EXAM PRACTICE

Worked Example

Question 1 (Extended)

A car of mass 1500 kg accelerates at 2 m/s². Calculate the resultant force needed.

Formula: F = ma

Substitution: F = 1500 kg × 2 m/s²

Answer: F = 3000 N
