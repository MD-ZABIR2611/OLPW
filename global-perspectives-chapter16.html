Research & Evidence for the Individual Report

The individual report is only as strong as its evidence. Knowing how to gather primary data and judge secondary sources separates a researched argument from an opinion.

06Key concepts
08Key terms
02Practice questions
—Your status
01

Key Concepts

01

The individual report starts from a focused research question linked to the chosen topic area, and every piece of evidence is gathered to answer that question.

02

Primary research collects first-hand data through surveys, interviews, observations or experiments, while secondary research uses existing sources such as news reports, academic papers and NGO publications.

03

Reliable sources are judged by the author's expertise, the publisher's reputation, the date of publication, visible evidence and references, and corroboration by independent sources.

04

Bias is detected by noting the author's purpose, funding and language; a balanced project deliberately samples contrasting perspectives on the issue.

05

Ethical research obtains informed consent, protects anonymity and avoids harm, especially when interviewing vulnerable people.

06

Evidence is logged with full citations as research proceeds, so the bibliography is complete and every claim in the report can be traced back to its source.

02

Key Terms

TermExam definition
Primary researchThe collection of first-hand data by the researcher, for example through surveys, interviews, observations or experiments.
Secondary researchThe use of existing data and sources collected by other people, such as news reports, books, websites and government statistics.
ReliabilityThe extent to which a source can be trusted to be accurate and consistent, judged by its author, publisher, evidence and date.
BiasA slant in a source that favours one side of an issue, caused by the author's purpose, funding, beliefs or audience.
CorroborationChecking that a claim is supported by at least one other independent source before relying on it as evidence.
Informed consentAgreement given freely by a research participant who understands how their data will be used before taking part.
AnonymityProtecting the identity of research participants so that their answers cannot be traced back to them.
BibliographyAn alphabetical list of all the sources used in a report, giving enough detail for each to be found again.
03

Exam Tip

Exam tip

Evaluating sources is a repeat question: judge the author, publisher, date and corroboration, then state explicitly whether the source is strong enough to use — examiners award the decision mark separately from the description marks.

04

Practice Questions

Structured practice — write your answer, then reveal the model.

FOUNDATION

Describe two advantages of primary research and two advantages of secondary research for an individual report.

Model answerPrimary research produces data that is current and tailored exactly to the research question (1), and the researcher controls the sample so local views can be targeted (1). Secondary research is quick and inexpensive compared with fieldwork (1), and expert sources such as the United Nations or national statistics provide large-scale, credible data (1).
CHALLENGE

A candidate plans to base their individual report entirely on posts from a single blog. Evaluate this plan and explain how the candidate should select and check sources instead.

Model answerA single blog is weak evidence: the author may lack expertise, the posts may be out of date or one-sided, and the claims cannot be verified (1). Strong reports judge sources by the author's expertise, the publisher's reputation and the date of publication (1). Key claims should be corroborated, meaning supported by at least one independent source (1). A balanced report deliberately samples contrasting perspectives rather than one viewpoint (1). Judgement: a blog may be used only alongside stronger independent sources, never as the sole evidence base (1).
