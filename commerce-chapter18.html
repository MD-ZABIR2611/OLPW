Entrepreneurship & the Business Environment

Entrepreneurs organise the factors of production and bear risk in pursuit of profit, while the surrounding business environment creates the opportunities and threats they must read and respond to.

Key Concepts

01

An entrepreneur is a person who organises the factors of production, bears the risks of the enterprise and innovates by introducing new products, methods or markets; profit is the reward for successful risk-taking and innovation.

02

The qualities associated with successful entrepreneurs include self-confidence, initiative, creativity, determination, leadership, organising ability and willingness to take calculated risks; these qualities explain why some new businesses succeed while most fail in their first years.

03

The business environment consists of external factors: economic (income levels, interest rates, inflation), social (population and fashion), political and legal (stability, taxes, regulations), technological (automation, the internet) and ecological (climate, sustainability); firms analyse these with a SWOT analysis of strengths, weaknesses, opportunities and threats.

04

The environment creates both opportunity and threat: rising incomes expand markets, and new technology creates new products while destroying old ones; governments can help through grants, low-interest loans, tax holidays, training and business incubators, or hinder through heavy regulation and taxation.

05

Small and medium enterprises (SMEs) dominate employment in most economies; governments support them because they create jobs, spread wealth, encourage competition and innovation, and can grow into major employers.

06

A business plan states the idea, market research, marketing plan, finance required and cash-flow forecast, and is essential when seeking loans or investors; enterprise skills can be developed through business education, apprenticeships and practice.

02

Key Terms

TermExam definition
EntrepreneurA person who organises the factors of production, takes the risks of a business and introduces innovations in pursuit of profit.
InnovationThe successful commercial introduction of new or improved products, processes or marketing methods.
Business planA written document setting out a firm's objectives, market analysis, marketing plan, finance required and cash-flow forecasts, used to attract finance.
SWOT analysisA planning tool that identifies a firm's internal strengths and weaknesses and the external opportunities and threats it faces.
Business environmentThe economic, social, political, legal, technological and ecological conditions outside a firm that affect how it operates.
Business incubatorA scheme or centre that supports new businesses with cheap premises, advice and finance during their early growth.
Risk-bearingAccepting the possibility of losing money when organising a business, for which profit is the reward.
Small and medium enterpriseAn independent firm with few employees and low turnover — commonly under 250 employees — that is a major source of jobs and innovation.

Exam Tip

Exam tip

Entrepreneurship essays are marked in levels: name the qualities, link each to a short example, then argue how two features of the business environment create the opportunities and threats the entrepreneur must exploit or avoid, finishing with a supported judgement.

04

Practice Questions

Structured practice — write your answer, then reveal the model.

FOUNDATION

State four qualities of a successful entrepreneur and explain briefly what each means.

Model answerSelf-confidence: belief in their own ideas and decisions (1). Initiative: acting on opportunities without waiting to be told (1). Willingness to take calculated risks: accepting possible loss for possible profit (1). Determination: continuing despite setbacks, plus creativity or organising ability (1).
CHALLENGE

Evaluate the ways in which a government can encourage the growth of new business enterprises.

Model answerLow-interest loans and grants reduce the capital barrier that prevents many start-ups from launching (1). Business incubators and advice centres give cheap premises, training and mentoring, raising survival rates (1). Tax holidays and simplified registration cut costs and red tape (1). Education in enterprise builds the skills of future entrepreneurs (1). However, support costs the government money, may be wasted on firms that would fail anyway, and can be captured by corruption, so it works best alongside sound legal and economic conditions (1).
