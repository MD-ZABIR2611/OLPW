Presentation & Discussion Skills

The presentation and discussion are where understanding is proved live. Structure, delivery and calm handling of questions turn good research into good marks.

Key Concepts

01

A successful presentation is structured with a clear introduction, signposted sections and a firm conclusion, and it keeps to the allocated time.

02

Visual aids should summarise key points in images and short phrases; slides that repeat paragraphs of text compete with the speaker and lose the audience.

03

Delivery skills — eye contact, an audible pace, confident posture and minimal reading from notes — keep the audience engaged and show ownership of the material.

04

The discussion assesses communication and collaboration: listen to the whole question, pause, and answer the question that was asked rather than a prepared script.

05

Strong candidates respond to challenges by supporting claims with cited evidence, acknowledging counterarguments and conceding limitations honestly.

06

Timed rehearsals and preparing a list of likely questions build confidence; the highest marks go to candidates who can discuss their topic beyond their script.

02

Key Terms

TermExam definition
PresentationThe assessed spoken component in which a candidate communicates their argument and evidence to an audience, alone or with a team.
DiscussionThe assessed question-and-answer part of a presentation in which candidates respond to the audience and develop their ideas.
Visual aidA slide, image, chart or object used during a presentation to support the spoken message rather than repeat it.
Eye contactLooking at the audience while speaking, a delivery skill that builds engagement and shows confidence.
Body languageThe non-verbal signals a speaker sends through posture, gestures, movement and facial expressions.
TimekeepingKeeping a presentation within its allocated time by rehearsing and prioritising the most important content.
Active listeningConcentrating fully on a question as it is asked so the answer responds to that question rather than a prepared script.
Open questionA question that cannot be answered with yes or no, encouraging fuller answers in interviews and discussions.
03

Exam Tip

Exam tip

In the discussion, pause before answering and address the question actually asked. Bringing one prepared piece of evidence that can be applied to an unexpected question shows the live understanding the top band rewards.

04

Practice Questions

Structured practice — write your answer, then reveal the model.

FOUNDATION

List four features of effective delivery in an assessed presentation.

Model answerA clear structure with an introduction, signposted sections and a conclusion (1). Confident body language and eye contact with the audience (1). Visual aids that support the message rather than repeat it (1). Keeping to the allocated time through rehearsal (1).
CHALLENGE

During the assessed discussion, a panellist challenges the evidence behind your main argument. Evaluate how you should respond, and justify your approach.

Model answerListen fully to the challenge and pause before answering the question actually asked, not a prepared script (1). Support the claim with the cited evidence, explaining why the source is reliable (1). Acknowledge the counterargument honestly and concede limits where the evidence is genuinely weak (1). Link the answer back to the overall argument to show the issue is understood beyond the script (1). Judgement: calm, evidence-based responses score higher than defending every point at all costs, because the assessment rewards candidates who consider different perspectives (1).
