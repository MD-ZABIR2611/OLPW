Electric Circuits

CORE
THEORY

Series Circuits

In a series circuit there is one loop: the current is the SAME everywhere; the supply voltage is SHARED between components in proportion to their resistances; total resistance is the sum of the separate resistances (Rt = R1 + R2 + ...).

One break or one blown bulb stops the whole circuit. Cell voltages in series add up.

Parallel Circuits

In a parallel circuit each branch gets the full supply voltage; the current SPLITS between branches (adding up to the supply current); total resistance is LESS than the smallest branch resistance: 1/Rt = 1/R1 + 1/R2.

Household lighting is wired in parallel so every lamp gets full voltage and works independently.

Sensing Circuits

A thermistor’s resistance falls when it gets warmer; an LDR’s resistance falls in brighter light. Wired in a potential divider, they switch on heaters, street lamps and night lights automatically.

DEFINITIONS

Series Circuit: A single loop where the same current passes through every component in turn.

Parallel Circuit: A circuit with branches, each connected across the same supply voltage.

Thermistor: A resistor whose resistance decreases as temperature rises; used in temperature-sensing circuits.

LDR (Light-Dependent Resistor): A resistor whose resistance decreases as light intensity increases; used in automatic lights.

Potential Divider: Two resistors in series that split the supply voltage so part of the circuit gets a chosen fraction of it.

FORMULAE & CALCULATIONS

Circuit Rules

Learn the series and parallel resistance rules and when each applies.

Series: Rt = R1 + R2 + R3 ...
Parallel: 1/Rt = 1/R1 + 1/R2 ...
V out (divider) = V supply × R2 / (R1 + R2)
PRACTICAL WORK

Build a sensing circuit: a thermistor and fixed resistor in series across a supply, with the output across the fixed resistor. Warm the thermistor - its resistance falls, more voltage appears across the fixed resistor - exactly how a fan heater’s thermostat works.

EXAM PRACTICE

Test Your Knowledge

Question 1

A 4 Ω and a 12 Ω resistor are connected in parallel. Calculate the total resistance.

1/Rt = 1/4 + 1/12 = 3/12 + 1/12 = 4/12 (1).

Rt = 12/4 = 3 Ω - less than either branch resistance (1).

Question 2

Explain why a street lamp with an LDR switches on automatically at dusk.

At dusk the light level falls, so the LDR’s resistance rises (1).

Its share of the supply voltage rises until the switching circuit detects enough voltage to turn the lamp on (1).
