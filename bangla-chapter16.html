Chapter 16 · Bangla

Major Writers and Poets of Bangla

Knowing the canon means knowing each writer's voice. This chapter profiles the major figures of Bangla literature, pairing each with representative works and the distinctive themes you can cite in any essay.

Key Concepts

01

Rabindranath Tagore (1861–1941) wrote the novel Gora, the poetry collection Gitanjali (গীতাঞ্জলি) and over two thousand songs (Rabindra Sangeet), winning the Nobel Prize in 1913 — the first non-European laureate in Literature.

02

Tagore's themes span humanism, the divine in nature, childhood innocence and critique of blind nationalism and rigid orthodoxy; his songs merge classical raga with folk baul influence.

03

Kazi Nazrul Islam (1899–1976) declared "break down, O beloved, the prison of convention" in Bidrohi (বিদ্রোহী), fusing rebel nationalism, Muslim devotion and folk rhythm; Agnibeena (অগ্নিবীণা) announced his arrival.

04

Bankim Chandra Chattopadhyay's Anandamath (1882) contained Vande Mataram, the hymn later central to Indian nationalism, while Durgeshnandini founded the historical romance in Bangla.

05

Sarat Chandra Chattopadhyay brought the rural poor, the widow and the outcast into fiction in Debdas and Parineeta; Jibanananda Das is the poet of modernist solitude, Bengal's landscape and twilight, in Rupashi Bangla (রূপসী বাংলা).

06

For exam answers, quote each writer's emblematic line or title: Gitanjali for Tagore's spiritual humanism, Bidrohi for Nazrul's defiance, Vande Mataram for Bankim, and "বাংলার মুখ আমি দেখিয়াছি" ("I have seen Bengal's face") for Jibanananda.

Key Terms

TermExam definition
Rabindra SangeetThe body of over two thousand songs composed by Rabindranath Tagore, blending classical raga with Bengali folk traditions.
GitanjaliTagore's celebrated verse collection, its English translation earning him the 1913 Nobel Prize in Literature.
BidrohiNazrul's 1921 long poem "The Rebel", a declaration of revolt against oppression that defined his identity as the Rebel Poet.
Vande MataramThe national hymn from Bankim Chandra's novel Anandamath, meaning "I bow to thee, Mother" in Sanskrit.
Rupashi BanglaJibanananda Das's poetry collection whose title means "Beautiful Bengal", hymning the land's landscape and solitude.
Nobel Prize in LiteratureThe award Tagore received in 1913 for Gitanjali, the first Nobel in Literature given to a non-European writer.
Baul traditionA Bengali folk-mystic tradition of wandering minstrels whose music influenced both Tagore and Nazrul.
DebdasSarat Chandra's tragic novel of unfulfilled love, repeatedly adapted into film and emblematic of his popular style.

Exam Tip

Exam tip

Memorise a "one line, one theme" card per writer — Tagore: Gitanjali, spiritual humanism; Nazrul: Bidrohi, rebellion; Bankim: Vande Mataram, nationalism; Jibanananda: "বাংলার মুখ আমি দেখিয়াছি", landscape and solitude. Four cards cover almost any set-text or essay question on the modern canon.

Practice Questions

Structured practice — write your answer, then reveal the model.

FOUNDATION

Match each writer to their signature work: Rabindranath Tagore, Kazi Nazrul Islam, Bankim Chandra Chattopadhyay, Jibanananda Das.

Model answerTagore — Gitanjali (গীতাঞ্জলি) (1); Nazrul — Bidrohi (বিদ্রোহী) (1); Bankim — Anandamath / Vande Mataram (1); Jibanananda Das — Rupashi Bangla (রূপসী বাংলা) (1).
CHALLENGE

Compare the nationalist voices of Tagore and Nazrul, and explain why both are called poets of Bengal yet are so different in manner.

Model answerShared ground: both made Bangla a language of freedom and both honoured Bengal's folk and spiritual heritage, drawing on baul and Sufi currents (1). Difference in manner: Tagore's nationalism is reflective and universalist — Gora interrogates sectarian pride and blind identity — while Nazrul's is confrontational and rhythmic, Bidrohi roaring against every tyranny with direct address and military imagery (1). Judgement: Tagore asks what a just nation should be; Nazrul demands the nation rise now — the lyric meditative voice versus the drumbeat rebel voice, together covering the full emotional register of Bengali nationalism (1).
