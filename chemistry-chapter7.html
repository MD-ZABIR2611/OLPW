Chapter Overview

What is it?

This chapter explores the speed at which chemical reactions occur (rate of reaction) and the factors that affect it, such as temperature, concentration, surface area, and catalysts.

Why It Matters

Controlling reaction rates is essential. In industry, maximizing speed maximizes profit. In everyday life, it prevents explosions and preserves food. Understanding kinetics is key to mastering chemistry.

03

Detailed Theory

01

Collision Theory

Definition: Chemical reactions occur when reactant particles collide. For a collision to be successful, particles must possess the activation energy (minimum energy) and the correct orientation.

Why it matters: If particles don't collide hard enough, or hit at the wrong angle, no reaction occurs. The rate depends on the frequency of successful collisions.

02

Factors Affecting Rate

  • Temperature: Higher temp = particles move faster. More collisions per second, and more particles have activation energy. Rate increases dramatically.
  • Concentration/Pressure: More particles in the same space = more frequent collisions. Rate increases.
  • Surface Area: Smaller pieces = larger surface area to volume ratio. More solid particles exposed to reactant. Rate increases.
  • Catalyst: Provides an alternative pathway with lower activation energy. More collisions are successful. Rate increases.
03

Catalysts & Enzymes

Catalyst: A substance that increases the rate of a chemical reaction without being chemically changed at the end.

Enzymes: Biological catalysts (proteins) that speed up reactions in living organisms. They are highly specific and work best at an optimum temperature. Above this, they denature and stop working.

Worked Examples

Case Study 02 / Intermediate

Explain Rate Increase

Question: Explain, in terms of particles, why increasing the temperature of a reaction mixture increases the rate.

1. Particles gain kinetic energy and move faster.

2. They collide more frequently.

3. A greater proportion of collisions have energy greater than the activation energy.

4. Therefore, there are more successful collisions per second.

09

Laboratory Protocol

Experiment 01 / Measuring Rate

Marble Chips & Acid

Method: React CaCO3 chips with HCl. Measure volume of CO2 gas produced over time using a gas syringe.

Variables: Independent = size of marble chips (surface area). Dependent = volume of gas produced.

Exam Application: Examiners ask how to prove faster rate. Answer: The graph of gas volume vs time is steeper for powdered chips.

11

Error Database

Wrong

Catalysts are used up in the reaction and become part of the products.

Correct

Catalysts are not used up. They lower the activation energy by providing an alternative pathway and remain chemically unchanged at the end.

Wrong

Increasing temperature increases rate because particles get larger.

Correct

Particles do not change size. They gain kinetic energy, move faster, and collide more frequently and with greater energy.
