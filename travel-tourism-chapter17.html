Chapter 17 · Travel and Tourism

Events, Conferences & Visitor Attractions

Attractions and events give tourists reasons to travel, and business tourism fills beds year-round. Hosting them brings benefits — and bills.

Key Concepts

01

Visitor attractions divide into natural attractions such as beaches, mountains and national parks, purpose-built attractions such as theme parks and aquariums, and heritage attractions such as castles and archaeological sites.

02

Events range from local festivals to mega-events such as the Olympic Games and World Expo, which bring global media attention and concentrated visitor spending.

03

MICE tourism — meetings, incentives, conferences and exhibitions — fills hotel beds in the off-season and spends more per head than leisure tourism.

04

Conferences require convention centres, business hotels and professional conference organisers to manage the bidding, logistics and delegate services.

05

Attractions manage visitor pressure with timed tickets, capacity limits, conservation work and interpretation, so the resource survives the tourism it attracts.

06

Hosting major events is contested: the economic boost, jobs and destination image must be weighed against cost, congestion, security and the risk of underused venues afterwards.

02

Key Terms

TermExam definition
Visitor attractionA place, event or activity that motivates tourists to visit a destination, such as a museum, theme park or festival.
Natural attractionAn attraction created by nature rather than built by people, such as a beach, mountain range or national park.
Purpose-built attractionAn attraction designed and constructed specifically to entertain or interest visitors, such as a theme park or aquarium.
Heritage attractionA place valued for its historical or cultural importance, such as a castle, monument or archaeological site.
ConferenceA formal meeting, usually lasting one or more days, where people in the same industry or profession share information and network.
MICEThe business tourism sector covering meetings, incentives, conferences and exhibitions, known for high spending per visitor.
Convention centreA large purpose-built venue designed to host conferences, exhibitions and major events.
Mega-eventA very large event with worldwide reach and major media coverage, such as the Olympic Games or a World Expo.
03

Exam Tip

Exam tip

Classify attractions precisely and justify the category in the same sentence. For events questions, balance the benefits with the costs and end with a judgement — the conclusion mark is awarded separately.

04

Practice Questions

Structured practice — write your answer, then reveal the model.

FOUNDATION

Classify each attraction as natural, purpose-built or heritage, justifying each choice: (a) the Great Barrier Reef, (b) a Disneyland theme park, (c) the ruins of Machu Picchu, (d) an aquarium built on a pier.

Model answerGreat Barrier Reef: natural attraction, created by nature and valued for its marine ecosystem (1). Disneyland: purpose-built, constructed specifically to entertain visitors (1). Machu Picchu: heritage, valued for its historic and cultural importance (1). Aquarium on a pier: purpose-built, designed and built to display marine life to visitors (1).
CHALLENGE

A city is deciding whether to bid to host an international conference for 5,000 delegates. Evaluate the likely benefits and costs, and reach a judgement.

Model answerBenefits: delegates spend heavily on hotels, restaurants and transport, boosting income and jobs (1). Hosting raises the city's image as a business destination and helps attract future events (1). Costs: bidding and staging are expensive, requiring spending on venues, security and transport (1). Risks: congestion and disruption for residents, plus underused venues afterwards (1). Judgement: the bid is worthwhile if the city already has suitable venues and a plan to reuse the facilities, so the long-term gains exceed the costs (1).
