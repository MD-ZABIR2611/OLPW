Digital Technology, Ethics & Social Impact

CORE
SECTION 01 — CHAPTER OVERVIEW

Computing in Society

The rapid growth of computing has transformed how we live, work, and interact. This chapter explores the ethical, social, and environmental impacts of technology, including the digital divide, artificial intelligence, and e-waste.

SECTION 02 — LEARNING OBJECTIVES

Chapter Goals

  • Explain the concept of the digital divide.
  • Distinguish between open-source and proprietary software.
  • Discuss the impacts of automation and AI on employment.
  • Describe the environmental impact of computing (e-waste, energy use).
SECTION 03 — CORE THEORY

Ethics & Environment

The Digital Divide is the gap between those who have access to modern information and communications technology and those who do not. This can be due to geography (rural vs urban), wealth, or age.

Software Licensing

Open-Source: Source code is freely available to use, modify, and distribute (e.g., Linux, Firefox).

Proprietary: Software is owned by an individual/company. Source code is hidden, and users must pay for a license to use it (e.g., Microsoft Windows, Adobe Photoshop).

Environmental Impact

Computing requires vast amounts of energy, contributing to carbon emissions. Furthermore, the rapid disposal of old hardware creates e-waste, which contains toxic materials that leach into the soil if not recycled properly.

SECTION 04 — KEY DEFINITIONS

Examination Definitions

DIGITAL DIVIDE: The unequal access to ICT and the internet between different groups of people.

E-WASTE: Discarded electronic devices and components that are no longer in use.

COPYRIGHT: The legal right protecting the creator of original work from unauthorized copying or distribution.

SECTION 06 — EXAM TECHNIQUE

Discussing Impacts

When asked to "discuss" or "evaluate", always provide balanced arguments. For example, if discussing AI: state the advantage (efficiency, 24/7 operation) and the disadvantage (job losses, lack of human empathy).

SECTION 07 — COMMON MISTAKES

Avoid These Errors

Mistake: Confusing open-source with free software.

Correction: Open-source means the code is public; it doesn't necessarily mean the software is free of charge (though it usually is). Proprietary software is always paid/licensed.

SECTION 08 — QUICK REVISION

Fast Facts

  • Open-Source = Free code. Proprietary = Closed code.
  • E-waste contains toxic materials; must be recycled.
  • Cloud computing reduces local storage needs but increases server energy use.
SECTION 09 — EXAM QUESTIONS

Test Your Knowledge

Question 1 (Standard)

Describe two environmental impacts of the rapid replacement of computing devices.

2 marks: 1) E-waste ends up in landfills, releasing toxic chemicals into the soil and water. 2) Huge amounts of energy are consumed in manufacturing new devices and running server farms, contributing to global warming.
