Momentum

EXTENDED
THEORY

Conservation of Momentum

Momentum is a vector quantity relating the mass of an object to its velocity. In a closed system, the total momentum before a collision or explosion is equal to the total momentum after the event. This is known as the principle of conservation of momentum.

When objects collide, they exert equal and opposite forces on each other (Newton's Third Law). If the collision is elastic, kinetic energy is conserved. If inelastic, kinetic energy is transferred to heat or deformation energy.

DEFINITIONS

Key Terms

Momentum: The product of mass and velocity.

Impulse: The change in momentum, equal to force multiplied by time.

FORMULAE & CALCULATIONS

Momentum Equations

momentum (p) = mass (m) × velocity (v)
force (F) = change in momentum (Δp) / time (t)

EXAM PRACTICE

Worked Example

Question 1 (Extended)

A trolley of mass 2 kg moves at 3 m/s. It collides with a stationary trolley of mass 1 kg, and they stick together. Calculate their combined velocity after the collision.

Momentum Before: p = (2 kg × 3 m/s) + (1 kg × 0 m/s) = 6 kg·m/s

Momentum After: p = (2 kg + 1 kg) × v = 3v

Conservation: 3v = 6

Answer: v = 2 m/s
