The Trial Balance

Explains the purpose, preparation and limitations of the trial balance, including the types of error it does and does not reveal and the use of a suspense account.

01

Overview

Once all transactions have been posted and the ledger accounts balanced, the bookkeeper lists every balance in a trial balance. If the double entry has been carried out correctly, the total of the debit balances will equal the total of the credit balances. The trial balance is therefore a check on the arithmetical accuracy of the ledger and the starting point for preparing the financial statements. However, a trial balance that agrees does not prove the books are free from error. Examiners test both sides of this idea: you must be able to prepare a trial balance from a list of balances and also explain the six types of error that it cannot reveal.

By the end of this chapter you should be able to:

  • State the purposes of a trial balance.
  • Prepare a trial balance from a list of ledger balances, placing each balance on the correct side.
  • Explain why a trial balance that agrees does not prove the accounts are correct.
  • Identify and give examples of the six types of error not revealed by a trial balance.
  • Identify errors that do cause the trial balance totals to disagree.
  • Explain the purpose of opening a suspense account.
  • Redraft an incorrectly prepared trial balance.
02

What Is a Trial Balance?

A trial balance is a list of all the balances in the ledger at a particular date, arranged in two columns: debit balances and credit balances. Because every transaction has been recorded with an equal debit and credit, the totals of the two columns should be equal. The trial balance is usually prepared at the end of each month and always at the end of the financial year, before the financial statements are drawn up.

The heading must include the name of the business and the date, for example 'Trial balance of Maya Stores at 31 December 2026'. It is a list at a single point in time, so the words 'at' or 'as at' are correct. The trial balance is not part of the double-entry system; it is simply a working document extracted from the ledger.

The trial balance serves three purposes. It checks the arithmetical accuracy of the double entry. It provides a convenient summary of all balances from which the income statement and statement of financial position can be prepared. It helps to locate errors, because if the totals disagree the bookkeeper knows that at least one error exists and can begin investigating.

  • Checks the arithmetical accuracy of the ledger entries.
  • Provides a summary of balances for preparing financial statements.
  • Helps in detecting errors when the totals disagree.
03

Placing Balances on the Correct Side

The side on which each balance appears depends on the type of account, following the same rules as double entry. Debit balances include assets (premises, equipment, inventory, trade receivables, bank if in funds, cash), expenses (purchases, wages, rent, carriage, discount allowed), sales returns and drawings. Credit balances include liabilities (trade payables, loans, bank overdraft), capital, income (sales, discount received, rent received, commission received) and purchases returns.

A useful check is to remember that the trial balance mirrors the accounting equation extended to include income and expenses: Assets + Expenses + Drawings = Capital + Liabilities + Income. If you are unsure about an item, ask yourself whether it is something the business owns or spends (debit) or something it owes or earns (credit).

The inventory figure in the trial balance is the opening inventory, that is, the inventory at the start of the year. Closing inventory is not normally in the trial balance because it is counted after the ledger has been balanced; it is given as a separate note and used in preparing the financial statements.

Key fact:Debit: assets, expenses, drawings, sales returns. Credit: liabilities, capital, income, purchases returns.
04

Limitations of the Trial Balance

A trial balance that agrees shows only that total debits equal total credits. It does not prove that every transaction has been recorded, that the correct accounts were used, or that the amounts were right. Some errors affect debits and credits equally and so leave the totals in agreement. These are the errors not revealed by a trial balance, and there are six main types.

Because of these limitations, businesses use other checks as well, such as bank reconciliations, control accounts and statements from suppliers. The trial balance is a useful first test but not a guarantee of accuracy. In an examination, when asked to explain why a trial balance that agrees may still contain errors, name the types of error and give a specific example of at least one.

05

Errors Not Revealed by a Trial Balance

An error of omission occurs when a transaction is completely left out of the books, so neither the debit nor the credit is made; for example, a sales invoice for $300 is lost and never recorded. An error of commission occurs when an amount is entered in the correct type of account but the wrong person's account; for example, a payment from P. Shah is credited to P. Shaw. An error of principle occurs when an amount is entered in the wrong type of account, such as treating the purchase of a machine (a non-current asset) as purchases or repairs (an expense).

An error of original entry occurs when the wrong amount is recorded in the book of prime entry and then posted to both accounts; for example, an invoice for $540 is entered as $450 and both the customer and sales are recorded at $450. A complete reversal of entries occurs when the correct accounts and amounts are used but the debit and credit are reversed; for example, a cheque received from a customer is debited to the customer and credited to bank.

A compensating error occurs when two or more separate errors of equal value cancel each other out; for example, the sales account is overcast by $100 and the wages account is also overcast by $100. Because the credit side and debit side are both overstated by the same amount, the totals still agree. A useful memory aid for the six errors is the phrase 'O C P O R C': omission, commission, principle, original entry, reversal, compensating.

  • Omission: transaction not recorded at all.
  • Commission: correct type of account, wrong individual account.
  • Principle: wrong type (class) of account used.
  • Original entry: wrong amount recorded in both accounts.
  • Complete reversal: correct accounts and amount, but debit and credit reversed.
  • Compensating: two errors of equal amount cancel each other.
06

Errors That Are Revealed

Errors that affect only one side, or affect the two sides by different amounts, cause the trial balance totals to disagree. These include a single-sided entry (for example, recording a cash sale in the cash book but not in the sales account), entering different amounts on the two sides (a transposition error in only one account, such as $360 debited and $630 credited), entering both sides of a transaction on the same side, casting (adding) errors in an account, errors in balancing an account, and omitting a balance from the trial balance or placing it on the wrong side.

A useful tip is that if a balance is placed on the wrong side of the trial balance, the difference in the totals will be twice the amount of that balance. If the difference is divisible by 9, a transposition error, where two digits have been reversed, may be the cause. These clues help the bookkeeper to track down errors quickly.

07

The Suspense Account

When the trial balance totals do not agree and the error cannot be found immediately, the difference can be entered in a suspense account so that the financial statements can be prepared. If the credit side is smaller, the difference is entered as a credit balance in the suspense account, and vice versa. The suspense account is a temporary account and must be cleared as soon as the errors are found and corrected.

When each error is discovered, a journal entry is made to correct it, with one side of the entry in the suspense account if the error affected the trial balance totals. Once all errors are corrected the suspense account will have no balance. Chapter 6 covers the correction of errors and the suspense account in full, including the effect of corrections on profit.

Key fact:Suspense account = temporary account holding the trial balance difference until errors are found.
08

Redrafting an Incorrect Trial Balance

A common examination question gives a trial balance prepared by an inexperienced bookkeeper, with some items on the wrong side, and asks you to redraft it correctly. Work through the list item by item, deciding whether each is an asset, expense or drawings (debit) or a liability, capital or income (credit). Then total both columns and check that they agree.

Watch especially for items that are commonly misplaced: sales returns (debit), purchases returns (credit), discount allowed (debit), discount received (credit), carriage inwards and outwards (both debit), bank overdraft (credit) and drawings (debit). If your corrected totals still do not agree, recheck your addition before assuming the question contains an error.

Key Terms

Worked Examples

Preparing a trial balance

Prepare the trial balance of Maya Stores at 31 December 2026 from these balances: capital $38,500; drawings $6,000; premises $30,000; motor vehicle $12,000; inventory 1 January 2026 $4,500; purchases $38,000; sales $65,000; sales returns $1,200; purchases returns $900; wages $9,800; rent $4,000; trade receivables $5,600; trade payables $4,300; bank (in funds) $2,100; loan (repayable 2031) $5,000; discount allowed $300; discount received $250; carriage inwards $450.

  1. Step 1: Debit column: Drawings $6,000; Premises $30,000; Motor vehicle $12,000; Inventory $4,500; Purchases $38,000; Sales returns $1,200; Wages $9,800; Rent $4,000; Trade receivables $5,600; Bank $2,100; Discount allowed $300; Carriage inwards $450.
  2. Step 2: Total debits = $113,950.
  3. Step 3: Credit column: Capital $38,500; Sales $65,000; Purchases returns $900; Trade payables $4,300; Loan $5,000; Discount received $250.
  4. Step 4: Total credits = $113,950.
  5. Step 5: The totals agree, confirming the arithmetical accuracy of the ledger (but not the absence of all errors).
Trial balance of Maya Stores at 31 December 2026 totals $113,950 on both sides.

Classifying errors

State the type of error in each case and whether it affects the agreement of the trial balance: (a) a new computer for $1,200 was debited to office expenses; (b) a credit sale of $780 to K. Lim was debited to K. Lin; (c) rent paid $650 was credited to rent and debited to bank; (d) the sales account was undercast by $200.

  1. (a) A non-current asset has been treated as an expense: error of principle. Debit and credit are both $1,200, so the trial balance still agrees.
  2. (b) Correct type of account (a customer) but wrong individual: error of commission. Trial balance agrees.
  3. (c) Correct accounts and amount, but sides reversed: complete reversal of entries. Trial balance agrees.
  4. (d) A casting error in one account only: the credit side is understated by $200, so the trial balance does not agree; the credit total will be $200 short.
(a) Principle, (b) commission and (c) complete reversal are not revealed; (d) undercasting of sales is revealed because credits are $200 less than debits.

Redrafting an incorrect trial balance

A trainee prepared this trial balance: Debits — capital $5,900, purchases $14,000, trade receivables $3,000, discount received $150, bank overdraft $600; Credits — sales $22,500, equipment $8,000, trade payables $2,100, sales returns $350, wages $3,400, drawings $2,500. Redraft it correctly.

  1. Step 1: Identify debit balances: Equipment $8,000; Purchases $14,000; Trade receivables $3,000; Sales returns $350; Wages $3,400; Drawings $2,500.
  2. Step 2: Total debits = $8,000 + $14,000 + $3,000 + $350 + $3,400 + $2,500 = $31,250.
  3. Step 3: Identify credit balances: Capital $5,900; Sales $22,500; Trade payables $2,100; Discount received $150; Bank overdraft $600.
  4. Step 4: Total credits = $5,900 + $22,500 + $2,100 + $150 + $600 = $31,250.
  5. Step 5: The trainee's version showed debits of $23,650 and credits of $38,850; moving the seven misplaced items (capital, discount received, bank overdraft, equipment, sales returns, wages and drawings) to their proper sides makes both columns total $31,250.
Corrected trial balance totals $31,250 on both sides once each item is placed on its proper side.

Common Mistakes & Exam Tips

Common mistakes

  • Stating that an agreed trial balance proves the accounts are correct. It only proves total debits equal total credits.
  • Putting closing inventory in the trial balance. The trial balance normally contains opening inventory only.
  • Placing a bank overdraft in the debit column. An overdraft is a liability and is a credit balance.
  • Confusing errors of commission and principle. Commission uses the wrong account of the right type; principle uses the wrong type of account.
  • Heading the trial balance 'for the year ended'. It is prepared at a date, so use 'at'.
  • Forgetting that a balance on the wrong side causes a difference of twice its amount, not the amount itself.

Exam tips

  • Learn the six errors with one clear example each; an example is usually required for the second mark.
  • Use the extended equation Assets + Expenses + Drawings = Capital + Liabilities + Income to check the side of any balance.
  • When the difference is divisible by 9, look for a transposition error.
  • When a question asks whether an error affects the trial balance, give a reason, such as 'debits and credits are both understated by the same amount'.
  • List the trial balance in a sensible order (non-current assets, current assets, liabilities, capital, income, expenses) to reduce the chance of omissions.
  • Always total both columns and underline them; examiners award a mark for correct totals.

Practice Questions

[1 mark]

1. State one purpose of a trial balance.

Model answerTo check the arithmetical accuracy of the double entry (1). Also accept: to provide a summary of balances for preparing financial statements; to help locate errors.
[4 marks]

2. State whether each balance appears in the debit or credit column of a trial balance: (a) discount allowed; (b) purchases returns; (c) bank overdraft; (d) drawings.

Model answer(a) Debit (1). (b) Credit (1). (c) Credit (1). (d) Debit (1).
[2 marks]

3. Explain what is meant by an error of original entry and give an example.

Model answerThe wrong amount is recorded in the book of prime entry and posted to both accounts (1). Example: a purchase invoice for $870 recorded as $780 in the purchases journal and posted to purchases and the supplier at $780 (1).
[2 marks]

4. Distinguish between an error of commission and an error of principle.

Model answerAn error of commission is when an entry is made in the correct class of account but the wrong individual account, e.g. wrong customer (1). An error of principle is when an entry is made in the wrong class of account, e.g. a non-current asset debited to an expense account (1).
[2 marks]

5. Explain why closing inventory does not normally appear in the trial balance.

Model answerClosing inventory is counted and valued at the end of the year after the ledger accounts have been balanced (1), so it has not been recorded in the ledger at the time the trial balance is extracted; it is given as a separate note (1).
[2 marks]

6. A trial balance's debit total exceeds its credit total by $840. Upon checking, a balance was found on the wrong side. What was the amount of the balance?

Model answerA balance on the wrong side causes a difference of twice its value (1), so the balance is $840 ÷ 2 = $420 (1), a credit balance that was entered as a debit.
[6 marks]

7. Name three types of error that are not revealed by a trial balance and give an example of each.

Model answerOmission: a sales invoice not recorded at all (1)(1). Complete reversal: cash received from a customer debited to the customer and credited to cash (1)(1). Compensating: sales overcast by $50 and rent overcast by $50 (1)(1). Also accept commission, principle and original entry with examples.
[3 marks]

8. Explain the purpose of a suspense account.

Model answerIt is a temporary account in which the difference in the trial balance totals is entered (1) so that the trial balance can be made to agree and financial statements prepared (1) while the errors are investigated; it is cleared when errors are corrected (1).
[2 marks]

9. State two errors that would cause the trial balance totals to disagree.

Model answerSingle-sided entry, e.g. a cash sale recorded only in the cash book (1). A casting error in one account, e.g. purchases undercast (1). Also accept: different amounts in the two accounts; balance omitted from the trial balance; both entries on the same side.
[7 marks]

Prepare a trial balance at 30 June 2026 from: capital $25,000; equipment $15,000; inventory 1 July 2025 $3,200; purchases $21,000; sales $36,400; trade receivables $4,100; trade payables $2,900; bank overdraft $1,300; wages $7,500; rent $3,600; carriage outwards $400; discount received $200; drawings $11,000.

Model answerDebits: equipment $15,000, inventory $3,200, purchases $21,000, trade receivables $4,100, wages $7,500, rent $3,600, carriage outwards $400, drawings $11,000 (2 for correct items) = $65,800 (1). Credits: capital $25,000, sales $36,400, trade payables $2,900, bank overdraft $1,300, discount received $200 (2 for correct items) = $65,800 (1). Correct heading (1).
[7 marks]

11. The trial balance of a business agrees. The owner concludes that her accounting records must be accurate. Evaluate her conclusion.

Model answerThe agreement shows that total debits equal total credits, so there are no arithmetical errors affecting one side only (1). However, some errors do not affect agreement (1): omission, where nothing is recorded (1); commission and principle, where the wrong account is used (1); original entry, where the wrong amount is used on both sides (1); complete reversal and compensating errors (1). These may cause profit or assets to be misstated (1). Conclusion: her conclusion is not justified; other checks such as bank reconciliation and control accounts should be used (1). Max 7.
[6 marks]

12. State which of the following errors would cause the trial balance not to agree, giving a reason: (a) motor repairs $300 debited to motor vehicles account; (b) discount allowed $45 recorded only in the customer's account; (c) sales invoice $1,650 omitted from the books.

Model answer(a) Agrees — both debit and credit entries were made for $300, only the wrong class of account was used (1)(1). (b) Does not agree — only the credit entry was made, so credits exceed debits by $45 (1)(1). (c) Agrees — neither side was recorded (1)(1).

Quick Recap

  1. A trial balance lists all ledger balances at a date in debit and credit columns; the totals should agree.
  2. It checks arithmetical accuracy, provides a basis for financial statements and helps locate errors.
  3. Assets, expenses, drawings and sales returns are debit balances; liabilities, capital, income and purchases returns are credit balances.
  4. Opening inventory appears in the trial balance; closing inventory is given separately.
  5. Six errors are not revealed: omission, commission, principle, original entry, complete reversal and compensating errors.
  6. Single-sided entries, casting errors and balances on the wrong side cause the totals to disagree.
  7. A suspense account temporarily holds the difference until the errors are found and corrected.
