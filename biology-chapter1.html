SECTION 03 — CORE THEORY

Life, Classification & Cells

Characteristics of Living Organisms

All living organisms share seven basic characteristics. These are often remembered by the mnemonic MRS GREN:

Movement: Change in position by part or all of an organism.

Respiration: Chemical reactions in cells to release energy.

Sensitivity: Detecting and responding to changes in the environment.

Growth: Permanent increase in size and dry mass.

Reproduction: Producing offspring.

Excretion: Removing toxic waste products of metabolism.

Nutrition: Obtaining materials for energy and growth.

Classification & Binomial System

Classification is the grouping of organisms based on their characteristics. The binomial naming system, developed by Carl Linnaeus, gives every species a two-part Latin name: Genus (capitalized) and species (lowercase). E.g., Homo sapiens.

Levels of Organisation

Multicellular organisms show a hierarchy of structural organisation: Organelles → Cells → Tissues → Organs → Organ Systems.

SECTION 04 — KEY DEFINITIONS

Examination Definitions

CELL: The smallest structural and functional unit of an organism.

TISSUE: A group of similar cells working together to perform a specific function.

ORGAN: A structure made of different tissues working together to perform a function.

SPECIES: A group of organisms that can reproduce to produce fertile offspring.

SECTION 06 — PRACTICAL LAB

Microscopy & Magnification

AIM

To observe plant and animal cells under a microscope and calculate magnification.

METHOD

1. Prepare a thin layer of onion epidermis on a slide.

2. Add a drop of iodine stain (to make the nucleus visible).

3. Lower a cover slip carefully to avoid air bubbles.

4. Observe under low power (10x), then high power (40x).

CALCULATION

Magnification = Image Size ÷ Actual Size

SECTION 07 — EXAM TECHNIQUE

Command Words

EXPLAIN: Give the reason or mechanism. Use "because" or "therefore".

SECTION 08 — COMMON MISTAKES

Avoid These Errors

Mistake: Saying "respiration is breathing".

Correction: Respiration is a chemical reaction in cells to release energy. Breathing is ventilation.

Mistake: Mixing up "tissue" and "organ". A tissue is one type of cell; an organ is several tissues.

SECTION 09 — QUICK REVISION

Fast Facts

  • MRS GREN defines life (Movement, Respiration, Sensitivity, Growth, Reproduction, Excretion, Nutrition).
  • Binomial names are Genus (capital) and species (lowercase).
  • Plant cells have: Cell Wall, Chloroplasts, Permanent Vacuole (Animal cells do not).
  • Magnification formula: Image ÷ Actual.
SECTION 10 — EXAM QUESTIONS

Test Your Knowledge

Question 1 (Foundation)

State the main function of the nucleus in a cell.

1 mark: Contains genetic material / controls cell activities.

Question 2 (Challenge)

A student observes a cell under a microscope. The image measures 40mm. The actual cell is 0.02mm. Calculate the magnification. Show your working.

2 marks:

Formula: Magnification = Image / Actual

Substitution: M = 40 / 0.02

Answer: M = 2000x
