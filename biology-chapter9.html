Diseases, Immunity & Drugs

SECTION 01 — CHAPTER OVERVIEW

Pathogens & Protection

Diseases can be communicable (infectious) or non-communicable. This chapter covers the types of pathogens that cause disease, how the body's immune system defends itself, and how medicines like antibiotics and vaccines work.

SECTION 02 — LEARNING OBJECTIVES

Chapter Goals

  • Identify types of pathogens (bacteria, viruses, fungi, protoctists).
  • Explain how the immune system defends the body.
  • Describe how vaccines provide immunity.
  • Explain why antibiotics only kill bacteria, not viruses.
  • Describe the process of double-blind drug trials.
SECTION 03 — CORE THEORY

Immunity & Medication

Pathogens are microorganisms that cause disease. Bacteria and viruses reproduce rapidly inside the body. Bacteria produce toxins that damage tissues; viruses invade host cells and replicate, destroying the cells.

Body Defences

Physical: Skin (barrier), mucus (traps pathogens), cilia (waft mucus away).

Chemical: Stomach acid (kills bacteria), lysozymes in tears (destroy bacteria).

Immune System: White blood cells. Phagocytes engulf pathogens (phagocytosis). Lymphocytes produce antibodies that bind to specific antigens on pathogens, destroying them. They also produce antitoxins to neutralize bacterial toxins.

Vaccination

Vaccines contain a dead or weakened form of a pathogen. When injected, it stimulates white blood cells to produce antibodies. This creates "memory cells", so if the real pathogen enters, the body responds rapidly, preventing illness.

Antibiotics

Antibiotics (like penicillin) are drugs that kill bacteria inside the body. They do not kill viruses, because viruses live and reproduce inside host cells, making them hard to target without damaging the body's own cells. Overuse of antibiotics leads to resistant strains (e.g., MRSA).

SECTION 04 — KEY DEFINITIONS

Examination Definitions

PATHOGEN: A microorganism that causes disease.

ANTIBODY: A protein produced by white blood cells that binds to a specific antigen on a pathogen, destroying it.

ANTIBIOTIC: A medicine that kills bacteria inside the body.

SECTION 06 — PRACTICAL LAB

Antibiotic Effectiveness

AIM

To investigate the effectiveness of different antibiotics on bacterial growth.

METHOD

1. Sterilize agar plate. Spread bacteria evenly over surface.

2. Place paper discs soaked in different antibiotics on the agar.

3. Incubate at 25°C for 48 hours.

4. Measure the "clear zone" (area of no bacterial growth) around each disc.

CONCLUSION

The larger the clear zone, the more effective the antibiotic is at killing that bacteria.

SECTION 07 — EXAM TECHNIQUE

Drug Trials

Remember the key terms for drug testing: Placebo (fake drug), Double-blind trial (neither patient nor doctor knows who has the real drug), Control group (given placebo to compare).

SECTION 08 — COMMON MISTAKES

Avoid These Errors

Mistake: Saying antibiotics kill viruses.

Correction: Antibiotics ONLY kill bacteria. Viruses are not killed by antibiotics.

Mistake: Confusing antibodies with antibiotics. Antibodies are natural proteins made by the body. Antibiotics are man-made medicines.

SECTION 09 — QUICK REVISION

Fast Facts

  • Bacteria produce toxins; viruses replicate inside cells.
  • Phagocytes engulf; Lymphocytes produce antibodies.
  • Vaccines provide active immunity using memory cells.
  • Antibiotics do not kill viruses.
  • Drug trials must be double-blind and placebo-controlled.
SECTION 10 — EXAM QUESTIONS

Test Your Knowledge

Question 1 (Standard)

Explain why doctors do not prescribe antibiotics for patients with the common cold.

2 marks: The common cold is caused by a virus. Antibiotics only kill bacteria, so they will have no effect on the virus. Overprescribing antibiotics also increases the risk of antibiotic resistance.
