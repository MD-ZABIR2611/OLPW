Graphs of Curved Functions

A good sketch comes from four signposts: intercepts, roots, turning points and asymptotes. Exponential curves never touch the axis, which is exactly the behaviour depreciation questions exploit.

Key Concepts

01

For y = ax² + bx + c the y-intercept is c, the roots solve ax² + bx + c = 0, and the turning point lies on the axis of symmetry x = −b/(2a).

02

A reciprocal graph such as y = 1/x has two branches and the axes as asymptotes, never touching them.

03

Exponential growth uses a multiplier greater than 1, and exponential decay uses a multiplier between 0 and 1, for example V = 20 000×0.85ⁿ for 15% annual depreciation.

04

The nth term of a depreciation sequence multiplies the starting value by the retention factor raised to power n; after 3 years the factor 0.85³ applies.

05

Estimating solutions of equations is done by reading intersections, such as where a curve meets a straight line, or by drawing a suitable line on the graph.

06

The gradient of a curve changes from point to point; a tangent at a point estimates the gradient there, which is positive, zero or negative according to the slope.

02

Key Terms

TermExam definition
ParabolaThe U-shaped curve produced by a quadratic function, symmetric about a vertical axis through its turning point.
Turning pointA point where a curve changes direction from increasing to decreasing or vice versa; for a parabola it lies on the axis of symmetry.
AsymptoteA line that a curve approaches ever more closely but never reaches, such as the axes for the graph of y = 1/x.
y-interceptThe point where a graph crosses the y-axis, found by substituting x = 0.
Exponential growthChange in which a quantity is multiplied by a constant factor greater than 1 in equal time intervals, producing a rising curve.
Exponential decayChange in which a quantity is multiplied by a constant factor between 0 and 1 in equal time intervals, producing a falling curve approaching an asymptote.
Reciprocal graphThe curve of a function such as y = 1/x with two separate branches, each approaching but never touching the axes.
Gradient of a curveThe rate of change at a point on a curve, estimated by the gradient of the tangent drawn there.
03

Exam Tip

Exam tip

List the signposts before you sketch: y-intercept, roots, turning point, asymptote. In depreciation questions, identify the multiplier as a decimal first, for example 0.85 for a 15% loss.

04

Practice Questions

Structured practice — write your answer, then reveal the model.

FOUNDATION

For the graph of y = x² − 4x + 3, find the y-intercept, the coordinates where the curve crosses the x-axis, and the turning point.

Model answery-intercept: when x = 0, y = 3 (1). Roots: x² − 4x + 3 = 0 gives (x − 1)(x − 3) = 0, so x = 1 or x = 3, crossing at (1, 0) and (3, 0) (1). Axis of symmetry x = −b/(2a) = 4/2 = 2 (1). When x = 2, y = 4 − 8 + 3 = −1, so the turning point is (2, −1) and it is a minimum (1).
CHALLENGE

A car is bought for 20 000 dollars and depreciates by 15% each year, with value V = 20 000×0.85ⁿ after n years. Explain what 0.85 represents, find the value after 3 years, and describe the shape of the graph of V against n.

Model answerEach year the car keeps 100% − 15% = 85% of its value, so the yearly multiplier is 0.85 (1). After 3 years, V = 20 000×0.85³ = 20 000×0.614125 (1) = 12 282.5 dollars, about 12 283 dollars (1). The graph is a decreasing exponential curve: steep at first, flattening as n increases, always positive and approaching the n-axis V = 0 as an asymptote without ever reaching it (1).
