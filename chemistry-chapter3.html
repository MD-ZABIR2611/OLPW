Chapter Overview

What is it?

This chapter explores the microscopic building blocks of matter. It defines atoms, elements, and compounds, delves into the subatomic structure (protons, neutrons, electrons), and explains how atoms interact through ionic, covalent, and metallic bonding to form the materials we see.

Why It Matters

You cannot understand chemical reactions without understanding what is reacting. This chapter provides the foundational rules for chemistry. Mastering bonding and structure allows you to predict macroscopic properties like melting point, electrical conductivity, and strength—core skills for IGCSE/O-Level exams.

Detailed Theory

01

The Atom & Subatomic Particles

Definition: An atom is the smallest particle of a chemical element that can take part in a chemical reaction.

Explanation: Atoms are not solid spheres; they possess an internal structure. The center of the atom is the nucleus, which contains protons (positive) and neutrons (neutral). Orbiting the nucleus in electron shells are electrons (negative).

02

Ionic Bonding

Definition: The electrostatic force of attraction between oppositely charged ions, formed by the transfer of electrons from a metal to a non-metal.

Structure: Ions arrange themselves in a giant 3D lattice to maximize attraction and minimize repulsion.

Properties: High melting points (strong electrostatic forces). Conducts electricity only when molten/aqueous (ions are free to move). Brittle (layers slide, similar charges align and repel).

03

Covalent Bonding

Definition: A chemical bond formed when two non-metal atoms share pairs of electrons to achieve full outer shells.

Simple Molecular Properties: Low melting/boiling points due to weak intermolecular forces (the covalent bonds themselves are very strong). Non-conductive as there are no free-moving ions or electrons.

Laboratory Protocol

Experiment 01 / Electrical Conductivity

Testing Ionic vs Covalent Substances

Objective: To determine if solid, molten, and aqueous ionic compounds conduct electricity, compared to covalent compounds.

Observations: Solid NaCl: No light. Molten NaCl: Light. Sugar solution: No light.

Exam Application: Examiners often ask why solid NaCl doesn't conduct but aqueous does. Answer: Ions are free to move in liquid/aqueous state but fixed in solid lattice.

Error Database

Wrong

Covalent bonds are weak because simple molecules have low boiling points.

Correct

The intermolecular forces between molecules are weak. The actual covalent bonds inside the molecule are very strong.

Wrong

Atoms lose protons to become negative ions.

Correct

Atoms NEVER lose or gain protons. They only gain or lose electrons.

13

Exam Simulation

Basic

1. State the relative mass and charge of a neutron.

Mark Scheme Answer [2 Marks]

Mass = 1, Charge = 0 (neutral).

Intermediate

2. Explain why solid sodium chloride does not conduct electricity, but molten sodium chloride does.

Mark Scheme Answer [2 Marks]

Solid: Ions are locked in a giant lattice and cannot move.
Molten: Ions are free to move and carry the electrical charge.

Advanced

3. Explain, in terms of structure and bonding, why diamond is hard but graphite is soft.

Mark Scheme Answer [3 Marks]

Diamond: Each carbon forms 4 strong covalent bonds in a rigid 3D tetrahedral structure, making it hard.
Graphite: Each carbon forms only 3 bonds, creating flat layers held by weak intermolecular forces. Layers can slide over each other, making it soft.
