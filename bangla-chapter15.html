Chapter 15 · Bangla

History of Bangla Literature and Movements

Bangla literature is read best against its history. This chapter maps the major periods and movements, from the earliest Buddhist mystic songs to the literary flowering that made Bangla a global language.

Key Concepts

01

Old Bangla literature begins with the Charyapada (চর্যাপদ), c. 10th–12th century Buddhist mystical songs by siddhacharyas, its sandhya-bhasha (twilight language) imagery studied for esoteric meaning.

02

The medieval period (14th–18th century) produced Vaishnava padavali poetry centred on Krishna, notably the bi-scriptural songs of Chandidas, and the Mangalkavya epics such as Chandimangal celebrating folk deities.

03

The Bengal Renaissance (19th century), fed by Fort William College and the Serampore Mission Press, modernised Bangla prose: Ramram Basu, Mrityunjay Vidyalankar and William Carey pioneered prose, while Ishwar Chandra Vidyasagar perfected it.

04

The Language Movement (ভাষা আন্দোলন) of 1948–1952 defended Bangla as a state language of Pakistan; 21 February 1952 (Ekushey February) became the martyrs' day now marked worldwide as International Mother Language Day.

05

The modern age divides into the Rabindric era, dominated by Rabindranath Tagore's poetry, songs, novels and plays, and the Kallol era (1922–1935), when the Kallol magazine group brought progressive realism and urban modernity.

06

Nazrul Islam (Kazi Nazrul Islam) revolutionised Bangla poetry with rebel (বিদ্রোহী) themes, Islamic devotion and folk rhythm, earning the title Bidrohi Kobi, the Rebel Poet.

Key Terms

TermExam definition
CharyapadaThe earliest known Bangla manuscript, a collection of Buddhist mystical songs composed around the 10th–12th centuries.
PadavaliA genre of medieval Vaishnava devotional poetry in Bangla celebrating the love of Radha and Krishna.
MangalkavyaA medieval Bangla narrative poem glorifying a folk deity, such as Chandimangal or Manasamangal.
Bengal RenaissanceThe 19th-century intellectual and cultural revival in Bengal that modernised Bangla language and literature.
Language MovementThe 1948–1952 movement in East Pakistan demanding Bangla be recognised as a state language, culminating in the 21 February 1952 killings.
Ekushey February21 February 1952, the day students were killed defending Bangla; now observed worldwide as International Mother Language Day.
Kallol eraThe literary period of the 1920s–30s named after the magazine Kallol, marked by progressive realism and modern urban themes.
Bidrohi KobiThe title "Rebel Poet" given to Kazi Nazrul Islam for his revolutionary and anti-colonial poetry.

Exam Tip

Exam tip

In literature-history questions, anchor every movement to one text and one date: e.g. the Language Movement to 21 February 1952 and Ekushey, the Renaissance to Vidyasagar's prose — examiners award context marks for precise pairing, and vague "in the olden days" phrasing earns nothing.

Practice Questions

Structured practice — write your answer, then reveal the model.

FOUNDATION

Name the earliest known Bangla literary work and state why the Language Movement of 1952 is commemorated internationally.

Model answerThe Charyapada (চর্যাপদ), Buddhist mystical songs from roughly the 10th–12th centuries (1). The 1952 martyrs died defending Bangla as a state language, and 21 February is now International Mother Language Day (1).
CHALLENGE

Discuss how the Bengal Renaissance changed Bangla literature, referring to developments in prose and the role of print.

Model answerProse creation: before the 19th century Bangla was dominated by poetry; Fort William College commissioned prose grammars and tales, and writers such as Mrityunjay Vidyalankar and Vidyasagar produced essays, textbooks and fiction, making Bangla a vehicle for reasoned non-fiction (1). Print and standardisation: Serampore Mission Press and vernacular printing spread standardised texts, fixed spelling and widened readership, so literature moved from manuscripts and oral performance to a mass printed culture (1). Judgement: the Renaissance turned Bangla from a poetic folk medium into a modern literary language able to carry science, reform and nationalism, laying the ground for Tagore (1).
