States of Matter & Particle Model

CORE
THEORY

Kinetic Particle Theory

All matter is made up of tiny particles in constant motion. In solids, particles are held tightly together in a regular lattice, only vibrating. In liquids, particles are close but can slide past each other, allowing flow. In gases, particles are far apart and move randomly at high speeds.

When a solid is heated, its particles gain kinetic energy and vibrate more violently, breaking their rigid structure. This leads to melting. Further heating causes boiling, where particles escape entirely to form a gas.

DEFINITIONS

Key Terms

Brownian Motion: The random, zigzag motion of visible particles (like smoke) caused by collisions with invisible, fast-moving air or liquid particles.

Absolute Zero: The lowest possible temperature (-273°C or 0 K) where particles have zero kinetic energy.

EXAM PRACTICE

Worked Example

Question 1 (Core)

Explain, in terms of particles, why a gas is easy to compress but a solid is not.

In a gas, particles are widely spaced with mostly empty space between them. When pressure is applied, the particles are pushed closer together.

In a solid, particles are already tightly packed in a rigid lattice with strong intermolecular forces. There is no empty space to compress, so it resists pressure.
