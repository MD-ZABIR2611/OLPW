Chapter Overview

What is it?

Qualitative analysis: how to identify unknown substances using flame tests, sodium hydroxide and ammonia precipitates for cations, specific tests for anions (carbonate, chloride, sulfate, nitrate), and the tests for every common gas.

Why It Matters

Identification of ions and gases is guaranteed marks on every exam paper - the tests and observations are exactly the same every year. Learn the tables and you bank the marks.

What You Will Learn
  • Flame tests for five metal ions
  • Precipitate tests with NaOH and NH₃ solution
  • Tests for carbonate, chloride, sulfate and nitrate
  • The six gas tests (H₂, O₂, CO₂, Cl₂, NH₃, SO₂)
  • Planning an unknown-salt analysis

03

Detailed Theory

01

Tests for Cations

Flame tests: clean a nichrome/platinum wire with concentrated HCl, dip it in the sample and hold it in a roaring blue Bunsen flame. Colours: Li⁺ crimson, Na⁺ intense yellow, K⁺ lilac, Ca²⁺ orange-red, Cu²⁺ blue-green.

Precipitate tests: add a few drops of sodium hydroxide solution (or ammonia solution) to the salt solution. Cu²⁺ gives a light BLUE precipitate; Fe²⁺ a light GREEN precipitate; Fe³⁺ a reddish-brown precipitate; Zn²⁺, Al³⁺ and Ca²⁺ give WHITE precipitates - distinguish them with EXCESS reagent: Zn(OH)₂ and Al(OH)₃ dissolve in excess NaOH, and only Zn²⁺'s precipitate dissolves in excess ammonia too; Ca²⁺'s does not dissolve in either.

02

Tests for Anions

  • Carbonate (CO₃²⁻): add dilute acid - effervescence; the gas turns limewater milky (it is CO₂).
  • Chloride (Cl⁻): acidify with dilute nitric acid, then add silver nitrate solution - a WHITE precipitate of AgCl.
  • Sulfate (SO₄²⁻): acidify with dilute hydrochloric acid, then add barium chloride solution - a WHITE precipitate of BaSO₄.
  • Nitrate (NO₃⁻): add aluminium foil (or Devarda's alloy) and sodium hydroxide, warm gently - ammonia gas released, turns damp red litmus blue.
03

Tests for Gases

  • Hydrogen: burning splint gives a squeaky 'pop'.
  • Oxygen: relights a glowing splint.
  • Carbon dioxide: turns limewater from clear to milky (white precipitate of calcium carbonate).
  • Chlorine: bleaches damp (moist) litmus paper white.
  • Ammonia: turns damp red litmus paper blue (the only basic gas).
  • Sulfur dioxide: turns acidified potassium manganate(VII) from purple to colourless (or dichromate orange to green) - it is a reducing agent.
04

Key Definitions

TermExam Definition
Qualitative AnalysisChemical tests used to identify WHICH ions or gases are present in a sample, not how much.
CationA positively charged ion, attracted to the cathode - identified by flame tests and precipitate colours.
AnionA negatively charged ion, attracted to the anode - identified with specific reagents such as silver nitrate or barium chloride.
PrecipitateAn insoluble solid formed when two solutions are mixed and their ions combine.
LimewaterCalcium hydroxide solution that turns milky with carbon dioxide - the test for CO₂ and carbonates.
Dichromate / Manganate TestOrange potassium dichromate or purple manganate(VII) changes colour when reduced by SO₂ - the test for sulfur dioxide.

Worked Examples

Case Study 01 / Intermediate

Identifying an Unknown Salt

Question: A colourless salt solution gives a white precipitate with dilute hydrochloric acid (no gas), and a white precipitate with acidified barium chloride solution. Identify the anion.

Effervescence with acid would show carbonate - there is none (1). The white precipitate with ACIDIFIED barium chloride is the definitive sulfate test (1). The anion is the SULFATE ion, SO₄²⁻ (1).

Case Study 02 / Advanced

Two White Precipitates

Question: Solutions X and Y both give white precipitates with NaOH. In excess NaOH, X's precipitate dissolves but Y's does not. In excess ammonia, Y's precipitate still does not dissolve. Identify X and Y.

X's white precipitate dissolves in excess NaOH → X contains Al³⁺ or Zn²⁺ (1). Y's does not dissolve in NaOH or NH₃ → Y contains Ca²⁺ (1). To separate Al³⁺ and Zn²⁺, use excess NH₃: Zn²⁺ dissolves, Al³⁺ does not (1).

08

Laboratory Protocol

Experiment 01 / Full Analysis

Unknown Salt: Step-by-Step

1. Flame test on the solid - identifies the cation.

2. Add dilute acid - effervescence + limewater milky means carbonate.

3. Acidify a fresh sample with HNO₃, add AgNO₃ - white ppt means chloride.

4. Acidify with HCl, add BaCl₂ - white ppt means sulfate.

5. Add Al foil + NaOH, warm - ammonia smell / red litmus blue means nitrate.

Tip: always acidify BEFORE silver nitrate and barium chloride, or carbonate ions would give false white precipitates too.

09

Error Database

Wrong

Chlorine turns litmus blue then bleaches it.

Correct

Chlorine BLEACHES damp litmus white (it may flash red first as it dissolves to form acid, but the exam answer is bleaching).

Wrong

Ammonia is tested with limewater.

Correct

Ammonia turns damp RED litmus BLUE - the only common basic gas. Limewater is the test for CO₂.

Wrong

Forgetting to acidify before adding barium chloride.

Correct

Without acidifying, carbonate ions also give a white precipitate with Ba²⁺ - the acid destroys carbonate first so only sulfate shows.

Exam Simulation

Basic

1. Describe a chemical test for hydrogen gas and state the positive result.

Mark Scheme Answer [2 Marks]

Test: hold a lighted splint at the mouth of the tube (1). Positive result: a squeaky 'pop' sound (1).

Advanced

2. Describe how you would test an unknown solution for sulfate ions and for nitrate ions, giving the positive observations.

Mark Scheme Answer [5 Marks]

Sulfate: acidify with dilute HCl, then add barium chloride solution (1); white precipitate of BaSO₄ is positive (1). Nitrate: add aluminium foil and sodium hydroxide, warm gently (1); test the gas with damp red litmus (1); litmus turns blue (ammonia released) is positive (1).
