Energy, Work & Power

CORE EXTENDED
THEORY

Energy Stores & Transfers

Energy cannot be created or destroyed; it can only be transferred from one store to another (Conservation of Energy). Common energy stores include kinetic, gravitational potential, elastic, chemical, and thermal. When a force moves an object, work is done.

Power is the rate at which energy is transferred or work is done. No machine is 100% efficient; some energy is always dissipated as thermal energy (heat) due to friction or air resistance.

DEFINITIONS

Key Terms

Work Done: Energy transferred when a force moves an object, measured in Joules (J).

Power: The rate of doing work or transferring energy, measured in Watts (W).

Efficiency: The ratio of useful energy output to total energy input.

FORMULAE & CALCULATIONS

Core Equations

work done (W) = force (F) × distance (d)
kinetic energy (E_k) = ½ × mass (m) × velocity² (v²)
power (P) = work done (W) / time (t)

EXAM PRACTICE

Worked Example

Question 1 (Extended)

A motor lifts a 5 kg mass vertically upwards by 2 meters in 4 seconds. Calculate the power output of the motor. (Assume g = 10 N/kg).

Force (Weight): F = mg = 5 kg × 10 N/kg = 50 N

Work Done: W = F × d = 50 N × 2 m = 100 J

Power: P = W / t = 100 J / 4 s

Answer: P = 25 W
