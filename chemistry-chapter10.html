Chapter Overview

What is it?

This chapter details the reactivity series of metals, how metals are extracted from their ores (carbon reduction vs. electrolysis), and the properties of alloys. It also covers the causes and prevention of rusting.

Why It Matters

Metals are the backbone of modern infrastructure. Understanding how to extract and protect them is crucial for construction, transport, and manufacturing industries worldwide.

What You Will Learn
  • The reactivity series
  • Metal extraction (Iron, Aluminum)
  • Properties of alloys (Steel, Brass)
  • Rusting of iron (conditions & prevention)
  • Reaction of metals with acids/water

03

Detailed Theory

01

The Reactivity Series

Definition: A list of metals arranged in order of their reactivity, from most reactive to least reactive.

Order: Potassium, Sodium, Calcium, Magnesium, Aluminum, (Carbon), Zinc, Iron, Tin, Lead, (Hydrogen), Copper, Silver, Gold, Platinum.

Why it matters: A more reactive metal will displace a less reactive metal from its compound. It also dictates how the metal is extracted.

02

Extraction of Metals

Metal above Carbon: Extracted by electrolysis of their molten ores (e.g., Aluminum from Al₂O₃). This requires massive amounts of electricity to split the ionic compound.

Metal below Carbon: Extracted by carbon reduction (heating with carbon) in a blast furnace (e.g., Iron from Fe₂O₃). Carbon is more reactive, so it steals the oxygen.

03

Alloys & Rusting

Alloy: A mixture of a metal with other elements (metals or non-metals) to make it harder and more useful. (e.g., Iron + Carbon = Steel, Copper + Zinc = Brass).

Rusting: Iron rusts when exposed to both oxygen and water. Rusting is accelerated by salt and acid.

Prevention: Galvanizing (coating with zinc), painting, oiling, or sacrificial protection (attaching a more reactive metal like magnesium).

Key Definitions

TermExam Definition

Worked Examples

Case Study 02 / Intermediate

Metal Displacement

Question: Will iron displace copper from copper(II) sulfate solution? Write the equation and state the observation.

1. Iron is above copper in the reactivity series.

2. Equation: Fe + CuSO4 $\rightarrow$ FeSO4 + Cu

3. Observation: Blue color fades, brown copper coats the iron.

Answer: Yes, iron displaces copper. Solution turns pale green (Fe2+).

09

Laboratory Protocol

Experiment 01 / Rusting Conditions

Testing Iron Nails

Method: Place nails in 3 test tubes: Tube A (water + air), Tube B (boiled water + oil layer, no air), Tube C (anhydrous calcium chloride, no water).

Exam Application: Examiners ask why Tube B is boiled. Answer: To remove dissolved oxygen, so the nail only lacks air. The oil prevents oxygen from re-entering.

11

Error Database

Wrong

Aluminum is unreactive because it doesn't corrode easily.

Correct

Aluminum is highly reactive, but it forms a protective, unreactive layer of aluminum oxide on its surface, preventing further corrosion.

Wrong

Alloys are weaker than pure metals.

Correct

Alloys are harder and stronger than pure metals. The different sized atoms disrupt the regular lattice, preventing layers from sliding over each other.

Exam Simulation

Basic

1. State two conditions necessary for the rusting of iron.

Mark Scheme Answer [2 Marks]

Oxygen (air) and Water.

Advanced

2. Explain why iron is extracted using carbon reduction, but aluminum must be extracted using electrolysis.

Mark Scheme Answer [3 Marks]

Iron is below carbon in the reactivity series, so carbon can reduce (remove oxygen from) its ore. Aluminum is above carbon in the series, so carbon is not reactive enough to displace it; electrolysis is required.
