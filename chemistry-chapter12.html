Chapter Overview

What is it?

This chapter covers the extraction of sulfur, the properties of sulfur dioxide, and the industrial manufacture of sulfuric acid via the Contact Process. It also details the properties and uses of sulfuric acid.

Why It Matters

Sulfuric acid is one of the most important industrial chemicals in the world, used to make fertilizers, detergents, and paints. Understanding its production is essential for industrial chemistry.

What You Will Learn
  • Properties of sulfur dioxide
  • The Contact process (catalyst, conditions)
  • Properties of sulfuric acid (acids, oxidizing, dehydrating)
  • Uses of sulfuric acid
  • Acid rain formation

Detailed Theory

01

Sulfur Dioxide (SO₂)

Properties: A toxic, colorless gas with a pungent, choking smell. It is soluble in water, forming sulfurous acid (H₂SO₃).

Uses: Used as a bleaching agent for wood pulp (paper) and as a food preservative (kills bacteria). However, the bleaching is temporary and color returns due to oxidation by air.

02

The Contact Process

Definition: The industrial process used to manufacture sulfuric acid.

  • Step 1: Burn sulfur in air to form sulfur dioxide (S + O₂ → SO₂).
  • Step 2: React SO₂ with oxygen to form sulfur trioxide (2SO₂ + O₂ ⇌ 2SO₃). This is reversible.
  • Step 3: SO₃ is dissolved in concentrated H₂SO₄ to form oleum (H₂S₂O₇), which is then diluted with water to form H₂SO₄.

Conditions: Catalyst = Vanadium(V) oxide (V₂O₅). Temperature = 450°C. Pressure = 1-2 atm. (The temperature is a compromise: low temp favors yield but is too slow; high temp is fast but reduces yield).

03

Properties of Conc. H₂SO₄

Acid: Reacts with metals, bases, carbonates.

Oxidizing: Concentrated H₂SO₄ oxidizes metals like copper (which don't normally react with acids) to form sulfur dioxide.

Dehydrating: Removes water from compounds. E.g., turns blue copper(II) sulfate crystals white, and chars sugar/wood (removes H and O, leaving carbon).

Key Definitions

TermExam Definition

DehydrationThe removal of water (or the elements of water) from a compound.
OleumFuming sulfuric acid (H₂S₂O₇), formed by dissolving sulfur trioxide in concentrated sulfuric acid.

Worked Examples

Case Study 02 / Intermediate

Explain Conditions

Question: Explain why a temperature of 450°C is used in the Contact Process, despite the reaction being exothermic.

1. The forward reaction is exothermic, so a lower temperature would favor the yield of SO₃.

2. However, at a low temperature, the rate of reaction is too slow to be profitable.

3. 450°C is a compromise, providing an acceptable yield at a sufficiently fast rate.

Answer: It balances yield and rate of reaction.

09

Laboratory Protocol

Experiment 01 / Dehydration of Sugar

Testing Conc. H₂SO₄

Method: Add concentrated sulfuric acid to a beaker of sugar (sucrose).

Observation: The white sugar turns black and expands into a solid, porous column (like a snake). Steam is evolved.

Exam Application: Examiners ask why it turns black. Answer: Conc. H₂SO₄ is a dehydrating agent. It removes water (H and O) from sucrose, leaving pure black carbon behind.

11

Error Database

Wrong

SO₃ is dissolved directly in water to form H₂SO₄.

Correct

Dissolving SO₃ directly in water creates a dangerous, highly exothermic mist of acid that is hard to absorb. It is first dissolved in conc. H₂SO₄ to form oleum, then diluted.

Wrong

A higher temperature increases the yield in the Contact Process.

Correct

The forward reaction is exothermic. A higher temperature shifts equilibrium to the left (Le Chatelier), decreasing the yield of SO₃.

Mark Scheme Answer [2 Marks]
