Chapter 18 · Bangla

Précis, Expansion and Summary Skills

Précis and expansion are precision exercises. This chapter sets the rules of compression — keeping facts, dropping ornament — and of expansion, turning a seed proverb into a full composed paragraph.

Key Concepts

01

A précis (সারাংশ) reduces a passage to about one-third of its length in your own words while preserving every fact, the author's stance and the order of ideas — never adding opinion or examples of your own.

02

The method is three passes: underline topic sentences and facts, delete illustration, repetition, quotation and figures of speech, then rewrite connectedly in formal register with a suitable title (শিরোনাম).

03

Length discipline is assessed: examiners compare your word count to the demanded fraction, and a précis over or under by more than a few words loses a mark.

04

Expansion of a theme or proverb (বাক্য প্রসারণ) reverses the process: take a seed sentence such as "অতি ভক্তি গোপালে ধ্বংস" (excess devotion destroys), state its meaning, then develop with explanation, example and a concluding moral in one coherent paragraph.

05

In summarising literary passages (story or poem in prose form), keep plot causality — who did what, why, with what result — and drop descriptive atmosphere, since narrative logic carries the marks.

06

Both tasks are scored for register: contraction demands plain formal Bangla without proverbs; expansion demands elevated, connected prose — using the wrong register loses credit in either direction.

02

Key Terms

TermExam definition
PrécisA compression of a passage to a specified fraction of its length in the writer's own words, preserving all facts and the author's viewpoint.
সারাংশThe Bangla term for a summary or précis: a shortened restatement keeping facts and order while removing ornament.
শিরোনামThe title given to a précis or expansion, expressing its central idea in a few words.
Bakya prosaranThe expansion exercise: developing a seed sentence or proverb into a full paragraph with meaning, illustration and conclusion.
One-third ruleThe standard précis length requirement: the summary should be about one-third the word count of the original passage.
Register shiftChanging the level of language to suit a task, e.g. plain formal Bangla for précis versus elevated prose for expansion.
CompressionThe deletion of examples, repetition, figures of speech and qualifiers while retaining facts and logical order.
Moral conclusionThe closing generalisation, expected in an expansion, which states the lesson of the developed theme.
03

Exam Tip

Exam tip

Count words at the end, not the start: draft the précis freely, then trim or pad to the exact one-third figure, and read it against the original once — every fact in the original must have a counterpart in the précis, and every sentence in the précis must trace to the original, which is exactly how the examiner checks.

04

Practice Questions

Structured practice — write your answer, then reveal the model.

FOUNDATION

State three things that must be preserved in a précis and three things that should be removed.

Model answerPreserved: all facts and figures (1); the author's order of ideas and viewpoint (1); the main argument of each paragraph (1). Removed: examples and illustrations (1); repetition and figures of speech (1); quotations and digressions (1).
CHALLENGE

You are given the proverb "অতি ভক্তি গোপালে ধ্বংস" ("Excess devotion destroys") to expand into one paragraph. Explain how you would structure the expansion.

Model answerMeaning first: open by explaining that devotion carried beyond measure turns harmful, stating the proverb's sense in plain words (1). Development: give one concrete illustration — excessive fondness that ruins a child through indulgence, or zeal that exhausts the devotee — and connect it explicitly back to the theme (1). Conclusion: close with a balanced moral, e.g. that devotion and all virtues must be tempered with judgement, so excess in any good thing becomes its opposite (1). Register: elevated connected prose throughout, one paragraph, no list-like sentences (1).
