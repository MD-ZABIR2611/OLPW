Chapter Overview

What is it?

This chapter covers the foundational skills required to work safely and effectively in a chemistry laboratory. It details how to accurately measure physical properties, classify matter, and physically separate mixtures into their pure components based on particle size, solubility, and boiling points.

Why it Matters

Almost every chemical reaction and industrial process relies on these techniques. Without the ability to measure accurately, separate products from reactants, and assess purity, chemical analysis and synthesis would be impossible. It is a highly practical, exam-critical chapter.

What You Will Learn

  • Laboratory safety and apparatus selection.
  • Measurement of mass, volume, temperature, and time.
  • Accuracy, precision, and error analysis.
  • Classification of matter (pure substances vs mixtures).
  • Physical separation methods (filtration, distillation, chromatography).
  • Testing purity and interpreting experimental data.

03

Detailed Theory

01

Laboratory Safety & Apparatus

Definition

Chemistry laboratories involve chemicals, heat, and glassware. Safety is the priority to prevent injury and inaccurate results.

Safety Rules

- Wear goggles and a lab coat.
- Never taste chemicals; waft gases gently.
- Add acid to water (never reverse).
- Point test tubes away from others when heating.

Common Apparatus

Beaker: Approximate volumes.
Measuring Cylinder: Accurate liquid volumes.
Pipette: Exact fixed volumes (e.g., 25.0 cm³).
Burette: Variable precise volumes for titrations.
Electronic Balance: Measures mass to 2+ decimal places.

Exam Connection

Exams often ask you to select the most appropriate apparatus for a specific precision level. (e.g., Use a pipette, not a beaker, for exact 25cm³ volumes).

02

Measuring Variables & Accuracy

Measurement Principles

Mass: Use electronic balance. Press 'tare' to zero with weighing paper.
Volume: Read the bottom of the meniscus at eye level to avoid parallax error.
Temperature: Bulb must be fully submerged, not touching glass bottom.

Accuracy vs Precision

Accuracy: How close a measurement is to the true value.
Precision: How close repeated measurements are to each other.

Data Types

Qualitative: Descriptions without numbers (e.g., "blue solution").
Quantitative: Measurements with numbers and units (e.g., "25.0 g").

03

Classification of Matter

Pure Substances

Elements: Made of one type of atom (e.g., Fe, O₂).
Compounds: Two or more elements chemically bonded in fixed proportions (e.g., H₂O, CO₂).
Pure substances have sharp, fixed melting/boiling points.

Mixtures

Two or more substances mixed together but not chemically bonded.
They do not have fixed proportions.
Mixtures melt/boil over a range of temperatures due to the different properties of their components.

04

Separation: Filtration & Crystallization

Filtration (Particle Size)

Separates an insoluble solid from a liquid (e.g., sand + water).

[ Residue ] --------------- | Filter Paper | --------------- |||||| [ Filtrate ]

Residue: Solid left on paper.
Filtrate: Liquid that passed through.

Evaporation & Crystallization (Solubility)

Evaporation: Separates soluble solid from liquid (e.g., salt + water). Heat until liquid boils off. Solid may decompose if heated strongly.

Crystallization: Heat solution until saturated (no more can dissolve). Allow to cool. Crystals form. Filter, wash with cold distilled water, and dry. Prevents decomposition.

05

Separation: Simple & Fractional Distillation

Simple Distillation (Boiling Point)

Separates a liquid from a solution (e.g., water from saltwater).

[ Solution ]---> [ Vapor ]---> [ Condenser ]---> [ Distillate ] (Heated) (Gas) (Cold Water) (Pure Liquid)

Condensate/Distillate: The pure liquid collected. Cold water enters condenser at the bottom, exits at the top.

Fractional Distillation (Boiling Point)

Separates multiple miscible liquids (e.g., ethanol + water).

Uses a fractionating column with glass beads. The liquid with the lowest boiling point boils first. Vapors of higher boiling point liquids condense on the beads and fall back. Used industrially to separate crude oil.

06

Separation: Paper Chromatography

Principles (Adsorption & Solubility)

Separates dissolved substances (e.g., dyes in ink).

Stationary Phase: The filter paper (does not move).
Mobile Phase: The solvent (moves up the paper).
Dyes separate based on solubility in the solvent and attraction to the paper.

Purity Testing

Pure substances produce a single spot. Mixtures produce multiple spots. Melting/boiling points can also test purity.

Diagram Protocol
| [ Solvent Front ] | | [ Dye Spot ] | [ Dye Spot ] ------|------- [ Baseline (PENCIL) ] [ Solvent Below Line ]

Baseline must be drawn in pencil—ink would dissolve in the solvent and ruin results.

PrecisionHow close repeated measurements are to each other.

ResidueThe solid left behind on the filter paper during filtration.
DistillateThe liquid collected after condensation during distillation.
Pure SubstanceA substance containing only one element or compound.
SolventThe liquid in which a solute dissolves to form a solution.
SoluteThe substance that dissolves in a solvent.

Condenser Water Flow

Cold water must enter at the BOTTOM and exit at the TOP. This ensures the condenser jacket is completely full of water and provides the best counter-current cooling effect.

Impurities & Boiling Points

Impurities lower the melting point and raise the boiling point of a substance, causing it to melt/boil over a range of temperatures rather than a sharp, fixed point.

06

Equations & Formulae

Retention Factor ($R_f$ value)
Rf = (Distance moved by substance) / (Distance moved by solvent)

Where:

Rf → Ratio (No units, always between 0 and 1)

Distance → Measured from the pencil baseline to the center of the dye spot

Solvent Front → Measured from the pencil baseline to the highest point the solvent reached

07

Worked Examples

CASE STUDY 01

BASIC
Question: A green food colouring is separated. The solvent travels 12.0 cm. A yellow dye travels 6.0 cm. Calculate the $R_f$ value.
Think: Identify formula. $R_f$ = Substance distance / Solvent distance.
Answer: $R_f$ = 6.0 / 12.0 = 0.5
Mark-Scheme Points:
✓ Correct substitution (6.0/12.0) — 1 mark
✓ Correct answer (0.5) with no units — 1 mark

CASE STUDY 02

INTERMEDIATE
Question: Unknown dye X travels 4.8 cm. Solvent travels 8.0 cm. Reference table shows Substance A ($R_f$ 0.60) and Substance B ($R_f$ 0.55). Identify X.
Think: Calculate $R_f$ of X first. 4.8 / 8.0 = 0.60. Compare to table.
Answer: Dye X is Substance A, because its $R_f$ value (0.60) matches exactly.
Mark-Scheme Points:
✓ Calculates 0.60 — 1 mark
✓ Identifies Substance A based on matching $R_f$ — 1 mark

CASE STUDY 03

ADVANCED
Question: A student heats liquid paraffin. Temp rises 40°C to 75°C (min 2-5). Stays at 75°C (min 5-8). Rises again at min 9. Is it pure? Explain.
Think: Pure substances boil at a single, fixed temperature. The energy goes into changing state, not raising temp.
Answer: Yes, the paraffin is pure. It boiled at a single, fixed temperature (75°C) and the temperature remained constant while it was changing state.
Mark-Scheme Points:
✓ States "Yes" / "Pure" — 1 mark
✓ Mentions temperature remained constant at 75°C — 1 mark
✓ Links constant temp to changing state / pure substance — 1 mark
08

Chemistry Case Studies

CASE STUDY 01

Simple Distillation

Process: Saltwater → Water + Salt

Conditions: Heat applied, condenser cold.

Observation: Bubbles form in flask. Liquid drips into receiver. White solid remains.

Explanation: Water (b.p. 100°C) boils into vapor, leaving salt behind. Vapor is cooled in condenser back to liquid (distillate).

CASE STUDY 02

Fractional Distillation

Process: Ethanol + Water → Ethanol, then Water

Conditions: Heat, fractionating column with glass beads.

Observation: Thermometer reads 78°C. Ethanol drips first. Temp rises to 100°C later.

Explanation: Ethanol has lower boiling point (78°C). It boils first. Water vapor condenses on the cooler beads and falls back.

09

Laboratory Protocol

EXPERIMENT 01

Paper Chromatography of Ink


Objective: Separate and identify the dyes in a black ink mixture.

Apparatus: Beaker with lid, filter paper, solvent (water), capillary tube, pencil, ruler.

Method:
1. Draw pencil baseline 1cm from bottom.
2. Spot ink on baseline, let dry.
3. Add solvent (depth < 1cm).
4. Suspend paper, cover with lid.
5. Remove when solvent nears top. Mark solvent front.

Variables:
Independent: Type of dye.
Dependent: Distance moved ($R_f$).

Safety: Some solvents are flammable/toxic. Keep away from flames, use fume cupboard if necessary.

Errors:
- Dye spot below solvent level (dissolves into beaker).
- Solvent evaporates from paper edges.

Improvements: Use a lid to saturate atmosphere. Use longer paper for better separation.

Exam Protocol

State

Meaning: Give a fact without explanation.

Write: "Distillate" or "Filtrate"

Mistake: Writing a full paragraph for 1 mark.

Describe

Meaning: Say what happens/what you see.

Write: "Three separate colored spots form at different heights."

Mistake: Explaining *why* it happens when not asked.

Explain

Meaning: Give the scientific reason.

Write: "Dyes separate due to different solubilities in the solvent."

Mistake: Just describing what happens without the "because".

11

Error Database

Wrong

The residue is the liquid because it "resides" in the beaker.

Correct

Residue is the solid left on the filter paper. Filtrate is the liquid that flowed through.

Wrong

Drawing the chromatography baseline with a pen.

Correct

Must use pencil. Pen ink is a dye that will dissolve in the solvent and ruin the chromatogram.

Wrong

Water enters the condenser at the top and exits at the bottom.

Correct

Water enters at the bottom, exits at the top. This ensures the jacket is full and cooling is efficient.

12

Rapid Revision

Separation Rules

  • Insoluble solid + liquid $\rightarrow$ Filtration
  • Soluble solid + liquid (decomposes) $\rightarrow$ Crystallization
  • Soluble solid + liquid (stable) $\rightarrow$ Evaporation
  • Liquid + dissolved solid $\rightarrow$ Simple Distillation
  • Miscible liquids $\rightarrow$ Fractional Distillation
  • Immiscible liquids $\rightarrow$ Separating Funnel
  • Multiple dissolved dyes $\rightarrow$ Chromatography

Key Facts

  • $R_f$ = Distance of substance / Distance of solvent
  • Pencil baseline, never ink.
  • Water in condenser: Bottom to Top.
  • Impurities lower m.p., raise b.p., cause a range.
  • Pure substances have sharp, fixed m.p/b.p.

Exam Simulation

Basic 2 Marks
1. Which method is best used to separate sand from water? Define the term filtrate.
Mark Scheme

Method: Filtration (1 mark)

Filtrate: The liquid that passes through the filter paper during filtration. (1 mark)

Intermediate 4 Marks
2. A student uses chromatography to separate black ink. (a) State why the baseline must be in pencil. (b) Solvent moves 8.0 cm, red dye moves 2.0 cm. Calculate $R_f$. (c) Define mobile phase.
Mark Scheme

(a) Ink would dissolve in the solvent / pencil is insoluble. (1 mark)

(b) $R_f$ = 2.0 / 8.0 = 0.25 (no units). (2 marks)

(c) The solvent (the liquid that moves up the paper carrying the dyes). (1 mark)

Advanced 6 Marks
3. A mixture contains propanone (b.p. 56°C), ethanol (b.p. 78°C), and water (b.p. 100°C). (a) Name the separation technique. (b) Explain how propanone is separated from the other two. (c) Explain what happens to the temperature while propanone is boiling.
Mark Scheme

(a) Fractional distillation. (1 mark)

(b) Mixture heated. Propanone boils first (lowest b.p). Ethanol/water vapors condense on glass beads and fall back. Only propanone vapor reaches top. (3 marks)

(c) Temperature remains constant at 56°C. Heat energy is used to overcome intermolecular forces/change state, not increase kinetic energy. (2 marks)

Mark Scheme Analysis

Common Mark Scheme Points


  • When testing purity: "Measure melting/boiling point and compare to known true value."
  • Condensation explanation: "Gas loses heat energy" + "particles slow down/come closer together."
  • Fractional distillation: Always mention "the substance with the lowest boiling point boils/evaporates first."
  • Chromatography separation: "Different solubilities in the solvent" AND "different attractions to the paper."
