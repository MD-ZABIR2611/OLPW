Chapter Overview

What is it?

This chapter covers foundational lab skills: measuring physical properties, classifying matter, and physically separating mixtures based on particle size, solubility, and boiling points.

Why It Matters

Without the ability to measure accurately, separate products from reactants, and assess purity, chemical analysis and synthesis would be impossible.

03

Detailed Theory

01

Measuring Variables & Accuracy

Accuracy: How close a measurement is to the true value. Precision: How close repeated measurements are to each other.

To avoid parallax error when reading a meniscus, your eye must be level with the liquid surface.

02

Classification of Matter

Pure Substances: Elements (one type of atom) and Compounds (two or more chemically bonded). They have sharp, fixed melting/boiling points.

Mixtures: Substances mixed but not chemically bonded. They melt/boil over a range of temperatures.

03

Separation Techniques

  • Filtration: Separates insoluble solid from liquid (Residue = solid, Filtrate = liquid).
  • Crystallization: Separates soluble solid from liquid. Heat until saturated, cool to form crystals. Prevents decomposition.
  • Simple Distillation: Separates liquid from solution (Condenser water goes in at the bottom, out at the top).
  • Fractional Distillation: Separates miscible liquids (e.g., ethanol + water). Uses a fractionating column.
  • Chromatography: Separates dissolved dyes. $R_f$ value = distance moved by substance / distance moved by solvent.
04

Key Definitions

TermExam Definition
FiltrateThe liquid that passes through the filter paper during filtration.
ResidueThe solid left behind on the filter paper during filtration.
Pure SubstanceA substance containing only one element or compound, with fixed melting/boiling points.
Stationary PhaseThe substance that stays fixed in place during chromatography (e.g., paper).
06

Equations & Formulae

Retention Factor ($R_f$ value)
Rf = (Distance moved by substance) / (Distance moved by solvent)
09

Laboratory Protocol

Experiment 01 / Paper Chromatography

Separating Ink Dyes

Method: Draw pencil baseline 1cm from bottom. Spot ink. Add solvent (depth < 1cm). Cover with lid.

Errors: Dye spot below solvent level (dissolves into beaker). Baseline drawn in ink (ink dissolves and ruins results).

Exam Application: Examiners ask why the baseline must be in pencil. Answer: Ink is a dye that would dissolve in the solvent and ruin the chromatogram.

11

Error Database

Wrong

The residue is the liquid because it "resides" in the beaker.

Correct

Residue is the solid left on the filter paper. Filtrate is the liquid that flowed through.

Wrong

Water enters the condenser at the top and exits at the bottom.

Correct

Water enters at the bottom, exits at the top. This ensures the jacket is full and cooling is efficient.

Exam Simulation

Basic

1. Which method is best used to separate sand from water? Define the term filtrate.

Mark Scheme Answer [2 Marks]

Method: Filtration. Filtrate: The liquid that passes through the filter paper.

Intermediate

2. Solvent moves 8.0 cm, red dye moves 2.0 cm. Calculate $R_f$.

Mark Scheme Answer [2 Marks]

$R_f$ = 2.0 / 8.0 = 0.25 (no units).
