Biological Molecules

CORE
SECTION 01 — CHAPTER OVERVIEW

The Building Blocks of Life

Living organisms are made up of complex molecules built from simpler units. This chapter covers the main biological molecules: carbohydrates, proteins, and lipids (fats and oils). It also details the chemical tests used in the laboratory to identify them.

SECTION 02 — LEARNING OBJECTIVES

Chapter Goals

  • Identify the monomers and polymers of carbohydrates, proteins, and lipids.
  • State the roles of these molecules in living organisms.
  • Describe the chemical tests for reducing sugars, starch, protein, and lipids.
  • Interpret positive and negative test results.
SECTION 03 — CORE THEORY

Macromolecules & Food Tests

Carbohydrates

Carbohydrates are the main energy source. They are made of simple sugars (monosaccharides) like glucose. Complex carbohydrates (polysaccharides) like starch (plants) and glycogen (animals) are storage molecules. Cellulose is a structural carbohydrate in plant cell walls.

Proteins

Proteins are structural molecules and enzymes. They are made of long chains of amino acids folded into specific 3D shapes.

Lipids

Lipids (fats and oils) are used for long-term energy storage, insulation, and making cell membranes. They are made of one molecule of glycerol attached to three fatty acid molecules.

Food Tests Summary

Benedict's test (Reducing Sugars): Add Benedict's reagent, heat in a water bath. Positive = Orange/Brown. Negative = Blue.

Iodine test (Starch): Add iodine solution. Positive = Blue/Black. Negative = Orange/Brown.

Biuret test (Protein): Add Biuret solution (NaOH + CuSO4). Positive = Purple/Violet. Negative = Blue.

Ethanol Emulsion test (Lipids): Mix with ethanol, then add water. Positive = Cloudy white emulsion. Negative = Clear.

SECTION 04 — KEY DEFINITIONS

Examination Definitions

MONOMER: A small, single molecule that can join with others to form a polymer.

POLYMER: A large molecule made up of many repeating monomer units.

SECTION 06 — PRACTICAL LAB

Testing Unknown Food Samples

SAFETY

Wear safety goggles. The Biuret test uses sodium hydroxide, which is caustic. Benedict's test requires heating in a water bath—do not heat directly with a Bunsen burner as it can boil over.

IMPROVEMENTS

Always use a clean pipette for each test to prevent cross-contamination. Use distilled water as a negative control to compare colours.

SECTION 07 — EXAM TECHNIQUE

Describing Tests

For 3 marks, you must state the reagent, the conditions, and the colour change.

Example: Add iodine solution (1 mark), no heating required (1 mark), colour changes from orange/brown to blue/black if starch is present (1 mark).

SECTION 08 — COMMON MISTAKES

Avoid These Errors

Mistake: Saying Benedict's tests for starch.

Correction: Benedict's tests for reducing sugars. Iodine tests for starch.

Mistake: Forgetting to mention heating for the Benedict's test.

SECTION 09 — QUICK REVISION

Fast Facts

  • Carbohydrates: Monomers are simple sugars (glucose). Polymer is starch/glycogen.
  • Proteins: Monomers are amino acids. Folded into specific 3D shapes.
  • Starch + Iodine = Blue/Black.
  • Reducing Sugar + Benedict's (heated) = Orange/Brown.
SECTION 10 — EXAM QUESTIONS

Test Your Knowledge

Question 1 (Standard)

Describe how you would test a food sample for the presence of protein.

3 marks: Add Biuret solution (sodium hydroxide and copper sulfate). Mix gently. If protein is present, the colour changes from blue to purple/violet.
