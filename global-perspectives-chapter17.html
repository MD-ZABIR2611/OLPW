Writing a High-Level Individual Report

Top-band reports are structured arguments, not collections of facts. Analysis, evaluation and a decisive conclusion are what the assessment objectives reward.

08Key terms

01

Key Concepts

01

A high-level individual report is structured: an introduction stating the question and context, a series of analysis sections, evaluation of evidence and perspectives, and a conclusion with a bibliography.

02

Analysis (AO2) goes beyond description: it breaks the issue into factors, explains causes and effects, and links each point back to the research question.

03

Evaluation weighs the strength of evidence and viewpoints — noting reliability, bias and limitations — and decides which side of the argument is stronger.

04

The conclusion must answer the research question directly, justify the judgement with the strongest evidence and may end with recommendations for action.

05

Signposting phrases guide the reader through the argument, and citations connect every claim to a source; examiners reward both explicitly.

06

Effective reports consider local and global perspectives and different stakeholders, presenting counterarguments and answering them rather than hiding them.

02

Key Terms

TermExam definition
AnalysisBreaking an issue down into its parts and explaining how they relate, going beyond simply describing facts.
EvaluationWeighing evidence and different viewpoints to reach a supported judgement about which is strongest.
Reasoned judgementA conclusion reached by weighing evidence and arguments, and justified by explaining why one side is stronger.
CounterargumentAn argument that opposes the position being put forward, which a strong report considers and answers.
ConclusionThe final section of a report that answers the research question directly and justifies the writer's overall judgement.
RecommendationA proposal for action, based on the conclusion of a report, suggesting what should be done about the issue.
CitationA reference in the text of a report that links a claim to its source, usually giving the author and year.
SignpostingPhrases that guide the reader through a report by indicating what each section will do and how it links to the argument.
03

Exam Tip

Exam tip

Command words matter: describe needs facts, explain needs a because, evaluate needs a supported judgement. A conclusion that actually answers the research question is the single biggest difference between the middle and top bands.

04

Practice Questions

Structured practice — write your answer, then reveal the model.

FOUNDATION

Outline the structure of a high-scoring individual report, naming four sections in order and stating what each one does.

Model answerIntroduction: presents the research question and why the issue matters (1). Analysis: breaks the issue into factors and explains them with evidence (1). Evaluation: weighs evidence, perspectives and counterarguments to judge which is strongest (1). Conclusion: answers the research question directly with a justified judgement, followed by a bibliography (1).
CHALLENGE

"A top-level individual report describes both sides of the issue equally and then stops." Evaluate this statement.

Model answerDescription alone scores low marks: analysis must explain causes, effects and the links between factors (1). Presenting both sides equally without a decision is evaluation without judgement, while AO2 requires a supported conclusion (1). The strongest evidence and viewpoints must be weighed so the report can answer its question (1). The conclusion should state and justify an overall judgement, and may recommend action (1). Judgement: the statement is wrong — describing both sides is necessary but not sufficient, and the deciding conclusion is what earns the highest marks (1).
