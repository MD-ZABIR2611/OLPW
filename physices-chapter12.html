Melting, Boiling & Evaporation

CORE
THEORY

Changes of State

Melting occurs when a solid turns into a liquid. During melting, thermal energy is absorbed but the temperature remains constant until all the solid has melted. This energy is used to break the bonds holding the particles in their lattice.

Boiling is a specific type of evaporation that happens at a fixed temperature throughout the liquid. Evaporation, however, occurs at any temperature, but only at the surface of the liquid.

DEFINITIONS

Boiling vs Evaporation

Boiling: A rapid change of state from liquid to gas that occurs at a specific boiling point, happening throughout the liquid and forming bubbles.

Evaporation: A slower change of state from liquid to gas that happens at any temperature, occurring only at the surface of the liquid.

Evaporation causes a cooling effect because the fastest-moving particles escape the liquid, lowering the average kinetic energy of the remaining particles.

EXAM PRACTICE

Worked Example

Question 1 (Core)

Explain why sweating cools the human body.

Sweat is a liquid on the skin. It evaporates at body temperature.

The fastest-moving water particles in the sweat escape into the air.

This reduces the average kinetic energy of the remaining particles, lowering the temperature of the liquid, which cools the skin.
