Accommodation & Hospitality Operations

Accommodation is where tourism becomes hospitality. Understanding room types, ratings, revenue measures and seasonality explains how lodging businesses stay profitable.

Key Concepts

01

Serviced accommodation such as hotels and guest houses provides meals and cleaning for guests, while non-serviced accommodation such as apartments, villas and campsites lets guests self-cater, usually at lower cost.

02

Official star ratings grade hotels from one to five stars on facilities and service, helping customers compare properties and setting price expectations.

03

Hotels earn most of their revenue from rooms; the key measures are occupancy rate (rooms sold ÷ rooms available), average daily rate (room revenue ÷ rooms sold) and RevPAR, which multiplies the two together.

04

The front office, housekeeping, food and beverage and maintenance departments must coordinate so guests receive a seamless stay, because the real product is the experience.

05

Many hotels belong to chains that operate by franchise or management contract, gaining global branding and booking systems while owners provide the capital.

06

Seasonality is the industry's central problem: occupancy swings between peak and off-season, so pricing, events and targeting new segments are used to level demand.

02

Key Terms

TermExam definition
Serviced accommodationAccommodation such as a hotel where services including cleaning and often meals are provided for guests during their stay.
Non-serviced accommodationSelf-catering accommodation such as apartments, villas and campsites where guests look after themselves.
Star ratingAn official classification, usually from one to five stars, that grades a hotel's facilities, services and quality.
Occupancy rateThe percentage of available rooms that are sold over a period, calculated as rooms sold divided by rooms available.
Average daily rateThe average room revenue earned by a hotel for each room actually sold, found by dividing room revenue by rooms sold.
RevPARRevenue per available room, a hotel performance measure found by multiplying average daily rate by occupancy rate.
FranchiseA business arrangement in which an independent hotel trades under a brand's name and systems in return for fees and a share of revenue.
All-inclusiveA holiday price that covers accommodation, meals, drinks and many activities, so guests spend little extra at the resort.
03

Exam Tip

Exam tip

Learn the three accommodation measures and always show your working: occupancy rate, average daily rate and RevPAR. Questions often give two of the figures and expect you to calculate the third.

04

Practice Questions

Structured practice — write your answer, then reveal the model.

FOUNDATION

A hotel has 200 rooms, sells 150 rooms on a given night, and earns $18,000 in room revenue. Calculate the occupancy rate and the average daily rate, showing your working.

Model answerOccupancy rate = rooms sold ÷ rooms available = 150 ÷ 200 (1) = 75% (1). Average daily rate = room revenue ÷ rooms sold = $18,000 ÷ 150 (1) = $120 (1).
CHALLENGE

A beach resort hotel suffers very low occupancy in winter. Evaluate three strategies the manager could use to raise winter revenue, and recommend the best.

Model answerCut prices or offer off-season packages to attract price-sensitive travellers and groups (1). Target year-round segments such as conferences, sports teams or wellness guests (1). Close and refurbish part of the hotel in the quietest months to cut costs and improve the product (1). Each strategy has costs: discounting lowers margins, new segments need marketing spend, and partial closure still carries fixed costs (1). Judgement: attracting conference and wellness business is the strongest option because it adds demand without eroding the hotel's price (1).
