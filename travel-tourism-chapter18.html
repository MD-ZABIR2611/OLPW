Niche & Specialised Tourism

Beyond the mass market lies a world of specialised niches — ecotourism, adventure, wellness and dark tourism — each with its own customers, ethics and sustainability questions.

Key Concepts

01

Niche tourism serves small, specialised segments defined by interest — ecotourism, adventure, wellness, culture — rather than the undifferentiated mass market.

02

Ecotourism, defined by the International Ecotourism Society, is responsible travel to natural areas that conserves the environment and improves the welfare of local people; Costa Rica's national parks show how visitor income can fund conservation.

03

Adventure tourism sells challenge and managed risk — trekking, rafting, climbing — and depends on qualified guides, safety standards and insurance.

04

Health tourism splits into wellness tourism, such as spa and fitness retreats, and medical tourism, where travellers go abroad for cheaper or faster treatment such as dental work.

05

Dark tourism means visiting places linked to death, suffering or disaster — Auschwitz, Pompeii, Ground Zero — where demand mixes education and memorial with real ethical concerns.

06

Case-study answers name the place, give facts and figures, identify the stakeholders and weigh sustainability: niche tourism earns higher spend per visitor with lower impact, but volumes are small and success depends on marketing to the right audience.

02

Key Terms

TermExam definition
Niche tourismSpecialised tourism that serves the particular interests of small, clearly defined market segments rather than mass tourists.
EcotourismResponsible travel to natural areas that conserves the environment and sustains the well-being of local people.
Adventure tourismTourism based on physically challenging activities in natural settings, such as trekking, climbing or white-water rafting.
Medical tourismTravelling to another country to receive medical treatment, usually because it is cheaper or available sooner than at home.
Wellness tourismTravel whose main purpose is to improve health and wellbeing through spas, therapies and healthy activities.
Dark tourismVisiting places associated with death, suffering or disaster, such as battlefields, memorials and disaster sites.
Community-based tourismTourism owned and managed by local communities so that income and decisions remain in the destination.
Responsible tourismTourism that minimises negative impacts on the environment and culture and maximises benefits for local people.
03

Exam Tip

Exam tip

Niche-tourism answers should read like mini case studies: name the type, define it, give a real example, and then weigh at least one benefit against one concern before concluding.

04

Practice Questions

Structured practice — write your answer, then reveal the model.

FOUNDATION

Define ecotourism and state three principles that an ecotourism business should follow.

Model answerEcotourism is responsible travel to natural areas that conserves the environment and improves the well-being of local people (1). It should limit visitor numbers to protect habitats (1). It should employ local people so the income stays in the community (1). It should educate visitors and support conservation, for example through park fees (1).
CHALLENGE

Evaluate the view that sites of dark tourism, such as Auschwitz or Ground Zero, should be open to paying visitors.

Model answerSupport: visits educate people about history and atrocities, keeping memory alive — remembrance is the stated mission of Auschwitz (1). Entrance fees and visitor spending fund the conservation of the sites and support local businesses (1). Concerns: tourism can commercialise suffering and becomes disrespectful when visitors behave inappropriately (1). Local communities may resent their home being associated with death and disaster (1). Judgement: such sites should remain open with managed visitor behaviour, small groups and respectful interpretation, because education and preservation outweigh the risks (1).
