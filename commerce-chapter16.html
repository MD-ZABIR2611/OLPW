E-commerce & Modern Trading Methods

E-commerce means trading over the internet, and together with teleshopping, vending machines and electronic payments it has transformed how goods and services reach consumers, bringing both advantages and risks.

Key Concepts

01

E-commerce is the buying and selling of goods and services over the internet; it is part of e-business, which covers all internet-based business processes such as marketing, ordering, payment and after-sales support.

02

Types of e-commerce include B2B (firm to firm, e.g. a manufacturer ordering parts online), B2C (firm to consumer, e.g. online retail), C2C (consumer to consumer through online marketplaces and auctions) and m-commerce through smartphones.

03

Benefits to consumers include 24-hour shopping, easy price comparison, a wider choice including foreign sellers, home delivery and customer reviews; benefits to firms include lower premises costs, a global market, automated stock control and targeted online advertising.

04

Drawbacks include card fraud, hacking and phishing, the impossibility of inspecting goods before purchase, delivery delays and costs, difficulties over returns and refunds, and the digital divide that excludes consumers without internet access.

05

Electronic payment methods include credit and debit cards, bank transfers, e-wallets such as PayPal, mobile money and QR-code payments; encryption and secure payment gateways protect transactions, and cash on delivery survives in some markets.

06

Other modern trading methods include teleshopping on television channels, automatic vending machines for drinks and tickets, self-service supermarkets, online banking and booking, social-media commerce and click-and-collect ordering.

02

Key Terms

TermExam definition
E-commerceThe buying and selling of goods and services over the internet.
E-businessThe use of internet technology for all business processes, including buying, selling, marketing and customer support.
Business-to-consumerE-commerce in which firms sell directly to individual customers through websites or apps.
Business-to-businessE-commerce in which firms trade with other firms, e.g. a retailer ordering stock online from a manufacturer.
Mobile commerceBuying and selling conducted through mobile phones or tablets, usually by apps or digital wallets.
Digital walletAn electronic account, such as PayPal or mobile money, that stores payment details and allows online or contactless payment.
Digital divideThe gap between people who have access to the internet and digital technology and those who do not.
Click and collectA retail method in which customers order online but collect the goods in person from a store.
03

Exam Tip

Exam tip

E-commerce questions are usually "discuss the advantages and disadvantages" — split the answer into benefits for consumers and benefits for businesses, then drawbacks such as security and delivery, and close with a judgement such as "overall, e-commerce will continue to grow because the benefits of convenience and lower prices outweigh the risks".

Practice Questions

Structured practice — write your answer, then reveal the model.

FOUNDATION

List three types of e-commerce and give an example of each.

Model answerBusiness-to-consumer (B2C): a customer buying clothes from a retailer's website (1). Business-to-business (B2B): a shop ordering stock from a manufacturer's online catalogue (1). Consumer-to-consumer (C2C): individuals selling to each other through an online auction or marketplace (1). Accept also mobile commerce (m-commerce), e.g. ordering a taxi through a smartphone app (1).
CHALLENGE

Evaluate the effects of the growth of e-commerce on traditional high-street retailers.

Model answerOnline sellers have lower costs, so they charge lower prices and draw customers away from shops, cutting high-street sales and profits (1). Customers gain convenience, wider choice and easy price comparison, so footfall falls further (1). High-street retailers can adapt by creating their own websites, offering click-and-collect, or providing personal service that online sellers cannot match (1). Some shops close, leaving empty premises and job losses in retailing, though new jobs arise in warehousing and delivery (1). Overall, retailers that combine online and physical selling are most likely to survive (1).
