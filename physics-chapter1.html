THEORY

Physical Quantities & SI Units

Physics is based on measurements. A physical quantity consists of a numerical magnitude and a unit. The Système International (SI) provides standard base units for fundamental measurements like length (metre, m), mass (kilogram, kg), time (second, s), and temperature (kelvin, K).

Derived units are combinations of base units, such as speed (m/s) or density (kg/m³). Measurements must be accurate (close to the true value) and precise (consistent when repeated).

DEFINITIONS

Scalars vs Vectors

Scalar Quantity: A physical quantity that has magnitude only (e.g., mass, distance, speed, time).

Vector Quantity: A physical quantity that has both magnitude and direction (e.g., weight, displacement, velocity, force).

FORMULAE & CALCULATIONS

Resultant Vectors

When two vectors act in the same direction, their resultant is the sum of their magnitudes. If they act in opposite directions, subtract the smaller from the larger.

Resultant = Vector₁ + Vector₂ (Same direction)
Resultant = |Vector₁ - Vector₂| (Opposite direction)
PRACTICAL WORK

Measuring Instruments

To measure length accurately, use a ruler for 1mm precision, vernier calipers for 0.1mm precision, and a micrometer screw gauge for 0.01mm precision. Always avoid parallax error by looking perpendicular to the scale.

EXAM PRACTICE

Test Your Knowledge

Question 1

A car travels 20 km North, then 5 km South. What is its displacement?

Formula: Resultant = North - South

Working: 20 km - 5 km = 15 km

Answer: 15 km North
