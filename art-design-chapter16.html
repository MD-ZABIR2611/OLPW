Developing a Personal Investigation

A personal investigation is a sustained enquiry: choosing a theme, researching artists, recording from primary sources and refining ideas through experiments into a resolved final outcome.

Key Concepts

01

A strong investigation starts from a theme the candidate cares about and narrows it into a focused line of enquiry that can sustain several weeks of recording, experimentation and development.

02

Primary recording — drawing and photographing from real objects, places and people — generates original source material that keeps the project personal and avoids dependence on second-hand internet imagery.

03

Researching relevant artists is not copying: analysing two or three practitioners' techniques, processes and ideas supplies strategies that the candidate tests, adapts and makes their own.

04

Development means a visible chain: each experiment should respond to the last, so annotating what worked, what failed and what to change next turns a folder of trials into a journey of ideas.

05

A resolved outcome must show selection and control — deciding scale, medium, composition and finish deliberately, with the supporting studies explaining how those decisions were reached.

06

The coursework assessment rewards the whole process of collecting, recording, experimenting, developing and realising, so every page should carry both visual work and concise annotation.

02

Key Terms

TermExam definition
Personal investigationA sustained, self-directed art project in which a candidate researches, records and develops ideas from a chosen theme to a final outcome.
Primary sourceOriginal material recorded directly by the artist, such as drawings, paintings or photographs made from real objects, people or places.
Secondary sourceExisting imagery by other artists or from photographs and print media, used as reference rather than direct observation.
Line of enquiryThe focused question or theme that guides a sustained investigation and keeps its development coherent from start to finish.
AnnotationConcise written notes alongside artwork that explain intentions, processes, decisions and links to artists or sources.
DevelopmentThe stage in which initial ideas are refined through experiments, feedback and changing materials towards a resolved outcome.
Resolved outcomeThe final, realised piece that demonstrates deliberate selection of composition, medium and technique, supported by the studies that led to it.
Artist analysisThe study of a practitioner's techniques, processes and ideas to extract strategies for the candidate's own work.
03

Exam Tip

Exam tip

Present the project as a journey: annotate every experiment with what you changed and why; the mark scheme rewards development of ideas over a folder of unconnected, finished-looking pieces.

04

Practice Questions

Structured practice — write your answer, then reveal the model.

FOUNDATION

List four stages of a sustained personal investigation, from first theme to final outcome.

Model answerAny four of: choosing and narrowing the theme into a line of enquiry (1); primary recording from observation (1); researching and analysing relevant artists (1); experimenting with media and ideas (1); developing and refining ideas in response to experiments (1); realising the final outcome (1).
CHALLENGE

Explain two reasons why primary recording is valued more highly than copied secondary images in a personal investigation.

Model answerPrimary recording produces original, personal source material that the candidate controls and can crop, exaggerate or combine, keeping the investigation individual (1). Copied secondary imagery fixes composition, viewpoint and lighting, so the outcome merely replicates another person's observation (1). Drawing from life develops the observational and recording skills that the assessment objectives reward (1). Primary sources also avoid copyright problems and can be revisited at different scales and angles to support development (1).
