Reproduction

CORE
SECTION 01 — CHAPTER OVERVIEW

Continuation of Life

Reproduction is the biological process by which new individual organisms are produced. This chapter covers the differences between asexual and sexual reproduction, the anatomy of both plant and human reproductive systems, and the menstrual cycle.

SECTION 02 — LEARNING OBJECTIVES

Chapter Goals

  • Compare asexual and sexual reproduction.
  • Label the parts of an insect-pollinated flower.
  • Label the male and female human reproductive systems.
  • Describe the menstrual cycle and the role of hormones.
  • Explain the role of the placenta.
SECTION 03 — CORE THEORY

Modes of Reproduction

Asexual vs Sexual

Asexual: One parent. No gametes. Genetically identical offspring (clones). Fast. E.g., bacteria, yeast, plants (runners).

Sexual: Two parents. Gametes (sperm/egg). Genetically varied offspring. Slower. E.g., mammals, flowering plants.

Plant Reproduction

Flowers are the reproductive organs of plants. Insect-pollinated flowers have large, bright petals and produce nectar to attract insects. Wind-pollinated flowers are small, dull, and produce huge amounts of pollen. Pollination is the transfer of pollen from anther to stigma. Fertilization occurs when the male gamete fuses with the female gamete in the ovule.

Human Reproduction

Fertilization occurs in the oviduct (fallopian tube) when a sperm nucleus fuses with an egg nucleus. The fertilized egg (zygote) implants in the uterus lining. The placenta acts as an exchange surface, allowing oxygen and nutrients to pass from mother to fetus, and CO2 and urea to pass from fetus to mother. Their blood does not mix.

SECTION 04 — KEY DEFINITIONS

Examination Definitions

POLLINATION: The transfer of pollen grains from the anther to the stigma.

FERTILISATION: The fusion of the male and female gamete nuclei to form a zygote.

GAMETE: A haploid sex cell (e.g., sperm or egg).

SECTION 06 — PRACTICAL LAB

Seed Germination

AIM

To investigate the conditions required for seed germination.

VARIABLES

Independent: Presence of water, oxygen, or temperature.

Dependent: Number of seeds that germinate.

CONCLUSION

Seeds require water (to activate enzymes), oxygen (for respiration), and a suitable temperature (for enzyme activity) to germinate. Light is generally not required.

SECTION 07 — EXAM TECHNIQUE

Placenta Explanations

When describing the placenta, always mention exchange. "The placenta has a large surface area and thin membrane to allow rapid diffusion of oxygen and nutrients from the mother's blood to the fetus."

SECTION 08 — COMMON MISTAKES

Avoid These Errors

Mistake: Saying the mother's blood and fetus's blood mix in the placenta.

Correction: They do not mix. Substances diffuse across a membrane between the two blood supplies.

Mistake: Confusing pollination with fertilisation.

SECTION 09 — QUICK REVISION

Fast Facts

  • Asexual = clones. Sexual = variation.
  • Wind-pollinated flowers have feathery stigmas to catch pollen in the air.
  • Fertilization occurs in the oviduct.
  • Placenta allows exchange of materials without blood mixing.
SECTION 10 — EXAM QUESTIONS

Test Your Knowledge

Question 1 (Standard)
