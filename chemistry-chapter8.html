Chapter Overview

What is it?

This chapter details the properties of acids and bases, the pH scale, and how they react to form salts. It explains how to choose the right method to prepare pure, soluble salts.

Why It Matters

Acid-base chemistry is everywhere. From stomach antacids to industrial fertilizer production, these reactions are vital. Understanding salt preparation is also a major practical exam component.

What You Will Learn
  • Acids, bases, and alkalis
  • Strong vs weak acids (pH)
  • Reactions of acids
  • Types of oxides

03

Detailed Theory

01

Acids, Bases & Alkalis

Acid: A substance that produces H+ ions when dissolved in water. pH < 7.

Base: A substance that neutralizes an acid. Alkali: A base that dissolves in water to produce OH- ions. pH > 7.

Strong vs Weak: Strong acids (e.g., HCl) ionize completely in water. Weak acids (e.g., ethanoic acid) only partially ionize.

02

Reactions of Acids

  • Acid + Metal → Salt + Hydrogen (e.g., Mg + 2HCl → MgCl2 + H2)
  • Acid + Base (Alkali) → Salt + Water (Neutralisation)
  • Acid + Carbonate → Salt + Water + Carbon Dioxide
03

Salt Preparation Methods

Method 1 (Acid + Insoluble Base/Carbonate): Add excess solid to warm acid. Filter to remove excess. Evaporate filtrate to crystallize.

Method 2 (Titration): Used for soluble salts formed from acid + alkali (e.g., NaCl). Uses a burette for exact volumes.

Method 3 (Acid + Metal): Not recommended for salts like FeCl2 (reaction is too slow) or KCl (metal is too reactive/dangerous).

07

Worked Examples

Case Study 02 / Intermediate

Salt Method Selection

Question: Describe how to prepare pure, dry crystals of copper(II) sulfate from copper(II) oxide.

1. React CuO with sulfuric acid (excess CuO to ensure all acid is used).

2. Filter to remove excess insoluble CuO.

3. Heat filtrate (CuSO4 solution) to evaporate water until saturated.

4. Allow to cool and crystallize. Filter and dry crystals.

09

Laboratory Protocol

Experiment 01 / Titration

Acid-Alkali Titration

Method: Use a burette to add acid to a known volume of alkali (measured with pipette) containing indicator. Stop at the endpoint (color change).

Exam Application: Examiners ask why the indicator is removed. Answer: To obtain pure crystals, as the indicator would contaminate the salt.

11

Error Database

Wrong

Acids donate protons (H+), so the proton goes into solution.

Correct

Correct. IGCSE defines acids as producing H+ ions in water. Never say "donates electrons", that is reduction.

Wrong

Sodium chloride (NaCl) can be prepared by reacting sodium with hydrochloric acid.

Correct

Too dangerous. Sodium is too reactive and explodes. Use Titration (NaOH + HCl) instead.

Exam Simulation

Basic

1. State the color of methyl orange in acid and in alkali.

Mark Scheme Answer [2 Marks]

Acid: Red/Pink. Alkali: Yellow.

Advanced

2. Explain why copper(II) sulfate cannot be prepared by reacting copper metal with sulfuric acid.

Mark Scheme Answer [2 Marks]

Copper is below hydrogen in the reactivity series. It cannot displace hydrogen from the acid, so the reaction does not occur.
