Chapter 18 · History

China 1900-1976

Two thousand years of empire ended in 1911, and after decades of warlord chaos, Japanese invasion and civil war, the Communists under Mao Zedong unified China and then subjected it to violent upheavals.

Key Concepts

01

The fall of the Qing: by 1900 China was exploited by foreign powers through unequal treaties and spheres of influence won after the Opium Wars (1839-42 and 1856-60); the anti-foreign Boxer Rebellion of 1898-1900 was crushed by an Eight-Nation Alliance that imposed a huge indemnity, further discrediting the dynasty; revolutionaries led by Sun Yat-sen launched the Wuchang uprising of October 1911, the last emperor Puyi abdicated in 1912, and more than two thousand years of imperial rule ended.

02

Warlord era and the May Fourth Movement: after Yuan Shikai's death in 1916, regional warlords fought for power and the central government collapsed; in May 1919 students protested in Beijing against the Treaty of Versailles handing German-owned Shandong province to Japan — the May Fourth Movement — sparking nationalism, the New Culture Movement and growing interest in Marxism; the Chinese Communist Party (CCP) was founded in Shanghai in 1921.

03

Nationalists against Communists: Sun Yat-sen's Nationalist Party (Guomindang, GMD) cooperated with the USSR and the young CCP to reunify China; after Sun's death in 1925, Chiang Kai-shek led the Northern Expedition (1926-28) and then turned on the Communists — the Shanghai massacre of April 1927 began a civil war; Mao Zedong responded by building peasant soviet bases in the countryside, notably the Jiangxi Soviet, arguing that poor peasants, not the tiny industrial working class, would make the revolution.

04

The Long March and war with Japan: encirclement campaigns forced the Communists to break out on the Long March of 1934-35, a retreat of about 6 000 miles from Jiangxi to the remote northern base of Yan'an; of roughly 86 000 who set out, fewer than 10 000 arrived, but the epic march spread Communist ideas and left Mao as undisputed party leader by 1935; Japan seized Manchuria in 1931 and set up the puppet state of Manchukuo under Puyi, then invaded the Chinese coast in 1937, committing atrocities such as the Nanjing massacre, which forced an uneasy GMD-CCP united front; Japan's defeat in 1945 resumed the civil war.

05

Communist victory in 1949: the Communists won because peasant support swelled their armies, land reform won the countryside, their discipline and guerrilla tactics outfought the Nationalists, and the GMD rotted from corruption and hyperinflation that destroyed the currency; Chiang Kai-shek and his surviving forces fled to Taiwan, while on 1 October 1949 Mao Zedong proclaimed the People's Republic of China from the Tiananmen Gate in Beijing.

06

Mao's China 1949-1976: land was redistributed to the peasants in 1950-52 and the First Five-Year Plan (1953-57) built heavy industry with Soviet aid; the Great Leap Forward of 1958-60 herded peasants into communes and demanded backyard steel — a catastrophic failure that caused a famine killing tens of millions; the Cultural Revolution of 1966-76 mobilised the Red Guards to attack old customs, culture, habits and ideas and to purge Mao's rivals, persecuting President Liu Shaoqi and sidelining Deng Xiaoping while schools closed and historic treasures were destroyed; China split from the USSR by the 1960s, tested an atomic bomb in 1964, and received US President Nixon in 1972; after Mao's death in September 1976 the Gang of Four were arrested and Deng Xiaoping's reform era began.

Key Terms

TermExam definition
Boxer RebellionThe anti-foreign uprising in China of 1898-1900, supported by the Qing court, which was crushed by an Eight-Nation Alliance of foreign powers.
May Fourth MovementThe student protests of 1919 against the Versailles decision giving Shandong to Japan, which awakened Chinese nationalism.
WarlordA regional military ruler who controlled part of China by force after the collapse of the central government in 1916.
GuomindangThe Chinese Nationalist Party founded by Sun Yat-sen and later led by Chiang Kai-shek, defeated by the Communists in 1949.
Long MarchThe Communists' retreat of about 6 000 miles from Jiangxi to Yan'an in 1934-35, which left Mao Zedong in control of the party.
Great Leap ForwardMao's crash programme of 1958-60 merging farms into communes to overtake Western industry; its failure caused a famine killing tens of millions.
Cultural RevolutionMao's campaign of 1966-76, using the Red Guards to purge rivals and attack old ideas, which devastated education and culture.
Red GuardsMilitant student groups mobilised during the Cultural Revolution to attack supposed enemies of Mao and destroy the old customs, culture, habits and ideas.

Exam Tip

Exam tip

China questions reward precise sequencing: know the order fall of Qing 1911, warlords, the 1927 split with the Nationalists, the Long March 1934-35, the Japanese invasion of 1937, and civil-war victory in 1949, then support each stage with a fact such as the proclamation of 1 October 1949.

Practice Questions

Structured practice — write your answer, then reveal the model.

FOUNDATION

Choose two of the following and describe each event and its importance: the Long March, the Great Leap Forward, the Cultural Revolution.

Model answerThe Long March, 1934-35: the Communists retreated about 6 000 miles to Yan'an, losing most of their number but surviving to fight on, and Mao emerged as party leader (1). The Great Leap Forward, 1958-60: peasants were organised into communes to boost industry, but the plan failed and a famine killed tens of millions (1). The Cultural Revolution, 1966-76: Red Guards attacked traditional culture and Mao's political rivals, closing schools and persecuting leaders such as Liu Shaoqi (1). Importance: each shows how Mao kept power through sacrifice, force and ideology at enormous human cost (1).
CHALLENGE

Evaluate the reasons for the victory of the Communists in the Chinese Civil War, 1945-49.

Model answerPeasant support was decisive: Communist land reform gave land to poor peasants, who supplied food, intelligence and millions of recruits to the People's Liberation Army (1). Communist leadership and tactics were superior: disciplined guerrilla warfare, experienced commanders and captured American equipment turned the military balance after 1947 (1). Nationalist weaknesses undermined Chiang Kai-shek: corruption, brutal conscription, and hyperinflation that wiped out savings destroyed popular support and morale (1). The Japanese invasion had also weakened the GMD, which bore the main burden of conventional fighting, while the Communists expanded behind Japanese lines (1). Overall victory came from Communist organisation and reforms exploiting Nationalist failure, which is why the GMD's larger armies and American aid could not save it (1).
