SECTION 01 — CHAPTER OVERVIEW

Visualizing Mathematics

Graphs allow us to visualize algebraic equations as geometric shapes. This chapter covers the Cartesian coordinate system, the equations of straight lines ($y = mx + c$), calculating gradients, midpoints, and distances, as well as plotting quadratic and cubic functions.

SECTION 02 — LEARNING OBJECTIVES

Chapter Goals

  • Plot coordinates and draw linear graphs from equations.
  • Calculate the gradient, midpoint, and distance between two points.
  • Find the equation of a line in the form $y = mx + c$.
  • Identify parallel and perpendicular lines.
  • Plot and interpret quadratic ($y = ax^2 + bx + c$) graphs.
SECTION 03 — CORE THEORY

Linear Geometry

The gradient ($m$) of a line measures its steepness. It is calculated as the change in $y$ divided by the change in $x$: $m = \frac{y_2 - y_1}{x_2 - x_1}$.

Equation of a Line

The standard form is $y = mx + c$, where $m$ is the gradient and $c$ is the y-intercept (where the line crosses the y-axis).

Parallel lines have the same gradient ($m_1 = m_2$).

Perpendicular lines have gradients that multiply to -1 ($m_1 \times m_2 = -1$).

SECTION 04 — KEY DEFINITIONS

Examination Definitions

GRADIENT: The steepness of a line, calculated as the ratio of vertical change to horizontal change.

INTERCEPT: The point where a graph crosses an axis. The y-intercept occurs when $x = 0$.

FUNCTION: A relationship that maps each input ($x$) to exactly one output ($y$).

SECTION 06 — CALCULATIONS & WORKED EXAMPLES

Finding the Equation of a Line

Find the equation of the line passing through points $A(2, 3)$ and $B(6, 11)$.

STEP WORKING NOTES
1. Calculate gradient ($m$) $m = \frac{11 - 3}{6 - 2} = \frac{8}{4} = 2$ $m = \frac{y_2 - y_1}{x_2 - x_1}$
2. Substitute into $y = mx + c$ $y = 2x + c$ Use $m = 2$
3. Solve for $c$ using point $A$ $3 = 2(2) + c \Rightarrow 3 = 4 + c \Rightarrow c = -1$ Substitute $x=2, y=3$
4. Final Answer $y = 2x - 1$ Equation of the line
SECTION 07 — EXAM TECHNIQUE

Drawing Graphs

Always use a sharp pencil and a ruler. Draw a full cross at the plotted points. Label axes with their variables ($x$, $y$) and include a scale. If plotting a curve (like a quadratic), draw a smooth freehand curve, NOT straight lines between dots.

SECTION 08 — COMMON MISTAKES

Avoid These Errors

Mistake: Calculating gradient as $\frac{x_2 - x_1}{y_2 - y_1}$ (run over rise).

Correction: Gradient is always $\frac{\text{change in } y}{\text{change in } x}$ (rise over run).

SECTION 09 — QUICK REVISION

Fast Facts

  • Gradient: $m = \frac{\Delta y}{\Delta x}$
  • Line equation: $y = mx + c$.
  • Distance: $d = \sqrt{(x_2-x_1)^2 + (y_2-y_1)^2}$.
  • Perpendicular gradients: $m_1 \times m_2 = -1$.
SECTION 10 — EXAM QUESTIONS

Test Your Knowledge

Question 1 (Standard)

The line $L$ passes through $(3, 4)$ and has a gradient of $-2$. Find the equation of $L$ in the form $y = mx + c$.

2 marks:

1. $y = -2x + c$

2. $4 = -2(3) + c \Rightarrow 4 = -6 + c \Rightarrow c = 10$

Answer: $y = -2x + 10$
