Temperature & Thermal Properties

CORE EXTENDED
THEORY

Heat & Temperature

Heat is a form of energy transfer that flows from a hotter region to a colder region. Temperature measures the average kinetic energy of the particles in a substance. When two objects are in thermal contact, thermal energy flows between them until they reach thermal equilibrium (same temperature).

Different materials require different amounts of energy to change their temperature. Specific heat capacity (SHC) is the energy required to raise 1 kg of a substance by 1°C.

DEFINITIONS

Key Terms

Thermal Equilibrium: A state where two objects in contact are at the same temperature and there is no net transfer of thermal energy.

Specific Heat Capacity: The amount of energy required to raise the temperature of 1 kg of a substance by 1°C.

FORMULAE & CALCULATIONS

Thermal Energy Equation

Energy (E) = mass (m) × SHC (c) × temp change (ΔT)
PRACTICAL WORK

Measuring SHC

To find the SHC of a metal block, an electric heater is inserted into the block, and a thermometer measures the temperature rise. Energy supplied is calculated using E = V × I × t, and then c = E / (m × ΔT).

EXAM PRACTICE

Worked Example

Question 1 (Extended)

Calculate the energy required to heat 2 kg of water from 20°C to 80°C. (SHC of water = 4200 J/kg°C).

Temp Change (ΔT): 80 - 20 = 60°C

Formula: E = m × c × ΔT

Substitution: E = 2 × 4200 × 60

Answer: E = 504,000 J (504 kJ)
