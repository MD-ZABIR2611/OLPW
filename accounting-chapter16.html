Chapter 16 · Accounting

Incomplete Records

Calculating profit and reconstructing missing figures when a trader does not keep full double-entry records.

05Key concepts
06Key terms
02Practice questions
—Your status
01

Key Concepts

01

With incomplete records, profit is often found by comparing opening and closing capital: closing capital − opening capital + drawings − capital introduced = profit.

02

A statement of affairs lists the assets and liabilities at a date; the difference is capital.

03

Missing sales are found from the trading results: cost of goods sold + mark-up, or sales = cost × (100 + margin%) / 100.

04

A missing cash balance or missing purchases figure is found by reconstructing the cash book or a total trade receivables/payables account.

05

The accounting equation Capital + Liabilities = Assets underpins every missing-figure calculation.

02

Key Terms

TermExam definition
Statement of affairsA list of assets and liabilities at a date, prepared from incomplete records; the balancing figure is capital.
Trade receivables control accountA reconstructed account used to find missing credit sales or missing receipts from customers.
Mark-upProfit expressed as a percentage of cost: selling price = cost × (100 + mark-up%) / 100.
MarginProfit expressed as a percentage of sales: cost of goods sold = sales × (100 − margin%) / 100.
Drawings adjustmentGoods or cash taken by the owner must be added back when calculating profit from the change in capital.
03

Exam Tip

Exam tip

For the capital-comparison method, always adjust for drawings (add back) and capital introduced (deduct) before calling the difference profit. Show the statement of affairs on both dates - method marks are given even if the arithmetic slips.

04

Practice Questions

Structured practice — write your answer, then reveal the model.

FOUNDATION

A trader’s capital was $18,500 on 1 January and $21,200 on 31 December. During the year she took $3,000 cash as drawings. Calculate the profit for the year.

Model answerProfit = closing capital − opening capital + drawings (1) = 21,200 − 18,500 + 3,000 = $5,700 (1).
CHALLENGE

A shop marks up all goods by 25%. Cost of goods sold was $9,600 but $800 of goods were taken by the owner for personal use. Calculate sales.

Model answerGoods available for sale cost $9,600 + $800 drawings = $10,400 sold at cost (1). Sales = 10,400 × 125/100 = $13,000 (1). Show the working: sales = cost × (100 + mark-up)/100 (1).
