Mensuration: Perimeter, Area, Surface Area & Volume

CORE
SECTION 01 — CHAPTER OVERVIEW

Measuring Space

Mensuration is the branch of mathematics dealing with the measurement of geometric figures. This chapter covers calculating the perimeter, area, surface area, and volume of 2D and 3D shapes, from basic rectangles to complex compound solids.

SECTION 02 — LEARNING OBJECTIVES

Chapter Goals

  • Calculate the area of triangles, quadrilaterals, and circles.
  • Calculate the volume and surface area of prisms, cylinders, and spheres.
  • Solve problems involving arc length and sector area.
  • Break down compound shapes into simpler components.
SECTION 03 — CORE THEORY

2D & 3D Measurements

Perimeter is the total distance around a 2D shape. Area is the space inside it. Volume is the 3D space inside a solid. Surface Area is the total area of all the faces of a 3D solid.

Key Circle Formulas

Circumference: $C = 2\pi r$ or $C = \pi d$

Area: $A = \pi r^2$

Arc Length: $\frac{\theta}{360} \times 2\pi r$ (where $\theta$ is the central angle)

SECTION 04 — KEY DEFINITIONS

Examination Definitions

PRISM: A 3D solid with a constant cross-section. Volume = Area of cross-section $\times$ length.

SECTOR: A pie-shaped part of a circle enclosed by two radii and an arc.

COMPOUND SHAPE: A shape made up of two or more basic geometric shapes.

SECTION 06 — CALCULATIONS & WORKED EXAMPLES

Volume of a Cylinder

Calculate the volume of a cylinder with radius $5\text{ cm}$ and height $12\text{ cm}$. Leave your answer in terms of $\pi$.

STEP WORKING NOTES
1. Write formula $V = \pi r^2 h$ Volume of cylinder
2. Substitute values $V = \pi \times (5)^2 \times 12$ $r=5, h=12$
3. Calculate $V = \pi \times 25 \times 12 = 300\pi$ Keep in terms of $\pi$
4. Final Answer $300\pi \text{ cm}^3$ Include units
SECTION 07 — EXAM TECHNIQUE

Leaving in Terms of $\pi$

If a question says "leave your answer in terms of $\pi$", do NOT multiply by 3.14. Simply leave $\pi$ in your final answer (e.g., $300\pi$). This guarantees full marks and avoids rounding errors.

SECTION 08 — COMMON MISTAKES

Avoid These Errors

Mistake: Confusing radius and diameter.

Correction: If the question gives the diameter ($d$), you must divide by 2 to get the radius ($r = d/2$) before putting it into the area formula $A = \pi r^2$.

SECTION 09 — QUICK REVISION

Fast Facts

  • Area of circle: $A = \pi r^2$.
  • Volume of prism: Base Area $\times$ Height.
  • Volume of sphere: $\frac{4}{3}\pi r^3$.
  • Arc length: $\frac{\theta}{360} \times 2\pi r$.
SECTION 10 — EXAM QUESTIONS

Test Your Knowledge

Question 1 (Standard)

A trapezium has parallel sides of $8\text{ cm}$ and $12\text{ cm}$, and a height of $5\text{ cm}$. Calculate its area.

2 marks:

1. Formula: $A = \frac{1}{2}(a + b)h$

2. Substitute: $A = \frac{1}{2}(8 + 12) \times 5$

3. Calculate: $A = \frac{1}{2}(20) \times 5 = 10 \times 5 = 50$

Answer: $50\text{ cm}^2$
