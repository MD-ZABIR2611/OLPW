The Struggle for Independence in South Asia 1900-1971

From the founding of Congress in 1885 to the birth of Bangladesh in 1971, South Asia's road to freedom ran through mass non-violent protest, communal conflict and two partitions.

Key Concepts

01

Early nationalism: the Indian National Congress, founded in 1885, demanded eventual self-government; the Muslim League, founded in 1906, sought to protect the interests of the large Muslim minority; the British partition of Bengal in 1905, into a Hindu west and a Muslim east, provoked the Swadeshi boycott of British goods and was reversed in 1911; the Morley-Minto reforms of 1909 offered only limited councils, disappointing nationalists.

02

Gandhi and mass politics: Gandhi returned from South Africa in 1915 and turned Congress into a mass movement using satyagraha, non-violent resistance; the Amritsar massacre of April 1919, in which troops under General Dyer killed several hundred unarmed protesters, destroyed faith in British justice; his campaigns included the Non-Cooperation Movement (1920-22), the Salt March of 1930 — a 240-mile walk to the sea at Dandi to break the salt tax, mobilising millions — and the Quit India movement of 1942, launched with the call to do or die, which filled the prisons with Congress leaders.

03

The road to partition: the Government of India Act of 1935 gave the provinces self-government, which Congress won; the Muslim League under Muhammad Ali Jinnah adopted the Lahore Resolution of 1940 demanding separate Muslim homelands (the name Pakistan had been coined in 1933), fearing permanent Hindu majority rule; the 1946 Cabinet Mission failed, and communal massacres, starting in Calcutta in August 1946, made partition seem unavoidable; the last Viceroy, Mountbatten, brought the transfer of power forward to August 1947.

04

Independence and partition, August 1947: British India was divided into Hindu-majority India, with Nehru as first Prime Minister, and Muslim-majority Pakistan, whose two wings — West Pakistan and East Pakistan — were separated by 1 600 km of Indian territory; the boundary lines drawn hurriedly by the Radcliffe Commission triggered one of the largest migrations in history, with up to 15 million people crossing in both directions and communal killings estimated at between several hundred thousand and a million or more; the princely state of Kashmir acceded to India in October 1947, sparking the first Indo-Pakistani war and a dispute that remains unresolved.

05

Pakistan's two-wing problem: from the start the new state carried millions of refugees in the west, a weak economy, and two wings divided by language and geography; Bengali speakers in East Pakistan resented rule from Urdu-speaking West Pakistan, demanding recognition of Bangla — protests in which students were shot in February 1952 eventually won Bengali status as a state language — while western elites kept control and East Pakistan's jute earnings flowed west.

06

Bangladesh 1971: in the December 1970 elections the Awami League of Sheikh Mujibur Rahman won an outright majority, but the military ruler Yahya Khan postponed the national assembly; on 25 March 1971 the army launched Operation Searchlight in the east, arrested Mujib and massacred civilians — estimates run from hundreds of thousands to three million dead — driving about 10 million refugees into India, which intervened in December 1971 and forced Pakistan's surrender on 16 December 1971; East Pakistan became the independent state of Bangladesh under Mujibur Rahman.

Key Terms

TermExam definition
Indian National CongressIndia's main nationalist party, founded in 1885, which led the country to independence under Gandhi and Nehru.
Muslim LeagueThe political party founded in 1906 that campaigned for a separate Muslim homeland, Pakistan, under Jinnah.
Amritsar massacreThe killing of several hundred unarmed Indians by troops under General Dyer in April 1919, which turned many Indians against British rule.
SatyagrahaGandhi's method of non-violent resistance, meaning truth-force, using civil disobedience without hatred of the opponent.
Salt MarchGandhi's 1930 protest walk of about 240 miles to the sea at Dandi to make salt illegally and defy the British salt tax.
Quit India movementCongress's 1942 campaign demanding an immediate British withdrawal from India, launched with Gandhi's call to do or die.
PartitionThe division of British India in 1947 into the independent states of India and Pakistan, accompanied by massive migration and communal killings.
East PakistanThe Bengali-speaking eastern wing of Pakistan, which became the independent state of Bangladesh after the 1971 war.

Exam Tip

Exam tip

Partition and independence answers need dates and statistics as evidence — the Lahore Resolution 1940, independence in August 1947, up to 15 million refugees — and the strongest answers weigh Congress's unity efforts against the Muslim League's fear of a permanent Hindu majority.

Practice Questions

Structured practice — write your answer, then reveal the model.

FOUNDATION

Describe Gandhi's methods of campaigning against British rule, using two examples.

Model answerHe used satyagraha, non-violent civil disobedience, refusing to obey unjust laws while accepting the punishment, which won moral sympathy at home and abroad (1). Example: the Salt March of 1930, when he walked about 240 miles to Dandi to break the salt tax, sparking mass protests across India (1). Example: the Non-Cooperation Movement of 1920-22, in which Indians boycotted courts, schools and British titles (1). Example: Quit India in 1942, demanding immediate British withdrawal (1).
CHALLENGE

Evaluate the reasons for the creation of Bangladesh in 1971.

Model answerGeography and language divided Pakistan from the start: the two wings were separated by 1 600 km of India, and Bengali speakers resented rule by the Urdu-speaking west (1). Economic grievance grew because East Pakistan's jute export earnings were spent in the west, while the east was treated as a colony and its 1970 electoral victory was denied (1). The army's crackdown of March 1971, with massacres and about 10 million refugees, made continuation of Pakistani rule impossible and began a liberation war (1). Indian military intervention in December 1971 was decisive: within about two weeks Pakistan surrendered, making independence achievable (1). Overall Bangladesh was created by Bengali nationalist determination combined with Indian power, after Pakistan's rulers chose force over democracy (1).
