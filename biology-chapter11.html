SECTION 01 — CHAPTER OVERVIEW

Nerves & Hormones

Organisms must detect and respond to changes in their internal and external environments to survive. This chapter covers the nervous system (rapid electrical impulses) and the endocrine system (slower chemical hormones), alongside the concept of homeostasis.

SECTION 02 — LEARNING OBJECTIVES

  • Describe plant tropisms (phototropism, gravitropism).
SECTION 03 — CORE THEORY

Coordination & Balance

The Central Nervous System (CNS) consists of the brain and spinal cord. Sensory neurons carry impulses from receptors to the CNS. Motor neurons carry impulses from the CNS to effectors (muscles or glands).

The Reflex Arc

A reflex action is a rapid, automatic, protective response that does not involve conscious thought. The pathway is: Stimulus → Receptor → Sensory Neuron → CNS (relay neuron) → Motor Neuron → Effector → Response.

Synapses

Synapses are gaps between neurons. An electrical impulse reaches the end of a neuron, triggering the release of neurotransmitters. These diffuse across the gap, bind to the next neuron, and initiate a new electrical impulse.

Homeostasis & Blood Glucose

Homeostasis is the maintenance of a constant internal environment. Blood glucose concentration is regulated by the pancreas. If blood glucose is too high, the pancreas secretes insulin, causing the liver to convert glucose to glycogen. If too low, the pancreas secretes glucagon, causing the liver to convert glycogen back to glucose (negative feedback).

Plant Tropisms

Plants respond to stimuli using hormones called auxins. Phototropism is growth towards light (shoots). Gravitropism/Geotropism is growth towards gravity (roots). Auxins accumulate on the shaded or lower side, causing cells to elongate faster, bending the plant.

SECTION 04 — KEY DEFINITIONS

Examination Definitions

HOMEOSTASIS: The maintenance of a constant internal environment.

SYNAPSE: A small gap between two neurons where neurotransmitters diffuse.

TROPISM: A plant's growth response to a directional stimulus (e.g., light or gravity).

SECTION 07 — EXAM TECHNIQUE

Nervous vs Hormonal

Always compare the two systems if asked. Nervous = fast, electrical, short-lived, specific target. Hormonal = slow, chemical, long-lasting, widespread.

SECTION 08 — COMMON MISTAKES

Avoid These Errors

Mistake: Saying auxins make roots grow down.

Correction: Auxins actually inhibit cell elongation in roots. They accumulate at the bottom, slowing growth on the lower side, causing the root to bend downwards.

SECTION 09 — QUICK REVISION

Fast Facts

  • Reflexes are automatic, protective responses.
  • Synapses use neurotransmitters to cross gaps.
  • Insulin lowers blood glucose; glucagon raises it.
  • Vasodilation cools the body; Vasoconstriction keeps it warm.
SECTION 10 — EXAM QUESTIONS

Test Your Knowledge

Question 1 (Standard)

Explain how synapses ensure that nerve impulses travel in one direction only.

2 marks: Neurotransmitters are only stored and released from the presynaptic neuron. The receptor molecules for the neurotransmitter are only found on the postsynaptic neuron.
