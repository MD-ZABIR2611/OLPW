Probability

CORE
SECTION 01 — CHAPTER OVERVIEW

Measuring Likelihood

Probability measures how likely an event is to occur. This chapter covers calculating theoretical and experimental probabilities, using Venn diagrams and tree diagrams to map outcomes, and understanding independent and mutually exclusive events.

SECTION 02 — LEARNING OBJECTIVES

Chapter Goals

  • Use sample spaces and two-way tables.
  • Construct and interpret Venn diagrams.
  • Draw and use probability tree diagrams.
  • Understand conditional probability.
SECTION 03 — CORE THEORY

Rules of Probability

Probability is expressed as a number between 0 (impossible) and 1 (certain). The formula is $P(A) = \frac{\text{Number of successful outcomes}}{\text{Total number of outcomes}}$.

Key Concepts

Mutually Exclusive: Events that cannot happen at the same time. $P(A \text{ or } B) = P(A) + P(B)$.

Independent Events: The outcome of one does not affect the other. $P(A \text{ and } B) = P(A) \times P(B)$.

Complementary Events: The probability of something NOT happening is $1 - P(\text{happening})$.

SECTION 04 — KEY DEFINITIONS

Examination Definitions

SAMPLE SPACE: The set of all possible outcomes of an experiment.

EVENT: A specific outcome or set of outcomes (e.g., rolling a 6).

CONDITIONAL PROBABILITY: The probability of an event occurring, given that another event has already occurred.

SECTION 06 — CALCULATIONS & WORKED EXAMPLES

Tree Diagram Calculation

A bag has 5 red and 3 blue marbles. Two marbles are drawn at random without replacement. Find the probability that both are red.

STEP WORKING NOTES
1. P(1st Red) $\frac{5}{8}$ 5 reds out of 8 total
2. P(2nd Red | 1st Red) $\frac{4}{7}$ 4 reds left out of 7 total
3. Multiply (AND) $\frac{5}{8} \times \frac{4}{7} = \frac{20}{56}$ Independent events rule
4. Final Answer $\frac{5}{14}$ Simplify fraction
SECTION 07 — EXAM TECHNIQUE

Fractions vs Decimals

Always leave probability answers as exact fractions unless the question asks for decimals. If using a tree diagram, write the probabilities on the branches as fractions to avoid rounding errors.

SECTION 08 — COMMON MISTAKES

Avoid These Errors

Mistake: Forgetting to remove the first item when calculating "without replacement".

Correction: If it is "without replacement", the denominator of the second branch MUST decrease by 1 (e.g., from 8 to 7).

SECTION 09 — QUICK REVISION

Fast Facts

  • $0 \le P \le 1$.
  • AND = Multiply ($\times$). OR = Add ($+$).
  • Not A = $1 - P(A)$.
  • Without replacement = denominators decrease.
SECTION 10 — EXAM QUESTIONS

Test Your Knowledge

Question 1 (Standard)

A fair six-sided die is rolled. What is the probability that it lands on a number greater than 4?

2 marks:

1. Numbers greater than 4 are {5, 6}.

2. $P(>4) = \frac{2}{6} = \frac{1}{3}$.
