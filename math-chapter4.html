SECTION 03 — CORE THEORY

Equations & Patterns

An equation states that two expressions are equal (uses an '$=$' sign). To solve an equation, you must isolate the unknown variable by doing the same operation to both sides.

Simultaneous Equations

When solving two equations with two unknowns (e.g., $x$ and $y$), use either:

1. Substitution: Make one variable the subject of one equation, and plug it into the other.

2. Elimination: Match the coefficients of one variable so they cancel out when added or subtracted.

Arithmetic Sequences

An arithmetic sequence increases by a constant difference ($d$). The nth term is given by $a_n = a + (n-1)d$, where $a$ is the first term.

SECTION 04 — KEY DEFINITIONS

Examination Definitions

EQUATION: A mathematical statement asserting the equality of two expressions, containing an equals sign ($=$).

INEQUALITY: A mathematical statement indicating that two expressions are not equal, using symbols like $<$, $>$, $\le$, $\ge$.

SEQUENCE: An ordered list of numbers following a specific rule.

SECTION 06 — CALCULATIONS & WORKED EXAMPLES

Simultaneous Equations (Elimination)

Solve the simultaneous equations: $3x + 2y = 7$ and $5x - 2y = 1$

STEP WORKING NOTES
1. Add equations $(3x + 5x) + (2y - 2y) = 7 + 1$ $y$ terms cancel out
2. Solve for $x$ $8x = 8 \Rightarrow x = 1$ Divide by 8
3. Substitute $x$ back $3(1) + 2y = 7 \Rightarrow 2y = 4 \Rightarrow y = 2$ Use first equation
4. Final Answer $x = 1, y = 2$ Solution is $(1, 2)$
SECTION 07 — EXAM TECHNIQUE

Checking Solutions

Always plug your values for $x$ and $y$ back into the ORIGINAL equations to ensure they satisfy both. This takes 10 seconds and guarantees you don't lose easy marks due to an arithmetic slip.

SECTION 08 — COMMON MISTAKES

Avoid These Errors

Mistake: Flipping the inequality sign incorrectly.

Correction: You only flip the inequality sign if you multiply or divide by a NEGATIVE number. E.g., $-2x > 4$ becomes $x < -2$.

SECTION 09 — QUICK REVISION

Fast Facts

  • Equation: Use '$=$'. Inequality: Use $<, >, \le, \ge$.
  • nth term of arithmetic sequence: $a + (n-1)d$.
  • Elimination matches coefficients. Substitution isolates one variable.
  • Open circle on number line = $<$ or $>$. Filled circle = $\le$ or $\ge$.
SECTION 10 — EXAM QUESTIONS

Test Your Knowledge

Question 1 (Standard)

Find the nth term of the sequence: $4, 9, 14, 19, ...$

2 marks:

1. Common difference $d = 9 - 4 = 5$.

2. First term $a = 4$. The term before the first is $4 - 5 = -1$.

nth term: $5n - 1$
