Density

CORE
THEORY

Mass per Unit Volume

Density is a measure of how much mass is contained in a given volume. A substance with high density (like iron) feels heavy for its size, while a substance with low density (like cork) feels light for its size.

If an object is placed in a fluid (liquid or gas), it will float if its average density is less than the density of the fluid. It will sink if its density is greater than the fluid's density.

DEFINITIONS

Key Terms

Density: The mass per unit volume of a substance, measured in kilograms per cubic meter (kg/m³) or grams per cubic centimeter (g/cm³).

FORMULAE & CALCULATIONS

Density Equation

density (ρ) = mass (m) / volume (V)
PRACTICAL WORK

Displacement Method

To find the density of an irregular solid, measure its mass using an electronic balance. Then, measure its volume using the displacement method: submerge the object in a measuring cylinder filled with water and record the rise in water level.

EXAM PRACTICE

Worked Example

Question 1 (Core)

A stone has a mass of 120 g. When submerged in a measuring cylinder, the water level rises from 50 cm³ to 95 cm³. Calculate the density of the stone.

Volume: V = 95 - 50 = 45 cm³

Formula: ρ = m / V

Substitution: ρ = 120 g / 45 cm³

Answer: ρ = 2.67 g/cm³
