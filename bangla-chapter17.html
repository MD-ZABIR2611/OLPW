Advanced Grammar: Bibhakti in Depth and Sentence Correction

Advanced Bangla grammar is tested through error correction and precise case use. This chapter works through the full Bibhakti system and the error patterns — agreement, case, word order — that carry the marks.

Key Concepts

01

Bangla has eight Bibhaktis, each pairing a kārak (কারক, case relation) with characteristic endings on the noun: kartā (কর্তা, nominative), karman (কর্ম, accusative), karaṇ (করণ, instrumental), sampradān (সম্প্রদান, dative), apādān (অপাদান, ablative), sambandha (সম্বন্ধ, genitive), adhikaraṇ (অধিকরণ, locative), and sambodhan (সম্বোধন, vocative).

02

Postposition phrases replace some Bibhakti endings: হাতে (in the hand) uses the locative ending, while হাত দিয়ে (by hand) uses the postposition দিয়ে for the instrumental — choosing between inflection and postposition is a classic test point.

03

Agreement errors occur when a verb does not match its subject in person and honorific level — সে যায়/তিনি যান/তুমি যাও are distinct, and mixing them in one clause is the most planted exam error.

04

Case errors include using genitive where accusative is required after certain verbs, and misusing the locative of time (সকালে বন্ধু এলো) versus locative of place (ঘরে বসে আছি).

05

Word-order errors: Bangla default order is subject–object–verb, and scrambling it for emphasis must still keep the finite verb final — a displaced finite verb marks a sentence as ungrammatical.

06

Correction method: locate the finite verb, identify its subject by honorific agreement, check each noun's case against its kārak role, then read aloud for idiom — errors of idiom rarely survive being spoken.

02

Key Terms

TermExam definition
BibhaktiThe Bangla case system: eight kārak relations shown by inflectional endings on nouns, e.g. kartā, karman, karaṇ.
KartāThe nominative kārak marking the subject of a verb, typically with no ending or the ending রা in plural, e.g. ছেলে পড়ে.
KarmanThe accusative kārak marking the direct object, with no special ending for inanimates, e.g. বই পড়ি.
KaraṇThe instrumental kārak marking the means or instrument of an action, e.g. কলমে লেখা — "writing with a pen".
ApādānThe ablative kārak marking separation or source, answering "from where/whom", e.g. বইখানা টেবিল থেকে পড়ে গেল.
SambodhanThe vocative kārak used for direct address, with the ending হে or ও, e.g. হে বন্ধু!
Honorific agreementThe requirement that Bangla verbs match the subject's level of respect — যায়, যান, যাও for neutral, honorific and intimate subjects.
Sentence correctionAn exam task in which a grammatically or idiomatically faulty Bangla sentence must be located, diagnosed and rewritten correctly.
03

Exam Tip

Exam tip

In error-spotting questions, test the verb first: check its honorific level against the subject, then check the finite verb is sentence-final. A large share of planted errors are agreement or order errors, and correcting them precisely earns full marks faster than hunting vocabulary.

Practice Questions

Structured practice — write your answer, then reveal the model.

FOUNDATION

Name any four of the eight Bibhaktis and give a one-word Bangla example of each.

Model answerAny four of: kartā — ছেলে যায় (1); karman — বই পড়ি (1); karaṇ — কলমে লেখা (1); sampradān — বন্ধুকে দিলাম (1); apādān — ঢাকা থেকে এলাম (1); sambandha — বইয়ের পাতা (1); adhikaraṇ — ঘরে বসে আছি (1); sambodhan — হে বন্ধু (1).
CHALLENGE

The sentence "আমার ছোট ভাই কলকাতা থেকে রাতে যান" contains errors of agreement and tense/context mismatch. Identify the errors and correct the sentence.

Model answerError 1 (agreement): the subject আমার ছোট ভাই is intimate/informal family reference, so the honorific verb যান is wrong — use যায় (1). Error 2 (logic): যান/যায় is motion "to go", which conflicts with থেকে "from" — going from Kolkata should be আসা, "to come", i.e. আসে (1). Corrected: আমার ছোট ভাই কলকাতা থেকে রাতে আসে (1). Method credit: locating the finite verb and matching honorific level and direction of motion shows the diagnosis examiners reward (1).
