Transport in Humans

CORE
SECTION 01 — CHAPTER OVERVIEW

The Circulatory System

Humans have a double circulation system involving the heart, blood vessels, and blood. This chapter covers the structure of the heart, the differences between arteries, veins, and capillaries, and the functions of the components of blood.

SECTION 02 — LEARNING OBJECTIVES

Chapter Goals

  • Describe the structure of the human heart.
  • Explain the advantage of double circulation.
  • Compare the structure and function of arteries, veins, and capillaries.
  • State the functions of red blood cells, white blood cells, platelets, and plasma.
SECTION 03 — CORE THEORY

Heart, Vessels & Blood

The heart is a double pump. The right side pumps deoxygenated blood to the lungs; the left side pumps oxygenated blood to the whole body. The left ventricle has a thicker muscular wall because it must pump blood further and at higher pressure.

Blood Vessels

Arteries: Carry blood away from the heart. Thick walls, narrow lumen, high pressure. No valves (except pulmonary).

Veins: Carry blood to the heart. Thin walls, wide lumen, low pressure. Contain valves to prevent backflow.

Capillaries: Exchange materials with tissues. One cell thick walls, very low pressure. No valves.

Blood Components

Red Blood Cells (RBCs): Biconcave shape (increases surface area), no nucleus (more room for haemoglobin). Haemoglobin binds to oxygen.

White Blood Cells (WBCs): Defend against disease. Phagocytes ingest pathogens; lymphocytes produce antibodies.

Platelets: Cell fragments that clot blood at wound sites.

Plasma: The liquid part of blood. Transports dissolved substances (glucose, amino acids, CO2, urea, hormones).

SECTION 04 — KEY DEFINITIONS

Examination Definitions

DOUBLE CIRCULATION: A circulatory system where blood passes through the heart twice for every one complete circuit of the body.

ARTERY: A blood vessel that carries blood away from the heart under high pressure.

HAEMOGLOBIN: The red protein in red blood cells that binds to oxygen for transport.

SECTION 06 — PRACTICAL LAB

Heart Dissection

AIM

To observe the internal structure of a mammalian heart.

SAFETY

Wear gloves and an apron. Use a scalpel carefully, cutting away from the body. Wash hands thoroughly after.

METHOD

1. Identify the left and right sides (left side has thicker ventricle wall).

2. Cut open the left ventricle to observe the thick muscular wall and valves.

3. Cut open the right ventricle to compare wall thickness.

SECTION 07 — EXAM TECHNIQUE

Explaining Adaptations

Always link structure to function. Do not just say "RBCs have a biconcave shape". Say "RBCs have a biconcave shape, which increases their surface area to volume ratio, allowing faster diffusion of oxygen."

SECTION 08 — COMMON MISTAKES

Avoid These Errors

Mistake: Saying arteries carry oxygenated blood and veins carry deoxygenated blood.

Correction: This is generally true, but the pulmonary artery carries deoxygenated blood to the lungs, and the pulmonary vein carries oxygenated blood back. The true definition is about direction (away from vs towards the heart).

SECTION 09 — QUICK REVISION

Fast Facts

  • Left ventricle has thicker walls than the right ventricle.
  • Veins have valves; arteries and capillaries do not.
  • Capillaries are one cell thick to allow rapid diffusion.
  • RBCs lack a nucleus to make room for haemoglobin.
SECTION 10 — EXAM QUESTIONS

Test Your Knowledge

Question 1 (Standard)

Explain why the left ventricle has a thicker muscular wall than the right ventricle.

2 marks: The left ventricle must pump blood to the entire body, whereas the right ventricle only pumps blood to the lungs. Therefore, the left ventricle needs more muscle to generate higher pressure.
